Nine Cells to a Thousand

A recording in this project carries anywhere from 9 cells to 62. Another lab’s carries up to 1,050. Every learned detector we have reduces that field to a single number in its second stage, and the number it picks is an answer to the field-size question whether or not anyone meant it as one. This is what the answer currently is, why it does not travel, and three designs that would.

Before anything else

Two notes on what was asked

“Tube and line.” There is no architecture called line in the registry. It holds six: tube, tube_guard, tube_ratio, tube_ratio_guard, tiny and trace. I have read line as trace — the one that collapses every cell into a single line before it filters anything, and which exists as the control for whether the cell axis matters at all. Everything below evaluates all six, so the answer holds either way; if line meant something that never reached this tree, say so and I will fold it in.

1 · the data

The field is not a parameter that varies a little

Two corpora have been assessed and both assessments are committed. This lab’s 85 recordings run from 9 cells to 62, median 32. The Cossart lab’s 59 recordings on DANDI:000219 run from 117 cells to 1,050, median 566. End to end that is a factor of 117, and the two corpora do not overlap at all.

And field size is not the only thing that moves with it. The per-cell event rate moves too, in the same direction, and by a comparable factor:

Cossart / DANDI:000219531 566 cells117–1050 cells 0.01636 Hz42 cells7.4 %

⚠ These two axes are confounded, and no measurement in this tree separates them. The larger field is also the busier one, by almost the same factor. Every cross-lab transfer number quoted below therefore reports the effect of both changes at once. Separating them needs a field-size sweep on simulation, which the bench cannot currently do — see §6.

It is not only a cross-lab problem either. Rate moves inside this lab’s own corpus, and it moves in opposite directions by stream: under TTX the fast stream’s coordination falls to a median 0.46 of its own baseline and the slow stream’s rises to 2.50, with 44 % of slices at or above baseline (FOUNDATIONS §9). A detector must be calibrated on baseline windows — the same section forbids sourcing coordination properties from treated ones — and then pointed at treated ones. So the deployment range is outside the calibration range by construction, on every run this project will ever do.

2 · the arithmetic

The honest floor is neither a count nor a fraction

Before asking what a network should compute, it is worth asking what the answer looks like when nothing is learned at all. Take the simplest null: cells fire independently, each at the corpus’s median rate, and a frame is 0.1 s. Set a budget of one chance frame per hour of recording. Then ask: how many cells have to fire in the same frame before that is not chance?

cells in the field floor, this lab’s rateas a fraction floor, Cossart’s rateas a fraction
9 cells2 cells22.2 % 4 cells44.4 %
13 cells3 cells23.1 % 5 cells38.5 %
32 cells3 cells9.4 % 6 cells18.8 %
50 cells3 cells6.0 % 7 cells14.0 %
117 cells4 cells3.4 % 10 cells8.5 %
566 cells6 cells1.1 % 23 cells4.1 %
1050 cells7 cells0.7 % 34 cells3.2 %
9 → 1050×3.5 ÷32×8.5÷14

Three things fall out, and the third is the design brief.

  1. A fixed count is wrong, and it gets worse as the field grows. Three cells is right at 32. Carried to 566 cells at this lab’s rate it fires 334× over budget; at the Cossart rate, 30,200×.
  2. A fixed fraction is wrong too, in the other direction and harder. 9.4 % of the field is the same three cells at 32. At 566 cells it demands 54 cells, whose chance probability is 6×10−89 of the budget — a bar so far above chance that nothing the preparation produces will reach it. And at 9 cells the same fraction rounds to one cell and fires 242× over budget.
  3. The floor grows sub-linearly and neither rule has that shape. Across the whole span the floor multiplies by 3.5 while the field multiplies by 117. A rule with the right shape is a power of the field size with an exponent strictly between 0 and 1 — which is the third proposal below, where the exponent is fitted rather than chosen.
⚠ This is an ordering and an order of magnitude, never an operating point. The null is independent cells at one homogeneous rate, and real recordings are neither: per-cell rates are heterogeneous, and same-cell intervals have a hard floor — 0.40 s fast and 3.20 s slow on the senktide baselines — which independence does not model. Those two errors run in opposite directions. The thing that gets this right on real recordings is a within-recording surrogate null, which bugarach.assess and CoactDetect already compute; the point of the arithmetic is to show why that matters, not to replace it.
A question this raises and does not answer. K — the coactivity floor a person sets during machine-assisted human identification of coordinated events (MAHICE) — is currently a percentage of each recording’s cell population, with an optional absolute floor under it. That is a human judgement about what counts as one event, which is a different question from the false-alarm floor above, and the two must not be conflated. But they are now both expressed as a percentage, and the arithmetic says a percentage is the wrong shape for the second. Whether that should reach the K rule is Tony’s call, not a session’s; it is flagged here and nowhere else.
3 · the nets we have

What the six actually read, and where each one breaks

All four tubes share their first two stages. Every cell’s onsets are widened, then the cell axis is averaged away, and everything after that reads one trace whose value is the fraction of the field that is active. The guard and the ratio both act downstream of that point.

Reading the table

  • trace and tiny are not weak detectors — they are non-detectors. F1 0.125 comes from making two detections in an entire fold, both of which happen to land on planted events, at a threshold sitting on the edge of its own grid in four folds of four. Their clean probe columns are worth nothing: a silent model cannot fire into the promiscuity block. Whatever trace was meant to be the control for, it has never functioned as one, because it never learned.
  • tube is the best detector in this project on a field it was fitted on, and it cannot travel. Refitted on the Cossart statistics it reaches 0.836 and tube_guard 0.885, clear of every hand-written detector. Carried over unchanged they find 25 and 19 of 120 planted events. Maximum benefit and maximum dependence are the same property.
  • CoactDetect transfers for a gap of 0.001 — 0.790 refitted against 0.789 carried over, onto a field seventeen times larger, at 107 of 120 events. That is the single most useful number on this page, and §5 is largely an argument about why.
  • The ratio arm lost participation as a variable. Plant k cells in one frame and read the score: tube and tube_guard sit at zero for one cell and need two at every background. tube_ratio scores 0.89 on a single cell in a quiet field, and tube_ratio_guard’s curve is flat across every k including k = 0 — it sits on its threshold whether eight cells fire, one fires, or none does. A difference of logs is scale-free by construction: it reports contrast and discards magnitude, and participation is exactly the magnitude it throws away.
  • Their perfect probe columns are a restatement of that arithmetic. The promiscuity probe is a hot window only. A model that divides by its surround cannot fire there, because the denominator is large. The failure it trades into is a small denominator, and the bench contains no quiet-field negative at all. hot_fa of 0.0 on a divide-shaped model measures nothing.

Three properties the tube is credited with, and what each one is really worth

Space invariance partly

Cells are summed, so the model never sees which cell is which and runs at any cell count. True, and it is only half of what is needed: running at any cell count is not the same as being calibrated at any cell count. The statistic it runs on is a fraction of the field, and Figure 2b is what a fraction costs.

Rate invariance measured, and it does not hold

The difference-of-Gaussian kernel area-normalizes to zero, so a uniform rate change cancels — in the mean. Not in the spread. Multiply the background by 8 on recordings with nothing planted and frames over threshold go 66 → 890, a factor of 13.5. Remove the bypass channel and it is 39 → 692, a factor of 17.7 — so the bypass is not the cause, and the one cheap explanation for the transfer asymmetry is closed.

One cell, one vote not delivered

The raster is already binary, so the max-pool that was supposed to cap each cell at one vote instead widens each onset, which increases what a repeatedly firing cell contributes. Measured at the shipped operating point: two cells bursting score a median 0.9996 against four genuinely distinct cells at 0.9988, and in 6 of 10 seeds the two bursting cells score higher. One cell bursting five times crosses its own threshold in 5 of 10 seeds. Two busy cells imitating a crowd is the exact false coordination this design exists to reject.

⚠ The 2×2 could not have found the field-size problem, because the axis is not in it. The screen crossed {subtract, ratio} with {no guard, guard}. Both axes change what the reference is and where on the time axis it is measured. Neither touches the reduction over cells, which happens before either of them and is identical in all four cells of the screen. That is not a criticism of the screen — it answered the question it was built to answer, and it answered it usefully: the ratio arm’s failure is a real result about divide-shaped detectors. It is an argument that the next screen has to move a different axis.
4 · the method

Design the net from the nuisance list, not from the layer catalogue

Here is the more scientific approach that was asked for, and it is not a new idea — it is how the six hand-written detectors were built, written down so a network can be built the same way. List the transformations a recording can undergo without the coordination in it changing. For each one, decide whether the architecture is invariant to it by construction, is trained to be invariant to it, or simply is not. Then the design is that assignment, and every row is a probe that can fail.

Six of them apply here. The ledger below is what the estate currently assigns.

what can change without the coordination changing tube familytinytraceevidence
relabel the cells — any permutation structural structural structural the encoder returns a bit-identical raster under permutation, and permute_rois tests it rather than asserting it
change the number of cells — 9 to 1,050 absent absent absent runs at any count; calibrated at none. 25 of 120 events carried over to a 17× larger field, against CoactDetect’s 107
multiply every cell’s rate by the same factor the mean only absent absent the kernel cancels the mean, not the spread: 8× background gives 13.5× the firings on empty recordings
give different cells different rates absent bands only absent rate-sorted rows are the only acknowledgement anywhere, and the mean over cells discards the order immediately
shift the recording in time structural structural structural convolution, up to the frame grid. Two of the hand-written six move their calls with the grid; the learned models have not been measured on this
change the sampling interval absent absent absent receptive fields are in samples and every bench recording shares one imaging rate, so nothing here can fail if the model has learned our microscope

Read down the middle columns and the diagnosis is not subtle. The one transformation the estate handles structurally is the one that was free — permutation, which a sum gives you. The two that decide whether a detector travels are both unhandled, and the third, rate scaling, is handled in its first moment and not its second, which is why a fixed threshold on a zero-mean signal still has a false-alarm rate that climbs with the background.

The ledger also says where a fix has to go. Cardinality and heterogeneity are both properties of the cell axis, and the tube destroys that axis in its second stage. No change downstream of Figure 3’s red box can address either one. That is the whole reason the three proposals below are not four more tubes.

5 · the proposal

Three classes, one per unhandled row

Each one moves a single axis of the same skeleton, so each can be run against the shipped tube as the control and re-measured in the same run — the discipline the tube screen set, for the reason it set it.

A · chorus — keep the cell axis until after the filter

The row it fixes: cardinality, and heterogeneity as a side effect.

The principle. Permutation invariance over a set of varying size is not an open problem; it is a solved representation question with a standard shape — encode each element with shared weights, pool with a symmetric function, decode the pool (Zaheer and colleagues, Deep Sets, 2017; Qi and colleagues, PointNet, 2017). The tube’s mean over cells is the degenerate member of that family: one element feature, one pooling function, and the pooling applied before any element encoder exists. The fix is not a bigger network. It is moving the pool to the right of the filter and returning more than one statistic from it.

# chorus, the part that matters. x is (batch, cells, frames).
h = phi(x.reshape(B*N, 1, T)).reshape(B, N, C, T)   # shared weights, per cell
v = h.sigmoid()                                     # bounded: one cell, one vote
pooled = cat([v.mean(dim=1),                        # the level
              v.std(dim=1),                         # the spread across the field
              v.topk(m, dim=1).values.mean(dim=1)], # the loudest m
             dim=1)                                 # (B, 3C, T): no N
return rho(pooled)

Why it answers the field-size question. The mean and the standard deviation of a bounded per-cell score both have the same expectation at 9 cells and at 1,050; only their sampling error shrinks. The top-m channel is an order statistic, which is the one summary that keeps meaning when the population changes size. And because the cell axis survives the filter, a later stage can distinguish many cells firing once from one cell firing many times — which is the property the tube’s docstring claimed, that the one-vote probe took away, and that the count-leak foot gun turns on.

What it costs. The per-cell stage runs N times instead of once. At 1,050 cells that is the whole question. tube scans a held-out fold in 0.014 s; tiny, which already pays this, takes 0.23 s — sixteen times more, and still 30,000× realtime. A budget has to be stated before this is built, not discovered after.

⚠ This class has already failed once in this tree, and that is the most important thing about it. tiny is a member of the family — per-cell filter, bounded vote, pooling by rate band — and it scored 0.125 across every run, with its threshold on the edge of its grid in 4 folds of 4. So chorus is not a new idea here; it is the idea that did not train. Three things differ, and none of them has been tested: tiny pools with an unbounded sum within each band, which puts the count leak straight back; it trains at a tenth of tube’s learning rate, so the architecture comparison was never controlled; and it normalizes nothing at the pool. Step 0 of this proposal is therefore not to build anything. It is to re-run tiny at tube’s learning rate with pos_weight and batch size held fixed. That is one run, and it decides whether this class is alive.

What it would have to show. That it trains at all, with a threshold off the edge of its grid. F1 within seed error of tube at home. Carried to the Cossart spec without refitting, F1 above 0.5 — the bar is CoactDetect’s 0.789, and tube’s 0.256 is the floor to beat. And a participation ladder that rises with the number of co-active cells at every field size, not only at 32.

B · gauge — build the bar from the recording being judged

The row it fixes: rate scaling, in the second moment as well as the first — and cardinality, for free, as a consequence.

The principle, and it is the one measurement on this page I would build on. CoactDetect scores 0.790 refitted on a 566-cell field and 0.789 carried over unchanged. A gap of 0.001 across a seventeen-fold change in field size is not a property of its statistic — its statistic is a coactivity count, the same quantity the tube reads. It is a property of its bar, which is a percentile of a null built by circularly shifting that recording’s own cells. The hand-written detectors that travel are exactly the ones that compare against a within-recording null; the two that do not travel, rate+context and SPIKE-synchronization, carry an absolute threshold. rate+context finds 120 of 120 planted events on the Cossart field and still scores 0.168, because a bar that is right for 32 cells is tripped constantly by 566.

So: stop treating calibration as something done to a model after it is trained, and make it a layer inside the model.

# gauge wraps ANY feature stage. `roll_rows` shifts each cell's row on its own.
f_obs = feat(x)                                     # (B, C, T)
f_null = stack([feat(roll_rows(x, g)) for g in gens])   # (n_null, B, C, T)
z = (f_obs - f_null.mean(0)) / (f_null.std(0) + eps)
return head(z)                                      # downstream is pivotal

Why it is the right shape. The tube’s own screen document identified this in one sentence and then did not act on it: area-normalized subtraction “cancels the mean of a rate change and not its variance.” The guard changed where the reference is measured; the ratio changed how the comparison is formed. Neither divided by the null’s spread, which is the term that actually holds a false-alarm rate constant. This is constant-false-alarm-rate detection with the reference estimated by resampling instead of by assuming a noise model — and the resampling is a circular shift, which costs one tensor operation.

What it costs. B+1 passes through the feature stage. At B = 15 that is 16×, which on tube’s 0.014 s per fold is still a fifth of a second. Two reductions are available and both need measuring rather than assuming: draw the surrogate set once per training window instead of once per step, and at inference estimate the null on a coarse sliding block rather than per frame.

⚠ This class inherits the surrogate problem, whole. A per-cell circular shift is the construction this project has spent weeks finding leaks in. It preserves each cell’s own rate and intervals exactly, which is what makes it a clean null for cross-cell timing — and the rigid-shift work has already shown that a shift can leak through shared modulation and through what happens at the window edge. gauge’s surrogate must clear the same leak screen as any training surrogate before the layer is trusted. One thing does differ, and it is worth saying: here the surrogate is a calibration reference inside a supervised network, not a training negative, so a leak biases the bar rather than manufacturing a learning signal out of nothing. That is a smaller failure, not an absent one.

What it would have to show. That the false-alarm rate per hour is flat across a field-size sweep from 9 to 1,050 cells and across a background-rate sweep from 1× to 8×. The existing rate-invariance probe already has exactly that shape and the tube fails it 66 → 890; the same probe re-run is the test. Then: carried to the Cossart spec without refitting, within 0.05 F1 of its own refitted score — CoactDetect’s 0.001 is the standard to be measured against. And the cost inside a stated budget.

C · quorum — decide by rank, not by count and not by fraction

The row it fixes: heterogeneous per-cell rates — the one nothing in the estate touches — and cardinality, with the right shape rather than a chosen one.

The principle. Figure 2 says the honest floor lies strictly between a fixed count and a fixed fraction, and grows sub-linearly with the field. Neither rule on offer has that shape, so neither should be hard-coded. Give the network the shape and let it fit the exponent. And score each cell against its own history rather than the field average, which is what makes the decision variable a rank — and a rank’s null distribution does not care how the rates are distributed.

# quorum. `occ` is each cell's own occupancy over a long trailing window.
s = x * -(occ(x, w).clamp_min(eps).log())        # a quiet cell counts for more
m = (a * N ** b).ceil().clamp(1, N)              # b fitted, initialised at 0.5
q = soft_topk_mean(s, m, dim=1)                  # an order statistic over CELLS
return head(q)

Why the cell axis and not the time axis. This is ordered-statistic constant-false-alarm-rate detection, and it was already on the list once: V3 of the tube screen, the censored surround, which was deliberately left out because an order statistic over a sliding time window is not a convolution and spends the one property the tube actually owns — 0.014 s to scan a fold. Over the cell axis the cost is a different shape entirely: a partial sort of N values per frame, which is one kernel, not a sliding window. That distinction is the reason this is worth running where V3 was not.

What it costs. A top-k over the cell axis per frame. Cheaper than chorus, more than tube. The per-cell occupancy is a box filter and is free.

⚠ Two things to watch. An order statistic is not differentiable at ties, so this needs a soft sort or a straight-through estimator, and which one is a real choice rather than a detail. And the fitted exponent is a new free parameter, so the tube’s own rule applies to it: if a fitted value runs to the end of its range, the model is telling you the range was wrong. Report the range with the value, every time.

What it would have to show. That the fitted exponent lands strictly inside its bounds — if it goes to 0 or 1, one of the two rules was right after all, and that is a result worth having either way. That recall holds when a tenth of the cells are made ten times busier than the rest, which is the heterogeneity probe nothing in the estate currently runs. And the participation ladder, again, at every field size.

What I would not build, and why

The obvious fourth candidate is the assembly-detection family — principal or independent components over co-activation, graph and item-set methods — which is where the coordination literature actually concentrates and which would handle cell identity natively. It cannot be scored here yet. The generator draws each planted event’s participants fresh, so no group of cells ever recurs, and every one of those methods works by finding patterns that repeat. Run one on this bench and it finds nothing, and the zero would be about the generator rather than the method. The order that makes it possible is already written down: ask the 85 real recordings whether participant groups recur, plant them if they do, then port. Until then a comparison against that family would be won meaninglessly.

6 · how to run them

Two things the bench cannot currently measure, and both come first

Neither of these is about the proposals. Both are gaps that would make any result from them unreadable, and both are cheap.

A field-size axis in the generator

Every bench recording today has one field size, taken from one spec. There is therefore no run in this project that varies field size with everything else held fixed — the only evidence is the cross-lab transfer, where field size and per-cell rate moved together by 17.7× and 19.6×. Field size is already a parameter the spec carries, so the axis is a sweep rather than new machinery, and it is what turns “the tube does not travel” into a statement about which of the two changes broke it.

A quiet-field negative

The promiscuity probe is a hot window only, so a divide-shaped detector passes it by arithmetic and the ratio arm’s 0.0 measures nothing. All three proposals divide or standardize, so all three would inherit the same free pass. The cheap version is a low-rate stretch with nothing planted, scored the same way, so the hot count gains a twin. Until it exists, no detector’s rate robustness has been measured — only half of it.

Then, in this order

  1. Re-run tiny under control. One run, no new code: tube’s learning rate, pos_weight and batch size fixed. It decides whether chorus is a live class or a repeat, and it also closes the standing complaint that the architecture conclusion was never controlled.
  2. gauge, because it has the strongest prior evidence. A gap of 0.001 across a 17× field change is the best transfer result anyone here has measured, and this is the attempt to buy it inside a learned model. It is also the smallest change: it wraps the existing feature stage and leaves the tube’s kernel alone, so the control is exact.
  3. quorum. Needs the soft-sort decision made first, and needs the heterogeneity probe written, which does not exist yet.
  4. chorus, if step 1 says the class is alive.

They compose, and that is a reason to run them one at a time. gauge is a layer that can wrap either pooling; quorum is a pooling that can sit inside chorus. So the eventual screen is a 2×2×2 with the shipped tube in the corner. Running the factorial before any single axis has been measured would repeat the mistake the tube screen names in its own text — and that screen at least had a measured interaction to justify crossing its two axes.

⚠ And the standing limitation, which bites hardest here. Every learned number in this project is one training run per fold. Going from 8 seeds to 24 already moved tube from 0.686 to 0.656 and reordered the leaders — LoCo and CoactDetect swap places between the two runs. A comparison between architectures is exactly where that thinness is least affordable, so any screen of these three needs its seed budget decided up front rather than after a result looks interesting.
7 · the limits of this page

What none of this establishes

  • Nothing here was trained, traced or scored. Every performance number is read out of an artifact already committed in the tree and named where it is used. The three architectures are designs; no line of them has been written.
  • The chance floor assumes independent cells at one rate. Real cells have a hard same-cell interval floor and heterogeneous rates, and those two errors push the true floor in opposite directions. Figure 2 is an ordering, not an operating point.
  • Field size and per-cell rate are confounded in every transfer number quoted. The two corpora differ by 117× in field size and 19.6× in rate, together. Which one breaks the tube is not measured, and the field-size axis in §6 is what would measure it.
  • The Cossart transfer table rests on an unreviewed spec. Its background shape is inherited from this lab, because that export yielded no usable baseline window, and a background is not a detail for detectors that estimate one. Those numbers are the best evidence in the tree and they are not publishable.
  • No prior-art pass has been run on any of the three. The set-encoder, constant-false-alarm-rate and order-statistic literatures are named because that is where each idea comes from, and naming a lineage is not the same as establishing that a combination is new. This project’s own earlier prior-art pass on learned coordination detection was retracted internally as too shallow to stand on.
  • This page has not been murderboarded, on instruction. It is working material for sessions in this tree.
