## 1. The spine — one sentence per section, its CLAIM, not its title

0. **Lede.** Tuning the nets' merge gap moves each chorus net toward CoactDetect and settles nothing that was open: under the budget CoactDetect still leads everywhere, and on F1 alone the sign of the lead depends on how the failed trainings are counted.
1. **The question.** The coded detectors' searches tuned their merge gap and the nets did not — they decoded at a fixed 2 s — so the fair comparison held one untested asymmetry, and the project lead asked for it to be measured. (Second paragraph switches claim: *no net was retrained; 3,881 saved fits were re-decoded at every gap in the grid.*)
2. **How the gap was chosen.** The gap was chosen by the run's own rules — training folds only, an 8-point grid, two objectives, goal 1's move rule including the crowded-recording check, two variants — and at 2 s the re-decoding reproduces the run exactly, not approximately.
3. **On F1 alone.** With the gap tuned, the chorus nets' margin against CoactDetect improves but stays near the 0.010 F1 noise scale, and whether either net ends ahead depends on a post hoc rule about refits that failed to train.
4. **Under the budget.** A wider gap lets a net fit the budget at a lower threshold, so every chorus net closes 10% to 45% of its shortfall — and every net still trails on average in both draws, with CoactDetect ahead in 29 of 32 folds.
5. **What the crowded check refused.** In all 64 choices the inner fits preferred a *wider* gap than the one taken, and the crowded-recording check refused it every time — because the bench's recordings (events ≥120 s apart) cannot penalise fusing, and the crowded recordings can.
6. **Every net, both draws (Table 1).** The full 16-row grid of net-minus-CoactDetect means; and (closing paragraph, a different claim) re-choosing the configuration changed it in 16 of 64 choices, 12 of which the run never refitted, so that column is mostly empty.
7. **Limits.** The report's limits hold; the nets' side is now tuned inside the crowded check while the coded side is not; the move rule favours the as-run choice; nothing was retrained; four folds.
8. **Where everything is.** Tool, outputs, tests, and the cost of regenerating the arrays.
9. **Provenance.** Build stamp.

**The arc I judged against** (declared, per my checklist): the default analysis arc — *the problem → what it costs → the method applied to it → what the method gets wrong → the fix/answer → the evidence → the residual risk*. This page is an addendum answering one question, so the legitimate local form is *the open question → what turns on it → how it was measured → the answer → why the answer is bounded the way it is → the evidence → the limits*. ## 3. Direct answers to the four questions asked

- **Opens with the problem or the procedure?** Neither, strictly. It opens with the **answer** (a bluff lede carrying eight numbers), then with the **procedure** (§1's second paragraph and all of §2). The *problem* — the coded detectors tuned their merge gap and the nets did not — is one sentence inside §1 and never appears in the lede. A bluff lede is a defensible deviation from the default arc, but an unstated one, and it is missing the half-sentence that would make it self-standing (findings 1, 3, 10).
- **Is the most interesting finding where a reader will meet it?** No. §5 is the fifth of eight sections, absent from the lede, and unconnected to the binned SCE veto that makes it matter (findings 1, 2).
- **Does §2 sit between the reader and the result?** Yes — it is the page's longest pre-result block, and it front-loads the crowded check as procedure three sections before that check becomes the finding (finding 3).
- **Does each section follow, and does the page end where it should?** The tail is right (limits → pointers → provenance). The middle is not: the lede and the body order the two results oppositely (4), the evidence table arrives after the prose that recites it (5), the re-chosen variant is explained after it has twice been shown (6), and the page never restates its answer before hedging it (8).
- **Is the result stated once?** No — lede, §3, §4 and Table 1 each carry the same numbers; §7 partially restates it again (finding 5).
