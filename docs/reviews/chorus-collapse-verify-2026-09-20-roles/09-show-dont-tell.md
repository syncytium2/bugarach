**Conventions I used, and how I tuned them.** The checklist's thresholds are written for slides; this is a scrolling report, so I state my adaptation rather than pretend the slide numbers applied. A "screen" = 900 px. First figure must land inside screen 1. A section's figure should hold ≥50% of the section's vertical extent. Two or more consecutive figureless sections is a flag. For text blocks, the raw 60-word rule would flag 14 of 16 body paragraphs on a technical report page and tell you nothing, so I used **≥150 words** for a flagged block and kept the 60-word line only for blocks that are *summary* rather than argument (the "The answer" box). All percentages below are measured on the render, never on requested widths.

## The three tables, answered directly

- **Table 1 (collapse by shape) — keep it a table.** Its cell denominators vary from 36 to 180 fits, which is precisely what a bar chart would flatten and mislead on, and Figure 2 already carries the pattern at finer grain. One linkage gap worth closing: Figure 2's marks are per-configuration shares "of its 36 inner fits", while Table 1's cells pool 1–5 configurations; nothing tells the reader that, so the two objects look inconsistent. Add a "configurations" count column, or a line in the tnote.
- **Table 2 (what tuning chose) — keep it, and add the bars (F4).** The table is the record; the four stacked bars are the reading. Not either/or.
- **Table 3 (warm-up fit by fit) — keep it a table, but it is currently broken (F1)** and Figure 7 is already its chart. Its job is to be the per-fit key to Figure 7's overplotted curves — which it cannot do while the outcome column is off-screen, and does only weakly anyway because no curve in Figure 7 can be traced to a row. If Figure 7 ever labels its one non-training curve with that fit's id, the table and the figure lock together.

## Redundancy across the seven figures

No two figures show the same thing, and the family resemblances are the good kind: Figures 3 and 4 share a dot-strip idiom and the blue/orange/hollow-on-floor convention; Figures 6 and 7 are the same loss chart at different scopes (one fit × five conditions; eight fits × one condition). The one genuine duplication is the **collapsed fit's as-run loss curve, drawn in both Figure 5B and Figure 6 at different y-scales** (F11) — fix the scale and it reads as deliberate continuity rather than a coincidence. Nothing should merge; **Figure 6 should gain a panel** (F9).
