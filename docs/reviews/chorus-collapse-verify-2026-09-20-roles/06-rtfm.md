## Findings

No blocking findings. The page's central result — collapsed fits have a silent head layer and a flat output, it tracks lr 0.03, and a 200-step warm-up prevents it — survives everything I could throw at it. The problems below are in how three claims are *framed* and in what four numbers actually count.

### MAJOR

**1 · §5 and the summary bullet — "6 of 7 … against 1.4 of 7" compares two different metrics.**
`wu_ok` counts replays whose **final training loss** is below 0.5 (builder l. 988, `trains = lambda d: final(d) < 0.5`). The 1.4 baseline is `reroll = math.fsum(1 − share(cfg))` (l. 1030), where `share` is the configuration's **collapse rate** — one call per recording on the folds it was *scored* on. Those are different measurements on different data. The page's sentence *"a change that merely re-drew each fit's luck would train each as often as its configuration's fits train, 1.4 of 7"* silently assumes loss < 0.5 and non-collapse are the same event; the only evidence offered for that is n = 2 (the assert `not trains(ref) and trains(work_fit)`). The same substitution appears one clause earlier: *"A replay with nothing changed reproduces the collapse, so doing nothing trains none of them"* — determinism establishes that the **calls** outcome reproduces, not that the loss stays above 0.5. This is exactly the "does the metric count what the prose says it counts" trap, and it sits under the page's headline result.
*Severity:* major. *Fix:* either score the 8 warm-up replays on held-out calls — the shipped criterion, which is what "trains" means everywhere else on the page — or state plainly that the baseline is a collapse rate, that the outcome is a loss, and that the comparison assumes they coincide. The disclosure already present ("These runs were judged by training loss, not by calls on held-out recordings") covers the outcome but not the baseline.
*Verifiable against a source:* **yes** (builder ll. 988, 1024, 1030; `collapse_table` l. 119).

**2 · §2 — "the seed's effect cannot be put down to its starting weights" asserts absence from an underpowered null.**
I reproduced the statistic independently: r = 0.2381, one-sided permutation p = 0.1517 over 200,000 draws (page: 0.24, p = 0.147 over 1,999). It is 60 points from 20 configurations, ~37 effective df. Fisher-z gives a 95 % CI of roughly **[−0.08, +0.51]** — the point estimate is *positive* and the interval is compatible with a substantial starting-weight carry-over. "does not do so in the other" and "cannot be put down to its starting weights" are both statements of absence. The page applies the correct standard two sentences later ("No fold-pair effect was **detected**"), so the inconsistency is internal.
*Severity:* major — the whole "the recordings it picks are at least as likely" reading of §2 hangs on this, and it is an accept-the-null.
*Fix:* "no carry-over was detected (r = 0.24, p = 0.147, 1,999 shuffles), but the test is compatible with anything up to about r = 0.5, so a starting-weight effect is not established rather than excluded; the recordings the seed picks are an equally good explanation."
*Verifiable:* **yes** (recomputed from `collapse_table.json`).

**3 · §4 — "lr 0.03 is 30 times that paper's suggested step size of 0.001" is the wrong yardstick for Adam.**
It is the page's only quantitative statement about why 0.03 is large, and Adam's α is not a gradient multiplier. Kingma & Ba §2.1: the effective step is approximately bounded by α *in parameter space* (`−α ⪅ Δt ⪅ +α`, "conceptually similar to … a trust region"), and bias correction makes step 1 exactly `±α` per parameter whatever the gradient. So the informative comparison is α against the **initialization scale**. PyTorch's `Conv1d.reset_parameters` gives `U(±1/√fan_in)`; the head's layers 2–8 have `fan_in = 8×3 = 24` (RMS ≈ 0.118) and layer 1 has 36 or 72 (RMS ≈ 0.096 / 0.068). **One Adam step at lr 0.03 moves every weight and every conv bias by 25–44 % of its initial magnitude**; four consistent steps flip a sign, and ~150 push a bias past x = −4 where `GELU′ = −5.0×10⁻⁴` and the unit is both flat and gradient-free. That is a mechanism the page's own warm-up result supports, and it is checkable. Meanwhile "30× the default" is misleading in the other direction: the grid's *lowest* rate, 0.003, is already 3× the Adam default, so the framing implies 0.03 is an outlier when the whole grid sits above 0.001. Kingma & Ba also scope their number ("for the tested machine learning problems"), which "suggested step size" drops.
*Severity:* major (framing of the page's core independent variable).
*Fix:* replace with the parameter-scale comparison — "at lr 0.03 Adam's first step moves every head weight by about a quarter of its initial magnitude (the weights start in ±1/√24); the whole grid sits above Adam's 0.001 default" — and drop or footnote the 30×.
*Verifiable:* **yes** (Adam §2.1 fetched; `_dilated_stack`; PyTorch init).

**4 · §4 — "counted 1 working fit as dead" is a chorus_norm-only count printed in a both-nets sentence; the true figure is 5, and the rule is misdescribed.**
From `census.json`, `min_share_positive == 0` marks **1** chorus_norm working fit *and* **4** chorus_gain_norm working fits dead — five in all — and it also **misses 3 collapsed chorus_gain_norm fits** (72 of 75 sign-dead vs 75 of 75 variation-silent) that the variation rule catches. The builder only ever computes `n_sign[('chorus_norm', False)]` (l. 1249), so the other 7 disagreements never reach the page, in the very sentence that justifies the page's central measure. Separately, the implemented sign rule is `(inner > 1e-3).any(dim=1)` — "ever rose above 0.001", not "ever went positive"; a GELU output in (0, 0.001] is positive and still counted dead.
*Severity:* major (a reported count that understates by 5× what it is offered as evidence for — and correcting it strengthens the page).
*Fix:* "counted 5 working fits as dead (1 chorus_norm, 4 chorus_gain_norm) and missed 3 collapsed chorus_gain_norm fits", and describe the round-1 rule as "whether a unit ever rose above 0.001".
*Verifiable:* **yes** (recomputed from `census.json`).

### MINOR

**5 · §2 — the permutation tests' resolution is not reported, and the third decimal is noise.**
`N_PERM = 1999` (builder l. 429) appears nowhere on the page; the only methods note is "collapse labels shuffled within each configuration". p is printed to three decimals throughout. Rerunning with an independent stream and 50,000 draws: chorus_gain_norm fold-pair p = **0.455** where the page prints **0.434**; chorus_norm fold-pair 0.934 vs 0.938; seed-in-first-draw 0.082 vs 0.083; seed-in-second-draw 0.0023 vs 0.002. The floor is 1/2000 = 0.0005. *Fix:* state "1,999 shuffles (p resolution 0.0005)" once, and print two significant figures. *Verifiable:* **yes.**
*(The null itself is right: labels shuffled within each (draw, configuration) conditions on the configuration's own collapse count, which is the correct exchangeability, and the statistic `len(vals)·Σv² − (Σv)²` ranks shuffles exactly as the across-level variance does. The shorter step-count twins are correctly excluded.)*

**6 · §2 — "111 … 111.5 fits expected" is given without dispersion.**
The formula is correct and unbiased (`Σ_c k₁k₂/n`, builder l. 892; I confirmed every configuration has exactly 18 fits in *both* draws, so the shared denominator is safe). But under that null the overlap is a sum of independent hypergeometrics with **SD = 2.50** for chorus_norm (observed 111 vs 111.50, z = −0.20) and **SD = 2.30** for chorus_gain_norm (38 vs 39.61, z = −0.70). "111 vs 111.5" reads as an exact match; the test can only exclude an excess of about ±5 fits, which is the number a reader needs before accepting "nothing is left for the particular fit". *Fix:* add the null SD or a ±2 SD band. *Verifiable:* **yes.**

**7 · §4 / §7 — the absolute silent threshold cannot be sensitivity-tested from the shipped data, and the separation is one unit wide for a large minority of working fits.**
`census.json` stores only `min_share_varying` — already thresholded at 10⁻³ — with no per-layer SDs and no layer scales, so the relative alternative the page itself cites (Sokar's Definition 3.1, |activation| normalized by the layer's mean) cannot be computed without re-running all 974 fits on a GPU. And the margin is thin: **34 of 279 working chorus_norm fits sit at `min_share_varying = 0.125`** — one of eight units varying — with 23 more at 0.25. §7's caveat is scoped to "at the starting weights"; the census claim is the one that carries the result and is not covered.
The *conclusion* is nevertheless safe and the page should say why: **169 of 974 fits have an output SD of exactly 0.0 in float64**, and the collapsed/working output-SD gap is 2.3×10⁻⁴ vs 0.82 — a bit-identical output over 26,000 frames is not a scale artefact. So it is the definition, not the finding, that is untested.
*Fix:* store per-layer SD and per-layer mean |output| in `census.json`, report the counts at a Sokar-style relative threshold beside the absolute one, and state the 169-exactly-zero fact where the absolute threshold is introduced. *Verifiable:* **yes** (recomputed), *re-running at a relative threshold:* **no** (no torch here).

**8 · §4 / Figs 3 & 5 — the trim is correct but its justification lives only in the source.**
I verified it: head radius Σ_{i=0..7} 2ⁱ = **255** frames; encoder radius 2^depth − 1 = 15 (depth 4) or **63** (depth 6); total **318** each way, so EDGE = 400 clears it with 82 frames of margin, and `receptive_field(8) = 511` samples = 51.1 s. The builder's comment (l. 61–64) says all of this; the page says only "400 frames trimmed from each end, where zero padding makes even a constant layer wiggle". 400 is the single number the entire silent-layer measure rests on and a reader cannot check it. *Fix:* "the net sees at most 318 frames (31.8 s) each way, so 400 clears the padding." *Verifiable:* **yes.**

**9 · §4 — "A GELU passes positive input" is not true, and the −0.17 depends on the exact form.**
GELU(x) = x·Φ(x), so it *scales* positive input: GELU(0.1) = 0.054 (54 %), GELU(0.5) = 0.345, GELU(1) = 0.841. Only asymptotically does it approach identity; "passes" is the ReLU property. The −0.17 is right for the exact-erf GELU that `nn.GELU()` builds by default — minimum **−0.169971 at x = −0.75179** — but the tanh approximation bottoms at **−0.170041**, just *below* −0.17, so the claim is form-dependent and worth pinning. "still carries a gradient" is true with two caveats that matter here: GELU′ is exactly 0 at the minimum (x = −0.7518), and it decays to nothing in the tail (GELU′(−4) = −5.0×10⁻⁴, GELU′(−5) = −7.1×10⁻⁶) — which is itself part of why a silenced layer does not recover. *Fix:* "a GELU scales positive input toward itself and maps negative input to a dip no lower than −0.17 (at x ≈ −0.75), which still carries a gradient except far into the negative tail." *Verifiable:* **yes** (computed on a 20 M-point grid).

**10 · §5 / §7 — "reproduced its saved checkpoint to the last bit" overstates what was compared, and omits its conditions.**
`max_abs_diff_vs_checkpoint` is a max over `model.state_dict()` only (builder ll. 417–420) and is 0.0 in both as-run replays — which *is* bit-exact for the weights, and `checkpoint.py`'s JSON round-trip (`float(x)` from a float32 `tolist()`, back to float32) preserves it. But the checkpoint also carries the threshold, merge gap, encoding contract and provenance, none of which is in the comparison; and bit-exactness held under conditions the page never states — the replay asserts the **same GPU** (`NVIDIA RTX A4000`), runs under `deterministic_cuda()`, pins torch to one thread, and recorded torch 2.14.0+cu126. *Fix:* "every weight tensor matched its saved checkpoint exactly, on the same GPU and torch build, with deterministic kernels and one thread." *Verifiable:* **yes.**

**11 · §4 — the architecture description omits the standardization window, which the architecture's own source flags as a trap.**
`chorus.py`'s docstring: *"The statistics are taken over whatever window the model is given, so a 4,096-frame training crop and a whole recording at inference are standardised over different spans."* The page says only "standardizes the encoder's output over time". The consequence for this page: Figure 3 measures silence on a **26,796-frame** recording and Figure 5 on **4,096-frame** crops — a 6.5× difference in the normalization span — and both are presented as the same "silent layer" quantity. Figure 5's caption notes the *data* differ but not that the net normalizes differently on them. *Fix:* one clause in §4 and one in Figure 5's caption. *Verifiable:* **yes.**

**12 · Figure 5's silence measure pools across the batch; Figure 3's does not.**
`_head_probe` (census) takes `o[0][:, 400:-400].std(dim=1)` — SD over time within one recording. The replay hook takes `o[..., 400:-400].transpose(0,1).reshape(C, -1).std(dim=1)` — SD pooled over **batch and time together**, across the 3 crops. A unit that is constant within each crop but at a different constant per crop counts as *varying* in Figure 5 and would count as *silent* by Figure 3's rule. That is the permissive direction for "the working fit's head wakes by step 50". *Fix:* say the training-time measure pools the batch, or take it per crop. *Verifiable:* **yes** (builder ll. 228 vs 373–375).

**13 · §4 / Figure 4 — the "as deaf as plain chorus" cut is an undisclosed max of three seeds, borrowed across architectures.**
`deaf = max(plain)` = 0.00028068 — the largest of exactly three untrained plain-chorus seeds. The two untrained controls also differ in more than `norm`: plain chorus is built at the registry defaults (encoder 4 × 4) and chorus_norm at Figure 1's configuration (4 × 6). And the probe is not architecture-neutral: on an all-zero window the encoder's output is exactly constant, so `norm` divides by `eps = 10⁻⁶` and one onset produces a near-saturated vote change — which is why untrained chorus_norm reads 0.02–0.03 against plain chorus's 0.0003. So a chorus_norm fit called "as deaf as plain chorus" is really ~100× below **its own** untrained baseline, which is the more informative statement. *Fix:* say the cut is the largest of three untrained plain-chorus seeds, note the encoder shapes differ, and state the within-architecture comparison. *Verifiable:* **yes.**

**14 · §5 vs §7 — the same "8" counts two different things.**
§5: "tried on 8 more collapsed fits … 6 of 7 distinct runs". §7: "tried on 8 distinct collapsed runs". Both are internally correct — 8 replays of which two are one trajectory, giving 7 distinct, plus Figure 1's own fit = 8 distinct in all — but the collision reads as a contradiction. I verified all 8 are genuinely collapsed inner fits in `collapse_table.json`, and that the kept twin is the longer one (2736f584, 1,800 steps), matching Table 3's annotation on 9ad792aa. *Fix:* "7 distinct runs, and with Figure 1's own fit, 8 distinct collapsed runs in all." *Verifiable:* **yes.**

**15 · Intro / §2 — "the whole recording … F1 0.125" is inferred, not measured, for 227 of the 228 collapsed fits.**
The implemented collapse test is only `n_detected == 1` (builder l. 119); nothing checks that the one call spans the recording or that F1 is 0.125. The arithmetic is right (15 events in every bench seed; 1 TP / 14 FN / 0 FP → 2/16 = 0.125) and two asserts make it near-certain (every collapsed fit's threshold is the grid floor; collapsed output SD ≤ 2.3×10⁻⁴), but the span and the F1 are directly observed only for the fit in Figure 1, whose call is `[0, 26796]` of 26,796 frames. *Fix:* "made exactly one call on every recording it was scored on — in the fit shown, the whole recording, an F1 of 0.125." *Verifiable:* **yes.**

**16 · Numerical reproducibility — the two named traps are genuinely closed; one residual.**
I re-ran the rebuild test here (Python 3.12.5, numpy 2.5.3, Windows) and the committed HTML matches byte-for-byte. The defences are real, not decorative: the cluster statistic is integer-valued (`len(vals)·Σv² − (Σv)²`), so 3.12's compensated `sum()` cannot move it; the carry-over correlation uses integer deviations `3x − Σv` over a denominator that is **invariant under the permutation**, so near-ties compare exactly rather than through a float ratio; `np.random.RandomState` is the version-stable legacy stream and is consumed in a deterministic order fixed by the JSON row order; `math.fsum` covers the two float reductions and `statistics.mean/median` are order-exact. Two residuals worth a line: (a) `page()` states its claims as bare `assert`s, so `python -O` or `PYTHONOPTIMIZE=1` would rebuild the page with **every self-check silently disabled** — the test asserts the bytes match, not that `__debug__` is on; (b) `logit_sd` is a numpy `.std()` over ~26,000 float64 values, whose pairwise-summation order is a numpy-build detail — immaterial to the page (which reads the stored JSON) but it means `census.json` is not bit-reproducible if regenerated on another numpy/platform, which the page's "a test rebuilds the page from them" could be read as promising. *Severity:* minor. *Fix:* raise instead of assert (or assert `__debug__` in the test), and scope the §8 rebuild claim to the page rather than the data. *Verifiable:* **yes.**
