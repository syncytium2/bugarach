## What a cold reader sees, figure by figure, and the false-friend test

- **Fig 1:** a black-and-white spike raster of 33 rows, sparse on the left and dense in a block from 20 to 25 min under a hatched band. Three down-triangles sit above. - **Fig 2:** four rows of four boxes each, flowing left to right into "event probability", with the first box after "raster" shaded. It reads as a pipeline diagram. Pass.
- **Fig 3:** on the left, a 4×4 grid with a dark diagonal marked "scored once"; on the right, a 3×3 grid with a blue diagonal marked "scores". This is the standard cross-validation diagram, used with its standard meaning. Pass.
- **Fig 4:** two stacks of four strips made of square cells. In A, three rows share the same five blue cells at the far left. In B, each row's blue cells are spread across the three non-dark blocks. The cells are gridded with gaps, so it does not read as a raster. **Partial risk:** the x direction has no axis label, so a stranger's default reading is left-to-right = time (a Gantt chart or an ethogram). The fold ranges ("fold 1: seeds 1000–1011" …) sit below the legend, away from both panels. Minor.
- **Fig 5:** a grey dot cloud rising to the right. A dashed vertical line shades the region to its right; there are two circled points and an orange square. It reads like a false-positive vs performance trade-off plot, and the axes do mean that here. Pass. Minor: the x label folds two different rates onto one axis.
