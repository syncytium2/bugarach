## Answers to the specific questions

**Does the problem arrive before the solution?** Yes — §3 precedes §5, correctly. But the problem is stated as *background*, not as a problem the reader feels: apparatus first, definitions second, crux third of three bullets (A-3), and the lede that frames the whole document leads with a detector inventory (A-2). The material is all there; it is in ascending order of importance where it needs descending.

**Is "What we are asking for" right at position 2?** *For:* it is a circulated-for-critique memo; a reviewer deciding whether to spend an hour deserves to know what kind of attention is wanted, and the section prevents the masthead being read as a results claim. *Against:* two of the three questions cannot be understood at position 2 — "surrogate" and "control" are both undefined there — so it buys orientation with a stretch of unintelligible text, and §13 already asks four more questions of the same reader eleven sections later. **Recommendation: split it.** The genre frame is intelligible cold and should stay at the top; the three specific questions are not, and should close the document merged with Open (A-1, A-6).

**Does the case build, or front-load?** It front-loads the *ask* and back-loads the *payoff*. The two claims most likely to change a reviewer's mind — the drift confound dissolved by dither, and the control that doubles as a real-data threshold calibrator — sit at positions 6 (well placed, as the climax of three reasons) and 9 (badly placed, and boxed). The drift argument is where it belongs. The calibration argument is not.

**Is anything load-bearing buried?** Yes, and it is exactly the one named in the brief: the control-as-threshold-calibrator at position 9, inside a panel, at the end of a section the corpus already pushed out of the design chain. See A-5.

**Does "Where we are" belong before the proposal?** Yes — it is load-bearing for §5 and §7 both, which cite it by reference. Keep it. Compress the lineage and lift the provenance caveat (A-9, A-11, A-12).

**Falsification / Limits / Open — one job?** Three jobs, distinct, but wrongly ordered and split by an audience aside. Do not merge Falsification into Limits; do merge Open into the closing ask; reorder to Limits → Falsification → closing ask, with the producer notes last (A-6, A-7, A-8).

**A reader who stops halfway?** Yes, comfortably — by the end of §6 (roughly 45% by word count) they have the problem, the critique, the construction, and why the surrogate choice is the whole design. This is the document's strongest structural property and every recommendation above preserves it. A reader who stops at **25%**, however, has the asks and the problem but not the thesis, and has been asked to judge two things they have not been shown — which is what A-1 fixes.

**Do opening sentences tell the reader why they are still reading?** In §5, §6, §7, §9, §12 — yes, and conspicuously well. In §1, §3, §4, §8 — no; they open on inventory or taxonomy and let the `h2` carry the claim (A-10).
