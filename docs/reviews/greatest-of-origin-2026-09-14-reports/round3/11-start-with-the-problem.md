## 1. The spine of §4.1, one claim per unit

1. Nobody has established where greatest-of began. This document used to credit a 1973 Hansen conference paper, which nobody here has read. The sources we hold describe greatest-of work from 1972 on and credit different people. Nothing in §4 depends on the answer.
2. The search: one notice confirms the 1973 author and title, but two sources give different ISBNs. A scan exists that nobody has searched. Two paper databases could not be queried and two others have no full text. No library request has been made.
3. (Lead-in) Here is what the sources we hold say, oldest first.
   - 3a. Hansen & Sawyers 1980 cite the 1973 paper for a greatest-of loss rule, so by their account it covered greatest-of. They also cite a 1972 Sawyers memo, which is the earliest dated treatment in our sources. Their results come from "independent work performed by the two authors".
   - 3b. A 1980 patent says the 1973 paper proposed a CFAR processor for Weibull clutter. The processor it reproduces averages both sides, and the patent never mentions greatest-of. It gives pages 1–8.
   - 3c. Rohling 1983 credits Moore et al. with proposing greatest-of, cites only the 1980 paper, and does not cite 1973. His "proposed" cannot mean first.
   - 3d. Gandhi & Kassam 1988 say Hansen 1973 proposed greatest-of. They give pages 325–332 and call the venue an IEEE conference.
4. What we do not know: whether it began with the memo, the 1973 paper or earlier work; what the paper and the memo contain; whether Hansen's other early papers bear on it.

**Arc used.** The brief's arc: the open question → what was claimed before, and that it is withdrawn → the search → what we know → what we don't know. Against the default analysis arc, "what it costs" is the "nothing depends" sentence and "residual risk" is the unknowns list. The "fix" step is left out on purpose, as the brief says. The spine follows this arc with no unstated deviation.

**Does it open on the problem?** Yes. The first sentence is the open question. It is also good that the "nothing depends" sentence comes after §4's paragraph establishing that `maxlt` is greatest-of, since it relies on that paragraph.

**§4 spine, for judging placement:** the 2×2 is a corner of CFAR → the mapping table (the `maxlt` row points to §4.1) → which papers are on the shelf (points to §4.1) → the censoring attribution correction → `maxlt` matches greatest-of's combination rule → this is convergent rediscovery, and a signal-processing reviewer will know it → **§4.1** → §5.
