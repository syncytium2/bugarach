## Figures: what a cold reader sees in each panel

**Figure 1, the surrogates.** Five stacked black-tick rasters: six rows per panel, time on the x axis over one minute, a note on the right of each. The top raster clearly shows two vertical columns of ticks, the coordinated events. In the rigid-shift raster those columns are gone and each row's pattern has slid. The shared-offset raster keeps both columns, moved right. The per-onset dither raster scrambles within rows. The circular-shift raster scrambles between rows with wraparound.
- This is a genuine raster (time horizontal, one row per cell), so it is not a false friend. It is readable.
- One note is cryptic: "keeps: each ROI's intervals **but one**". The reader is not told that "one" means the interval that straddles the wrap point.

**Figure 2, the leak tests.**
- **Panel A:** accuracy against *J* for four marker kinds. Pink diamonds float near 0.75–0.8, orange triangles sit just above 0.55, and purple dots and open squares hug the dotted 0.5 line. Readable. The legend gives shared offset ("should read chance") and dither ("must separate") a role, but **not the circular shift**, the control the ⚠ paragraph calls the one that proves the test has power.
- **Panel B:** the same layout for the slow stream. The purple rigid-shift dots rise off 0.5 at the two largest *J*, and pink dither climbs with *J*. Readable. The x values (1.4, 2.8 … 44.8) differ from panel A's, and only the prose explains why.
- **Panel C:** filled grey and blue marks in a band near 0.67 at every *J*, and open marks near 0.5 below them. Readable. Its legend draws the filled/open key with **diamonds**, a shape that appears nowhere in the panel. The y label says "cells-mean channels", which is defined 45 lines further down.
- **Panel D:** black dots high (about 0.88), purple diamonds starting at 0.5 and rising to about 0.8, pink squares and open circles scattered around 0.5 with wide bars. Readable. The purple series, the one the argument depends on, has **no role label** ("shared modulation, no events", but what should it do?). The y label uses "hand-built initial bank", which is defined only after the table.

**Figure 3, the bake-off.**
- **Panel A:** a dot plot of held-out F1 by detector. The three coloured counting builds sit at the right with `tube`, CoactDetect and LoCo, and `tiny` and `trace` sit near 0.12. Readable. There are no family separators, although the caption says rows are grouped by family.
- **Panel B:** **this looks like a forest plot, and it is not one.** Horizontal grey lines through a zero line, one row per comparison, with a black filled diamond on each. In a forest plot the filled diamond is the pooled estimate. Here it is *the single fold with the largest difference*, and the pooled mean is a thin vertical tick. A reader who knows meta-analysis sees "`line` beats CoactDetect by +0.18", which is exactly the fold-3 outlier the prose warns against. The labels also say "(second sensor)" and "(bounded vote)", while the table says "concentration channels" and "two changes".
- **Panel C:** three small log-scale line plots headed "÷ burst", "÷ fuzz" and "÷ wave". The lines fall with plant size against burst and rise gently against fuzz, and the grey `tube` line is usually lowest. The shape is readable. The **y axis reads "line plant's response ÷ other plant's"**, and "line plant" appears nowhere in the text (which says "synchronous plant"). It also collides with the model named `line`, so a cold reader takes it as "the `line` model's plant". I could write the "what it shows" sentence only after reading the body text, so this is a defect.

**Figure 4, training without labels.**
- **Panel A:** three strips, one per event-rate cap. Each has x categories supervised, untrained, sim 10 s … count 20 s. Supervised marks sit high (0.5–0.7), untrained marks sit on the floor, trained marks cluster around 0.1–0.35, and black count marks include a "+" at 0.3–0.48. Readable. **Its legend is in panel B**, and the mark shapes for the three count baselines are defined only there.
- **Panel B:** a scatter of F1 against the share of the recording covered. One cluster sits top-left (narrow detections, high F1), one sits at the far right (covers almost everything, F1 about 0.5), and three marks lie in between. Readable, and it makes its point well.
- **Panel C:** a strip chart in four headed groups ("must read chance", "must move", "trained for", "coordination, or slow modulation?"). The chance group sits at 0.5 and the others rise to 0.6–0.95. Readable as a layout. But the note says each cluster holds only "trained on simulated, trained on real, count baselines". **Supervised models are not visibly in the panel**, and the filled marks encode "supervised, *or* trained on real". So the section's key argument, that supervised models read the modulation twin just like the trained ones, cannot be seen in the figure.

**Figure 5, real recordings.** A four-column dot plot with one row per detector, each followed by an "on its rigid shift" row.
- **Panel A:** filled marks near 0.8–1.0 for supervised models and count baselines, and near 0.1–0.2 (sitting on the grey × and | chance marks) for the models trained without labels. Open shift marks sit near 0. Readable, and this is the clearest panel on the page.
- **Panel B:** small shares near the dotted 0.8 % line, with open shift marks further right. Readable.
- **Panels C and D:** overlap shares with | chance ticks. **Every "on its rigid shift" row is blank**, and so is CoactDetect's own row in panel C, with no note saying why. A cold reader suspects missing data.
- Row labels say "**no labels**, J = 10 s". The text says "trained against rigid shift", and Figure 4 says "sim" and "real". Figure 5 never says these are the *real*-trained arms.

## Can each decision in "What waits on Tony" be made from the page?

- **Does slow shared modulation count as coordination?** *Partly.* The question is clear. The page does not say what each answer implies for *J* (if it counts, 10–20 s is right; if not, *J* has to shrink below the modulation timescale). The key fact, how much real recordings co-modulate, is explicitly off-page, on branch `unsup/slow-comodulation`.
- **Does any learned build stay, and which?** *Yes, mostly.* The options (keep registered, or unregister with its tests) and the evidence (F1 differences, probe calls, fit time, real co-activity) are all present. The scope is unclear: "any learned build" against "all three are registered" leaves open whether `tube` and `tube_guard` are also on the table. The fact that `line` and `line_bound` have not been reviewed as code, which bears on this decision, is in "What this does not settle" rather than here.
- **Is the objective worth another attempt?** *Partly.* "As built" makes it unclear whether yes means rerunning this objective (tuned?) or building the count-paying design the same paragraph proposes. Neither option comes with a cost.
- **Which event rate should the label-free threshold target?** *No.* Real-recording event rates are given only at the "≤ 2 per 10 min" cap. At the 0.5 and 1 caps, Tony cannot compare against CoactDetect's 2.70 and LoCo's 3.72, and that comparison is the obvious way to choose. The page also leaves open whether the real choice is a rate, or whether to cap the recording's rate instead of the shifts'.
