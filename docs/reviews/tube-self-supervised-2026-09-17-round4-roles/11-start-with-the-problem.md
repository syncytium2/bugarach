# Argument-order review: "Can a detector learn coordinated events from rigid shift alone?"

**Artifact:** `<worktree>/docs/learned/tube_self_supervised/README.md` at f2278da. I also read `<worktree>/docs/goals/unsupervised-learning.md`, but only to see the goal. I opened nothing under reviews, handoffs or todo.

## Spine (one claim per section)

1. **The problem.** None of the lab recordings is annotated, so a detector that needs no labels must train against a copy of each recording that changes only the alignment between ROIs. Rigid shift is the candidate, and it can fail in two ways: it may leak other statistics, or it may also remove slow co-modulation. Alongside that sits a question about which architecture to use, with `line`'s supervised score as the ceiling.
2. **Terms.** Definitions. This section makes no claim.
3. **What this run found.** Rigid shift leaks nothing to a per-ROI classifier. The population average separates real recordings from their shifts by an amount that does not depend on *J*. No counting build separates from CoactDetect. Training against rigid shift loses to a detector with no parameters. The trained models respond to sub-second events, not slow modulation. On real recordings they do not pick out co-activity.
4. **Exploratory note.** Nothing here is promoted, and ⚠ marks a claim not to lean on.
5. **Figure 2, the leak tests.** The per-ROI test has power and does not see rigid shift. The population-average channels separate real from shifted at every *J*, which fits sub-second co-activity but cannot identify it, because the cells-mean trace does almost all of the work.
6. **Figure 3, the bake-off.** No learned model separates from CoactDetect, and one fold carries every margin. `line` beats `tube` on all four folds. The plant probe suggests that counting builds favour synchronous onsets, as a direction only.
7. **Figure 4, training without labels.** `count_excess` beats every trained condition. Training beats the untrained models at strict rates. The paired checks cannot tell events from slow modulation. Scored directly on synthetic twins, the trained models respond to events and only weakly to shared modulation.
8. **Figure 5, real recordings.** Supervised models and `count_excess` call co-activity that a rigid shift removes. Calls from the models trained against rigid shift hold no more co-activity than activity-weighted chance. The rate cap holds on the shifts, not on the recordings. More events fall near a recording's ends because of the detectors, not the recordings.
9. **What this does not settle.** A list of residual risks.
10. **What waits on Tony.** Four decisions: whether slow co-modulation counts as coordination, whether any learned build stays, whether the objective deserves another attempt, and which event rate the threshold should target.
11. **The published lineage.** Nearly every component is prior art. What is this project's own is narrow.
12. **References** and **Provenance.** Appendix material.

## Arc used

I used the default analysis arc: **the problem → what it costs → the method → what the method gets wrong → the fix → the evidence for the fix → the residual risk**, with the decisions at the end.

The page maps onto it: problem and cost in the problem section; method (the surrogate plus the architectures); what can go wrong (leak, slow co-modulation); the tests (Figures 2, 4 and 5); residual risks; decisions. It says so in its four questions, and it states its one real deviation: the architecture question runs "beside" the surrogate question and supplies the ceiling. So the overall order holds up. The findings below are about where individual pieces land.

## Cold open

The reader first sees the title question, then: "a coordinated event is several cells becoming active within a fraction of a second. None of the 84 … recordings … is annotated … A detector that never sees a real event cannot be told it was wrong about one." **That is the problem, and its cost follows immediately.** The first figure shows the method (the surrogates) rather than the problem. That is acceptable, because the problem here is missing labels, which has no picture, and Figure 1 still shows what a coordinated event looks like.
