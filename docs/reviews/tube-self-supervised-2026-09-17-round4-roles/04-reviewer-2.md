## The two problems that matter most

1. **The evidence that a rigid shift removes co-activity is a scoring bug** (finding 1). Events called on the shift are scored against the real recording's onsets. Scored against the shifted raster, the supervised models' shift events hold ≥ 3 ROIs in 0.43–0.54 of cases (`count_excess` 0.67), not 0.05–0.07. The shift is also in-sample for the threshold, so its low rate is guaranteed. The Figure 5 claim that these detectors "lose it on a rigid shift" has to be rewritten from a correct measurement.

2. **"The trained models see events at crop scale and do not localize them" is not tested, and the data argue against the "chance" half** (findings 2–6). On real recordings the trained models' events sit 0.5–3 s from co-activity, well above activity-weighted chance at 1–3 s. About half of them fall on silent gaps inside busy stretches, which the report never mentions. The ±0.2 s window reads them as chance. On the twins:
   - the events response is at ceiling on an easy twin;
   - the 1.6 s modulation cell cannot detect modulation;
   - the 20 s modulation cell also responds to pure coincidence counting;
   - about 120 correlated pairs with no intervals put the noise floor near ±0.1 (a null reads 0.383).
   
   The direct test is missing: threshold the checkpoints on a twin with known event times and score localization.
