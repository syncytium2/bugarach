# Murderboard run — the detector review for outside readers

**What was at stake.** Tony asked for one document an outside reviewer could read cold: every
coordinated-event detector, hand-written and learned, each with a figure of it deciding; what
surrogates are and why a circular shift beats a shuffle; the simulator; how detectors are graded
and tuned; the detectors on real TTX and senktide recordings; and each one's strengths and
weaknesses — jargon-free, at a sixth-grade reading level. A document like that gets quoted. Every
sentence in it had to be true of the code and the measurements, and every claim about another
lab's software had to be exact.

**What the review changed, beyond wording.** Three blind rounds, eleven roles each, turned up
three things that changed what the document says:

1. **The busy-block blind spot.** The bench never plants events inside its busy block, and busy-block
   calls are left out of precision, so a detector that goes blind there loses nothing (round 2, role 4).
   A new measurement plants events inside the block. Detectors that judge chance from nearby time
   lose most of them — CoactDetect finds 15% of mid-sized events inside against 95% outside — and
   the ratio tube models find none. The first draft's headline ("a local bar is the better design")
   became a trade-off.
2. **That trade-off was then too tidy.** Round 3 (roles 1, 3, 4) showed rate+context, a local-bar
   detector, holding both its recall and its false-alarm limit inside the block. The test was widened
   rather than the sentence softened: equal spacing inside and outside, 95% ranges, chance-corrected
   recall, and a second run at three times the block's intensity. There rate+context breaks its limit,
   SPIKE-synch calls about 14 times a minute by chance, and CoactDetect and LoCo go blind. The
   document now says every design's behavior moves with how busy the cells are (Figures 13 and 19).
3. **binned SCE was scored unfairly** (round 3, role 6). `sce_detect` reports a call from its bin edge
   but only as long as its events' spread, and the scorer reads that as the call's stretch. Re-scored
   over each call's own bin through the same rounds, tuned binned SCE goes from F1 0.45 to 0.70 on the
   quiet background. The detector's MATLAB contract is parity-locked, so the fix is filed for a decision:
   [`docs/todo/2026-09-15-binned-sce-calls-are-scored-over-the-wrong-stretch.md`](../todo/2026-09-15-binned-sce-calls-are-scored-over-the-wrong-stretch.md).

Other substantive corrections: locust uses the same event time as the other detectors on the lab's
files (a round-1 "fix" had said the opposite); the quiet-level "mismatch" came from a different
baseline set, and the fitter's own set matches the levels; the simulator's busiest cells bunch about
eight times too much, and its background runs about 15% below its nominal rate; a precision column
built from a coverage count was withdrawn; CICADA is cited at the version that contains the ported
code, and its repository has since declared itself superseded; SPIKE-synch's window cap is this
project's, not PySpike's paper's; the TTX paragraph no longer quotes pooled drug-effect numbers.

**How it generalizes.** Two lessons worth keeping. A benchmark that excludes a failure mode from its
score will rank detectors that have that failure mode well; a probe that only counts false alarms
needs a partner that counts misses. And a detector's output contract (onset, width) and the scorer's
reading of it can disagree silently for months when parity tests lock one side and nothing tests the
pair.

This review found and fixed many defects. **It is not a correctness proof.** The convergence table
below measures how quickly reviewers stopped finding things, not whether anything remains — and this
run did not converge.
