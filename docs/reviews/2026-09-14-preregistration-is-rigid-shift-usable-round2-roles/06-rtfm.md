**Review of the rigid-shift pre-registration (blob 2c9813d), blind pass, role 6 (methods expert)**

**Summary.** Seven problems rank from Medium to High. The worst is the saturation rule, which takes exactly the cases where the shift leaves coordination in place out of the destruction gate. Next is the can-pass check, which can overrule a candidate that has already passed. All numbers below come from synthetic data or event-model runs. I ran no rigid-shift surrogate on real data and no rigid-shift destruction score. Scratch work is in `<review-session-scratchpad>/r2role06/` (a scratch venv with elephant 1.2.1, and `shape.json`).

**Measured layout of the lab folder** (window layout and counts only, no surrogates):
- Each stream has 84 recordings and 44 mice, with a 0.1 s frame interval.
- Each stream has 1,669 windows, of which **1,501 are interior**. Every mouse has between 18 and 54 interior windows.
- The median recording has 31 ROIs (range 9 to 61) and 12,000 frames.
- **No ROI has two onsets in one frame, in either stream.**

| # | Location | Issue | Severity | Suggested fix | Verified? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Destruction gate, "Gated K: Not saturated" and "saturation table" | **The saturation rule removes exactly the cases where the shift fails to destroy coordination.** A probability of 0.95 or more that the largest bin still holds K planted participants *after the shift* is not a limit of the measure. It means the surrogate leaves 1 s coincidences in place, which is what this gate exists to catch. The event model (1-frame jitter, uniform whole-frame shift, random bin phase, 6 or 16 participants from 31 ROIs) shows the effect in three places. **(a)** Even with no shift (do-nothing), 6 participants at a 1.0 s bin give P(largest bin ≥ 4) = 0.958, so the planted event itself counts as "saturated". **(b)** Fast 1.6 s, participation 0.5: K = 3 and K = 4 are ungated (1.00 and 1.00). **(c)** Slow 1.4 s at the 2.0 s bin, participation 0.5: every K is at or above 0.95 (1.00, 1.00, 1.00, 0.996). No K is gated, so that cell is **VOID by construction**, and a VOID cell can never make a stream FAIL. The rule also never says which K set applies to the controls; do-nothing is "saturated" at every K up to its participant count. | High | Drop saturation as a reason to ungate. Gate every K where the planted events are visible, and treat residual co-binning as a failure. If a "cannot show removal" exclusion is kept, define it on the measure (for example, excess before the surrogate at its ceiling), never on what the shift leaves behind. Say which K set the controls use. | yes (event model; synthetic) |
| 2 | Leak gate, "Can-pass check" | **The can-pass check is one noisy draw, and it can overrule a PASS.** Two independent rigid-shift draws form an exchangeable pair, so their true accuracy is 0.5. As a null that is fine. But the rule reads that pair's *realised* 98.33rd percentile, which moves with that draw's chance accuracy. I simulated 150 datasets of 1,389 pairs over 44 mice, with 47 features and exactly 0.5 accuracy, using the specified refit bootstrap. **24 % of them had an upper bound at or above 0.55.** So the check calls a perfect surrogate UNDECIDED about a quarter of the time, and does so even when the candidate's own bound is already below 0.55. That is logically redundant: a passing bound already shows the gate could pass. Decided FAIL already needs a lower bound above 0.55, so the check adds nothing to FAIL either. There is also a small pessimistic bias. Two independent shifts are displaced up to 2*J* relative to each other, against up to *J* for real versus surrogate. That leaves fewer identical-feature ties than the candidate pairs have, on windows this sparse (mean 0.85 onsets per ROI per window). | High | Test the **width** of the interval, not a realised bound: 0.5 plus (98.33rd percentile minus the bootstrap median) must be below 0.55. Or use the median bound over several can-pass replicates. Never let the check overrule a candidate whose own upper bound is below 0.55. Give the can-pass draws cell ids distinct from the candidate's draw. | yes (synthetic) |
| 3 | Leak gate, the bounds and "The bootstrap refits" | **The refit mouse bootstrap is valid, but it is conservative and the gate has low power.** In the same simulation the refit interval never fell below the true expected accuracy at 0.5 accuracy, and did so 0.7 % of the time at 0.53 and 0.57 (nominal 1.67 %). The mouse bootstrap in `measure_recording_identity.py`, which resamples correctness without refitting, **under-covers**: 4.7–7.3 % against 1.67 %. So refitting is the right call. The refit half-width is about 0.044 at 0.5 accuracy, wider than the true 2.13 SD (about 0.036). Resampled datasets hold fewer distinct mice, which pulls the bootstrap mean down by 0.007–0.012 when a leak exists. The practical result: **P(upper bound < 0.55 \| perfect surrogate) ≈ 0.76**. Combined with the can-pass check in #2, about 0.58. Clustering of correctness within mice on real data would lower this further. Lower bounds are biased the same way, so a positive control needs a true accuracy of roughly 0.60 to count as valid reliably. **Cost is negligible:** one 5-fold refit takes 3.6 ms at 1,389 pairs and 47 features, so 2,000 resamples take about 7 s. `forced_choice` with 199 permutations takes 0.9 s. Also, the signed text names `mouse_bootstrap`, which does not refit; the amendment needs new code. | Medium | Accept the refit bootstrap. Price the gate's power on these data before the run (the check in #2 does this once it tests width) and report it. Put the refit in a named function, with a test that fails if it stops refitting (fold assignment must vary across resamples). | yes (synthetic; real clustering not measured) |
| 4 | Destruction gate, "Replication" and "Pass" | **Five twins are too few for a 98.33 % bound.** A percentile bootstrap over 5 top-level units under-covers. Simulated with twin-to-twin spread dominant, the upper bound fell below the truth **5–9 % of the time against 1.67 % nominal**. That is anti-conservative for PASS. The two-stage resample (twins, then draws) only compensates when draw noise is large. The rule also does not say how retained is combined across twins (mean of per-twin ratios, or ratio of pooled means). Near the visibility floor the ratio's denominator is small, so the choice matters. More twins are cheap: one excess score takes about 0.05 s at 31 ROIs and 12,000 frames, 0.08 s at the 2.0 s bin. | Medium | Use 20 or more twins (about 3 minutes per cell), or a *t*-based bound with 4 degrees of freedom. Resample twins only, and declare how retained is combined. | yes (synthetic) |
| 5 | Destruction gate, `freeze_half` control and the 0.25 threshold | **`freeze_half` works, but retained is a threshold function, not a graded share.** `freeze_half` exists as a generic flag on `surrogate_stats.destruction` (`freeze_half=True` composes with `"homogeneous_resample"` by name). I scored the controls on synthetic twins (5 twins × 40 draws × 200 assessor surrogates, 1.0 s bin). At K = 4 the graded control reads **0.21–0.29 at participation 0.2 and 0.45–0.47 at 0.5**: inside the 0.10–0.90 band and at least 0.10 from homogeneous resample (−0.004 to 0.017). Do-nothing with a different after-side seed reads 0.97–1.01. **But** at K = 6 and participation 0.2, the same control reads **0.01–0.06**. A surrogate that leaves half the ROIs perfectly coordinated would pass the "at most 0.25" rule at that K, and sits on the threshold at K = 4. Only the "every K, both participation levels" requirement protects the gate. The control is validated at K = 4 but the gate applies at every gated K. | Medium | Require the graded control to read at least 0.10 at **each** gated K and participation level, and ungate any K where it does not. At minimum, report the graded control beside every gated K. | yes (synthetic) |
| 6 | Amendments, "Randomness" | **Several seeds are not covered by the salting list.** `surrogate_stats.py:1234` chooses the freeze-half ROIs with `rng_of(("freeze-half", p, k))`: no run tag, stream, cell or twin index, and not in the amendment's list. `coact_excess` has the seed `20260722` built in, and `destruction()` has no seed argument to pass through. The existing salt routes do work: `draw_surrogates` uses `(rec, stream, cell_id, k)` and `destruction` uses `(f"twin-{p}", stream, cell_id, k)`, both salted through `cell_id`. | Medium | Add seed parameters to `destruction`/`coact_excess` and to the freeze-half choice, and add a twin index. Simulated at exactly 0.5 accuracy, with 11 mice per group and pooled SD 0.017, **P(any of the four groups ≥ 0.55) = 0.149**. | Medium | Use the group's lower bound above 0.55, Bonferroni-corrected over the four groups, or report the group numbers without letting them decide. | yes (synthetic) |
| 8 | Destruction gate, "Visible" | **The visibility rule needs its units and scope stated.** Coactivity excess is in **co-active ROI·events per minute**. The synthetic twins plant one event per minute (`n_events = max(5, T//60)`). So 1.0 is about one sixth of a planted event at participation 0.2 (6 participants, before ≈ 4–5.7) and about one fifteenth at 0.5 (before ≈ 11–15). The rule does not say whether "excess before" means the planted twin's excess or the planted-minus-unplanted difference (the code's `before`). It also does not say whether it must hold in each twin or on average; K = 8 at participation 0.2 read 0.38 in one twin and 0 in the others. | Low | Name the difference (`before`), say it must hold in every twin, and state the units. | yes |
| 9 | Destruction gate, do-nothing control | **The seeds for the candidate's before and after sides are not stated.** The code shares one assessor seed across before/after and planted/unplanted, which removes Monte Carlo noise from the difference. Do-nothing is to use a different seed on its after side. If the candidate keeps the shared seed, the do-nothing control checks a different pipeline from the one the candidate runs. | Low | Declare one seed scheme for the candidate and the controls. | yes (code read) |
| 10 | Amendments, "The count gate" | **The count-gate arithmetic is correct; four smaller points remain.** A two-sided 96.67 % interval inside ±2 % is equivalent to two one-sided tests at 0.05/3. `edge_thinning` at 5 s on synthetic interior windows gave an occupied-frame change of **−4.36 %** (SD 0.9 %), close to the expected −*J*/(2 × 60 s) = −4.17 %, so it will fail the gate as a valid control should. The four points: **(a)** On interior windows, rigid shift's count change cancels window to window: only onsets crossing the two outer edges of the interior block count. The gate is close to passing by construction and is weak evidence. **(b)** "Occupied frames" equals onset count on this folder (measured), so the change does nothing here. **(c)** The statistic is averaged within mouse (each mouse weighted equally), but the margin is a pooled mean per ROI per window. **(d)** "Fall outside ±2 %" does not say whether the point estimate or the whole interval must be outside. | Low | Use one weighting for statistic and margin. | 12 | Choice of rigid shift, literature regime | **Declared displacements are well outside the regime where the literature tested this surrogate.** Louis et al. 2010 recommend spike-train dithering (their name for rigid shift) at a ±20 ms dither against 1 ms coincidence resolution and ±1 ms injected jitter, a ratio of about 20. **Checked and found sound:**
- **Negative-control voiding rule:** P(4 or more of 20 flags \| 0.05) = 0.0159 and P(3 or more) = 0.0755. With 199 permutations the actual test size is at most 0.045, which gives 1.1 %. Orientation is redrawn for each seed, so flags are close to independent given the data.
- **Multiplicity:** the 98.33 % Bonferroni for "any of three *J* passes" is right. The requirements to pass at both bins, every K and both participation levels need no further correction, because all of them must pass.
- **Elephant 1.2.1 `dither_spike_train`:** it draws one offset per call whatever the spike count, and with `edges=True` it drops onsets outside the window. Twins sharing a key therefore get identical per-ROI offsets, as the method needs. The frame mapping keeps the shift rigid in whole frames.
