# Role 8, "You Lost Me": naive-reader accessibility. Chapter 1 (Aim 0), blind verify round 2

**Artifact:** `docs/dissertation/chapter-1-aim0.md`. I read it in full and opened all eleven figures with Read.
**Reader:** a neuroscientist on the committee who works outside point-process statistics and has never seen this repository.
**What I checked:** for each numbered section and figure, the terms it introduces and whether they are defined there; whether a cold reader can follow it; whether any sentence a reader follows easily leads to a false conclusion; whether each figure looks like a familiar chart type whose axes mean something different here; whether each panel is readable, with one sentence on what a cold reader sees; whether mechanisms are illustrated; and tone. Items owned by other roles (number accuracy, citations, mechanical figure rules) are left out except where they actively mislead a reader. Those are marked with their boundary.

## Overall verdict

Section by section the chapter is much easier to follow than a typical theory chapter. Figure 1.2, a real raster plus what each tool counts, is an excellent cold-reader anchor. No figure uses a familiar chart type with different axis meanings: Figure 1.2 A is a true raster, with time on the horizontal axis and cells as rows.

I would not sign off yet, for two reasons:

1. **Order.** The chapter opens (section 1.1, Figure 1.1) with results stated in terms of three tools, a "joint fit" and "coordinated share", none of which is defined until 1.2.2 or 1.4.6.
2. **What readers will conclude.** Several true sentences lead a reader to the wrong place:
   - "the tools, not the data, limit how close two events can be"
   - "the committee review asked for"
   - "reviewed by eleven Claude review agents"
   - the 1–5-event power analysis, which the chapter never motivates
   - two figures that disagree on the safe threshold at the realistic background

## What a cold reader sees in each figure (one sentence per panel)

- **1.1:** A–C and E–G show ratio-to-planted lines lying flat near 1 on log axes, except the 1 s window falling at high jitter, and two assessor lines floating near 10× in F and near 0.5× in E. D and H show recovered against planted jitter with an identity line; the yellow and pink lines go flat in H. Readable once the tools are known, not before (F1).
- **1.2:** A is a raster of 33 cells over one minute with two vertical stacks of ticks. B and C each zoom in on one event: ticks, a per-frame histogram under a Gaussian, and window bars split 4/6 or whole 10. D–F show stacked bar counts per window or bin with a dashed threshold. Readable. Panel letters run A, B, D, C, E, F in reading order (mechanical, agent 10).
- **1.3:** A shows two S-curves of window-capture probability against w/σ, with dashed uniform-jitter twins almost on top of them. B shows three falling curves of k̂/k with dots and yellow ticks. Readable.
- **1.4:** A–C and E–G are predicted-versus-measured scatters on the identity line, with gray clouds offset. D and H plot chance clusters on log–log axes. Readable. "95 % interval about the marker size" is an odd way to say the interval is smaller than the marker.
- **1.5:** Three falling lines of chance clusters against K, with a dashed one-per-hour line and shaded unmeasured K. Readable, a good figure.
- **1.6:** A is a variance ratio rising with reuse, on a log axis. B is percent flagged, rising to 100 % and then collapsing at the rightmost point. Readable, but nothing tells the reader what reuse below 1 means (F15).
- **1.7:** A is a rising arctan-shaped curve. B and C are straight falling lines on log–log axes. Readable.
- **1.8:** Power curves rising with events (1, 2, 3, 5), solid and dashed by template count. Readable. σ = 0.2 s appears only inside the figure, not in the caption.
- **1.9:** A–C show resolvable separation falling gently with cells per event, an oracle line lower, horizontal reference lines, and downward triangles on the floor. D shows S-shaped power curves. I can say what it looks like but **not why a cell-identity test "resolves at every separation"**. A cold reader cannot explain the triangles (F6).
- **1.10:** Step-shaped probability curves near 2–3 s (assessor) and 4–6 s (count detector), with plateaus below 1. E–H look nearly identical to A–D, and nothing says what they add (F16).
- **1.11:** A grid of filled and open circles over jitter × cells, with two shaded boxes between columns. Readable, apart from "bench level" (F11).
