# Argument-order review: the slow co-modulation page

**Artifact:** `docs/learned/slow_comodulation/README.md`. I also opened `fig1_two_kinds.png`, `fig3_what_the_surrogates_remove.png`, `docs/goals/unsupervised-learning.md` (line 58) and the rigid-shift report's open questions (`docs/learned/tube_self_supervised/README.md`, lines 395–398).

## The spine (one claim per section)

1. **"Why this page exists" (the opening box).** A model trained against rigid shift is rewarded for whatever the shift destroys. Shared rate change posing as synchrony is an old confound (1967, 1999). The label-free thread has an open question about shared modulation over tens of seconds, so this page measures shared change in the recordings.
2. **"The short answer".** Every dataset has both sub-second events and shared change over a minute or more. On the fast stream that change is concentrated in some recordings. On the slow stream most shared activity is events. The Dard et al. data are large everywhere. The benchmark generator has more drift than the lab. Rigid shift leaves the minute-scale change in place, so the models were paid for events and faster structure.
3. **"Two ways ROIs are active together".** Opens with a description of the data. Then: shared activity comes as coordinated events or as shared modulation, and here is what rigid shift, circular shift, the block control and CoactDetect are. Figure 1 shows the two kinds in synthetic worlds.
4. **"Two measurements".** The excess-coincidence correlogram says *at what lag* shared activity sits. The count-variance ratio says *how much* there is at a bin width. Both are measured against the window's own average.
5. **"What the surrogates remove".** Where the answer is known (Figures 2 and 3): rigid shift smears events but removes modulation only faster than about *J*. Removing episodes spares drift. The block control keeps 2-minute change. The generator's promiscuity probe is itself drift.
6. **"What the recordings hold".** Applied to real data (Figures 4 and 5):
   - Fast stream: a real but concentrated minute-scale excess, about a third of it a straight-line trend.
   - Slow stream: mostly events.
   - Dard et al.: large, but mostly because it images more ROIs.
7. **"By group".** Also holds an unrelated aside. The pooled excess is uneven across groups, with DI highest on fast and MALE on slow. Group is confounded with imaging day. The aside gives three readings of the slow-stream dip.
8. **"What this changes for the label-free thread".** The minute-scale change was on both sides of the training contrast. The contrast paid for events and structure faster than about 40 s. The 10–45 s band is not separated.
9. **"The decision this sets up".** Is change over a minute or more coordination, background, or the producer's to explain? Nothing here says where it comes from.
10. **"What this does not settle".** The residual caveats.
11. **"Published lineage" and "Reproduce".** Reference material; appendix.

## Arc used and verdict

**Arc:** the default for an analysis deliverable, adapted to an explainer. The problem, then what it costs the thread, then the measurement, then what the measurement gets wrong, then the measurement where the answer is known, then the evidence in the recordings, then what follows for the thread, the decision, and the residual risk.

The page states its own order at line 38, so putting the summary first and the consequences late is a declared deviation, not a defect. The middle of the page is sound. Showing what each surrogate removes on synthetic worlds (Figure 3) before the recordings is the right order: the reader can judge Figures 4 and 5 because Figure 3 came first.

**Cold open:** the reader first sees a definitional title. Then a box made of a mechanism sentence, two historical citations and a note about which branch the report is being revised on. Then status flags. Then six summary bullets whose numbers depend on terms defined two sections later. The first picture is Figure 1, a set of synthetic definitions in which panels A–C (share of ROIs lit per 10 s in the three synthetic worlds) look much alike.

**Is the first thing the reader sees the problem?** Partly. The worry is named, but only abstractly. The picture that shows the problem is rigid shift following drift while circular shift does not. The real version of it (`one_recording.png`) is rightly kept to the darkroom. No synthetic stand-in appears until Figure 3, the third figure.

**Main defect: the page does not come back to its own question.** The opening question, following the rigid-shift report, is about modulation over *tens of seconds*, which that report puts at 10–45 s. The closing decision is about change over *a minute or more*. That is the band rigid shift leaves alone, so it is not the band the models could have been paid for. The asked-about band is reduced to one sentence in the label-free section ("not separated here"), and the change of question is never announced.

**Verdict:** revise the order. The middle holds. The opening and the closing are about different questions. One aside sits in a section it has nothing to do with. Several definitions arrive after the claims that use them.
