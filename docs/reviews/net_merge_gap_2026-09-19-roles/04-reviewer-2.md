## Blocking

**B1 · lede, §3, Table 1, Figures 1–2 · the set-aside treatment is never applied to the as-run baseline, so the headline lead is confounded with the exclusion rule.**
The page compares "gap tuned, failed refits set aside" against an as-run baseline that still counts the failures. Apply the same counting rule to both sides and the tuning nearly vanishes: replicate `chorus_norm` is **+0.0052 (4 of 4 folds) as run at 2 s** with the failures set aside, rising to +0.0150 tuned — an increment of **+0.0098, inside the page's own 0.010 band**. Replicate `chorus_gain_norm`: as-run+aside **+0.0028** → tuned+aside +0.0133, increment +0.0105. So under the counting rule the lede leads with, *the net was already ahead before the gap was touched*, and the page's central question ("does tuning the gap move the verdict?") is answered by differencing two different counting rules.
*Fix:* add the "as run, refits set aside" row to Table 1 and to Figures 1 and 2, and state the tuning increment **within** each counting rule. Verify: **yes** (recomputed from both draw JSONs).

**B2 · §7 Limits bullet 2, §5 · "CoactDetect's choices pass that check after the fact, so for CoactDetect the difference is none" is a check with no power, and it passed.**
`crowded_check.json` `reference_rule` = "the setting a choice replaces: meta.json `coded_base`", and coact's reference params are `{... "merge_gap_sec": 8.0 ...}` with `crowded_mean_f1` 0.8259. The baseline against which coact's 8 s is tested **already contains the 8 s merge**. The nets' baseline is their as-run 2 s, so their check measures the cost of widening; coact's measures nothing. Same tolerance, different reference — not the same check. Compounding it: `meta.json`'s coact grid is `merge_gap_sec [0.0, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 5.0, 8.0]`, so 8 s is the grid edge and there was nothing wider for a check to refuse, while the nets' grid runs to 30 s and the check refused at least one wider preferred setting in **64 of 64** choices.
*Fix:* delete "for CoactDetect the difference is none". State that the coded reference already carries the 8 s gap, that coact's grid ends where the nets' refusals begin, and that the check is therefore **untested**, not passed, on the coded side. Verify: **yes**.

**B3 · lede ("Under the budget the answer holds"), §4, Table 1 · the strongest bolded claim is asserted over a cell the page's own criterion calls "not a result", and the quoted shortfall range is treatment-dependent.**
With the report's own set-aside rule, the replicate's `chorus_norm` under the budget is **−0.009** — inside the ±0.010 band that Figure 1's caption says "is not a result". Table 1 prints it unmarked and §4 says "Every net still trails on average ... whether or not the failed trainings are set aside". Separately, "closes 10% to 45% of the shortfall" is computed only in the all-refits treatment; in the set-aside treatment the same cell closes **51%** and the range becomes **39–51%**, so the "10%" low end is an artifact of two collapsed refits, not of the gap.
*Fix:* mark within-noise cells in Table 1 (`_paired` already computes `within_noise` — it is in the JSON and unused by the page); give the shortfall range in both treatments; soften to "no net's mean turns positive; one is inside the noise band". Verify: **yes**.

## Major

**M6 · lede · "the lead clears that scale" holds in one draw of two and the sentence does not say so.** The four set-aside cells on F1 alone are: this run's draw `chorus_norm` **+0.004** (inside the band) and `chorus_gain_norm` **−0.007** (negative, inside the band); replicate +0.015 and +0.013. **M7 · §3 · the set-aside's leverage is never stated, and "4 of 4 folds" is not four-fold corroboration.** Replicate `chorus_norm`: two refits at 0.125 F1, both in fold 1, move that fold from **−0.2391 to +0.0139** and the four-fold mean from −0.048 to +0.015; the other three folds are byte-identical before and after. **M8 · §3, §6, Figure 1 caption · the 0.010 noise scale omits the larger noise source, so "past the noise scale" is not the test the page thinks it is.** 0.010 is the median change from swapping *recordings*. The refit-to-refit noise inside a single fold is bigger: with the failures already removed, the standard error of a fold's five-refit mean has **median 0.0114 and maximum 0.0233, exceeding 0.010 in 18 of 32 chorus-net folds**. Propagating that alone puts roughly ±0.011 (2 SE) on the replicate's +0.015. The parent report is blunter still: "the scorer's own documentation says to read the order in which it ranks detectors, never the decimal places of one score", and margins of 0.007–0.010 F1 "say little".
*Fix:* name both noise sources and quote a combined interval, or stop using a single scalar band as a verdict rule; carry the report's decimal-places warning forward. Verify: **yes**.

**M9 · §2 grid bullet, §5, Figure 3 and its caption · the chosen gap is a censored boundary, not an optimum, and "moves each chorus net toward CoactDetect" invites a convergence reading the data do not support.** In **64 of 64** choices a wider gap scored better on the inner fits and was refused. In the tool's own `heldout_f1_by_gap` — computed and never shown — held-out F1 is maximal at **30 s, the top of the grid, in all 32 folds of both draws**, leaving 0.015 to 0.092 F1 (mean ≈0.05, five noise units, larger than the whole net-vs-coact gap being argued about) on the table at the chosen gap. CoactDetect's 8 s is likewise *its* grid edge. Figure 3 renders this perfectly — a wall of refusal crosses at 15 s and 30 s immediately right of every chosen dot — and the caption describes the mechanics without drawing the inference. The two sides did not converge; they stopped at two different ceilings, one imposed by an unsigned tolerance and one by a grid.
*Fix:* say the choice is boundary-limited in every fold; say coact's 8 s is a grid edge, not a preference; either drop the convergence language or extend coact's gap grid to 15 and 30 s and re-run it. Verify: **yes**.

**M10 · §5 · the bench cannot penalize merging at all, the crowded set is the only counterweight, and the page reports its extremes instead of its distribution.** Planted events are ≥120 s apart (`hand_search.min_sep_sec` 120.0), so on this bench a wider gap can only delete duplicate calls — the harm the gap causes lives entirely in the crowded set. *Fix:* show the distribution of the held-out crowded loss across all 64 choices; state that the bench rewards merging monotonically and that the crowded set is the only thing bounding it. Verify: **yes**.
