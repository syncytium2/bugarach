# Murderboard run — `site/index.html`, reorganized to lead with the model

## The problem this review was pointed at

The front page argued for six ported MATLAB detectors and reached the deep-learning work
in its fourth section. Tony, 2026-08-31: *"i want to put the deep learning up front … this
is fundamentally a portfolio project to show off the deep learning approach to data with no
ground truth."* The reorganization moves the model to the top, puts a measured view of it
above the problem figure, and reduces the hand-written detectors to a short section with
links out.

Reordering a page does not change what it claims. It changes **which claims a stranger
reads first**, and it puts sentences that were safe in one context at the top of a public
portfolio artifact. That is what this review was for.

**It found a wrong attribution to another laboratory, on the live page's own subject.** The
page credited the coactivity-vs-shuffle rule to Cossart, Aronov & Yuste (2003). It is not
theirs: that paper's own reference 12 credits the interval-reshuffling procedure to **Mao,
Hamzei-Sichani, Aronov, Froemke & Yuste (2001)**, *Neuron* 32:883–898, and Cossart 2003
applies it to UP states. This is the same class of defect that caused the 2026-08-28 deploy
hold to be lifted early — a public page making an unverified claim about another lab beside
this project's own numbers.

It also found that **the page's novelty framing was broader than the evidence**, in two
directions at once, and both are now stated on the page rather than left for a reader to
discover.

## What was found and fixed

**Blocking**

1. **Wrong root for the coactivity-vs-shuffle rule** (role 2). Verified against the primary
   text, not inferred. Fixed: Mao et al. 2001 named as the root, Cossart 2003 as the
   application. ⚠ The chain is verified **to one step short** — Mao 2001 is paywalled and
   could not be opened, and the surrogate-test literature attributes the shuffle test for
   excess synchrony to Abeles & Gerstein (1988) thirteen years earlier. Do not upgrade the
   wording past Mao without that PDF.
2. **"None of them works on calcium imaging"** (role 2), placed beside a detector built on
   Kreuz's SPIKE-synchronization — whose own authors apply it to wide-field calcium imaging
   (Kreuz et al. 2022, *J Neurosci Methods* 381:109703; Cecchini et al. 2021, *PLoS Comput
   Biol* 17:e1008963). Fixed: the claim is now scoped to the four named learned methods, and
   a note concedes the occupied ground explicitly.
3. **The hero figure's alt text said "Six detector lanes … six analysis traces"** while its
   own caption said five and the body said "five in this build" (role 3). Pre-existing; a
   screen-reader user got the wrong count. Fixed, along with a third instance — "All six
   work by finding moments that stand out" — found by the spine pass.

**Major**

4. **Planted-ground-truth training is not new** (role 2). SpindleNet (Kulkarni et al. 2019)
   trained partly on synthetic spindles with known onsets *for the reason this page argues
   in its opening sentence*, that experts disagree; DeepWonder (Zhang et al. 2023) trains on
   synthetic calcium recordings outright. Fixed: both named on the page, and the surviving
   claim narrowed to what the search actually supports — no **learned** detector emitting
   population coordinated events with times on calcium imaging — flagged as a search that
   came up empty rather than a proof.
5. **"matched to its MATLAB original to 1e-9 … so it can be cited in its place"** (role 2)
   read as *our port is the published method*. The project's own `detector_history.md`
   denies it. Fixed: matched to **the implementation it was ported from**, with the
   distinction stated.
6. **SPIKE-synch was named with no attribution** on a page that says "cite them, not this
   repo" about another method (role 2). Fixed: Kreuz, Mulansky & Bozanic (2015) cited, plus
   a pointer to the deep page's full citation table for the remaining methods.
7. **The lead figure was smaller than the figure below it** (roles 9, 10). The first draft
   held the model to the 46rem text column, rendering the page's subject at 738px above a
   hero at 1205px. The reasoning was also arithmetically wrong — the height being avoided was
   1,399px, not the ~2,600 estimated from the rem value. Fixed by measuring the render
   instead of the stylesheet.
8. **The method statement appeared twice, near-verbatim** (role 5) — moving it to the
   opening left the original in place. Fixed; the downpage paragraph keeps only its unique
   half.
9. **Panel C was explained twice**, once in the caption and once beneath it (role 5). Fixed.

**Minor / flagged, not fixed**

- SEED detects spindles **and K-complexes**; corrected on the page. It is still the only one
  of the four given no link.
- A CSS comment explains a selector fix in terms of a CICADA link that no longer exists in
  the caption it describes. Left; reconcile before the next edit there.
- The build now carries a second copy-and-refuse asset block shaped like `reality.png`'s.
  Two is tolerable; a third should become a helper.

## Residual ⚠ — what a person still has to resolve

- **Mao 2001 is unread** (paywalled). The root is verified from Cossart 2003's reference
  list, not from Mao itself, and Abeles & Gerstein (1988) may sit behind it.
- **The novelty claim is not cleared, only unfalsified.** Unsearched: the cell-assembly
  detection literature (Grün; Lopes-dos-Santos; Russo & Durstewitz; Mölter) — the most
  plausible home for population-event prior art; radar/CFAR, which is rate+context's own
  stated root; astrocyte and cardiac calcium-event detection; MEA burst detection; fMRI
  co-activation patterns. And the one worth doing first: **injection-recovery outside
  neuroscience** — gravitational-wave astronomy, seismology, high-energy physics — where
  planting synthetic signals in real background because nothing can be labelled is the
  standard methodology, not a novel move. The page now says this itself.
- **No correspondence with the Cossart lab is recorded anywhere in the tree**, and that is
  the cheapest remaining check on the locust/CICADA chain.
- **Two citation errors in the internal record**, found in passing and *not* fixed here
  because they are outside this artifact:
  `docs/todo/2026-08-24-kreuz-answered-the-spike-synch-questions-in-april.md` cites Cecchini
  et al. as **2022** (it is 2021, 17(5):e1008963) and Kreuz et al. as **2024** (it is 2022,
  381:109703, which `detector_history.md` has right).
- **Every F1 on this page predates two unmerged corrections** — the difficulty-axis
  correction (RESET §5) and `bench-background-is-not-flat` (DEPLOY_HOLD). This is why the
  page quotes **no** numeric scores and argues the comparison qualitatively instead.
- **The page is still marked Draft**, and the deploy is governed separately by
  `docs/DEPLOY_HOLD.md`. Nothing here deploys it.

**Stopping reason: severity floor reached** — two consecutive rounds with no blocking or
major findings. Not a round cap.

**This review found and fixed 9 defects. It is not a correctness proof.** The table above
measures how quickly reviewers stopped finding things, not whether anything remains — and
three of the nine were found only because one role was given the blindness to search
independently. A convergence table cannot distinguish a page with nothing left to find from
one whose reviewers were all looking in the same wrong place.

## A note on how this run was staffed

Role 2 ran as a **separate agent**, per the process's rule that attribution is the one role
the size scaling may not collapse — *"a single-pass self-review inherits the drafter's search
history, so it stops in the same place for the same reason."* That rule earned its place
here: **the two blocking findings and three of the six majors came from role 2, and the
self-reviewed roles found none of them.** The other ten roles ran as a single-pass
self-review walking each checklist in turn, because this session was instructed not to spawn
subagents unless asked, and role 2 was the exception the process names in terms.
