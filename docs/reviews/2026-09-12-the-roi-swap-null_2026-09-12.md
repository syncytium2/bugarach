# Murderboard run — the ROI-swap null

## What was at stake

Tony proposed building the null for a label-free coordination detector differently: not by displacing
onsets inside a recording, which is how the first detector died, but by **swapping whole ROIs across
recordings**. Every train in such a chimera is a real train, so it cannot manufacture an interval that
real data never produces — the exact failure that killed the dither family. The idea was sound enough
to design properly, and it was written up as a staged experiment with a stop-or-go at every stage.

This review was asked to decide whether that experiment should run.

## What it found

**It should not run as designed, and the review did not converge.** Three rounds, and they failed in
an instructive order.

**Round 1 — eleven roles on revision 1 — found the same defect that withdrew both earlier reevaluations
in this thread, for the third time.** The draft said the negative control was failing on the fast stream
and quoted accuracy 0.5386 at P = 0.035. That was **seed 0** of a control that holds twenty seeds in the
same run folder, at `flag_rate` 0.05 and mean accuracy 0.4948 — a control passing at its nominal rate.
Four roles found it independently and by four routes: the production JSON, the
`negative_control_pairs` docstring on the screen branch, a build-agent review this same session had
landed an hour earlier, and an arithmetic check that 830 pairs is half of 1669. The draft cited the
earlier withdrawals while repeating their cause.

**That finding outlives the proposal.** The screen voided **126 of 248 fast candidates — every fast
candidate that returned a number — on that one seed**, while the twenty-seed rate sat beside it. It is
filed on its own
([todo](../todo/2026-09-12-126-fast-candidates-were-voided-on-one-seed-of-twenty.md)), because it is a
defect in how the screen applied its control and it stands regardless of this proposal.

**Round 2 — a blind pass on revision 2 — found errors of fact in a plan that still looked sound**, and
one of them was a false claim this session made about its own work: revision 2 said four papers had
been added to `lit_needed.md` when they had not. They are added now, so the claim is true rather than
withdrawn. The same pass found that group identity is **perfectly nested in imaging day** on this
cohort — 42 dates, none carrying more than one group — which this session verified before acting on.

**Round 3 — two blind passes on revision 3 — stopped finding slips and found the plan cannot be
built.** The swap requires each swapped ROI from a different donor recording, donors confined to the
target's mouse fold. On this cohort, median 31.5 ROIs and most mice contributing two recordings, the
reviewer's simulation leaves **3.7 of 84 targets buildable at a full chimera** and **none** for the
within-group arm. And the two stages the proposal offered for free approval **cannot fail**: the
dispersion stage cannot reach its STOP, and the construction-validity stage's own predicted outcome
had no consequence attached. Approving them would have produced a record of passed gates that
validated nothing.

Round 3 also caught **two integrity errors revision 3 introduced**, which is the reason a blind pass
is not optional. Words were attributed to Tony in quotation marks that were a handoff author's
instruction, not his. And a month count — "9 of 22 months" — that this session had presented as
independently verified came from its own bug: an eight-character `YYYYMMDD` date sliced at seven
characters, splitting each month by the tens digit of the day. The true count is 10 of 14.

## What would validate the idea, and how it generalises

The support-violation argument survives all three rounds: a null assembled from real trains cannot
commit the failure that killed the dither family. What never got tested is whether that buys anything
over the **within-recording** nulls, which keep slice identity. `rigid_shift` on fast already measures
per-ROI discriminator accuracy **0.495-0.524 across all six displacements** — re-checked against the
run files. The proposal argued against it without checking those numbers.

So the next artifact is not a fourth revision. It is **one leak-versus-destruction table** placing
`rigid_shift`, `circular_shift`, `trial_shift` and the ROI swap side by side on one cohort, preceded by
a **donor-feasibility table** and carrying **paired** gates with declared margins. Only if the swap
beats the within-recording nulls on that table does it have a case.

The lesson that generalises beyond this project: **a stop-or-go gate has to be able to stop.** Two of
this plan's gates could not, and they read as rigour. A pre-registered gate that cannot fail is worse
than no gate, because it manufactures evidence of scrutiny.

## What a clean run would not have warranted — and this run was not clean

This review found and corrected a large number of defects across three rounds. It is not a
correctness proof, and because it **did not converge**, it is not a recommendation either. The
convergence record below measures how reviewers kept finding things, not whether anything remains —
and round 3 found more consequential defects than round 2, which is the opposite of convergence.

### Adjudication

**Applied in revision 2:** every round-1 finding that concerned fact, attribution, units, citation,
link, encoding or argument order; the group section rewritten around the treatment-versus-baseline
distinction; the intercept/slope decomposition withdrawn.

**Applied in revision 3:** every round-2 finding; the four `lit_needed.md` asks added so revision 2's
claim became true; the group section rebuilt around the verified day nesting.

**Applied after round 3, and why the rest were not.** The two integrity errors — the misattributed
quotation and the wrong month count — were corrected, because an error about a real person's words or
about a number presented as verified must not stand even in a document that is not recommended. The
design-level findings were answered by marking the proposal **NOT RECOMMENDED** and adding a verdict
section, **not** by a fourth revision: patching a plan whose grid cannot be built would polish something
that should be replaced. Round 3's remaining fact findings are listed, unapplied, in the proposal's
revision note, so a successor inherits them.
