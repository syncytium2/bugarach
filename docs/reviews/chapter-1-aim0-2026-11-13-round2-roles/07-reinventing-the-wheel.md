# Role 7: Reuse audit of docs/dissertation/figures/ch1_intro_figures.py (chapter-1-aim0.md, blob 0acfccbb)

I compared every piece the script writes out against the repository's code, and checked the important ones numerically over the script's own 200 draws (seeds 1000–1199).

## Where it already reuses or matches the repository (checked, no finding)
- **Fixed-bin and sliding detectors.** They call `count_detect` and `count_sliding_detect` directly.
- **`chance_calls_per_hour` / `chance_floor`.** These match `blind_a/theory_floor.py` `u` and `floor_analytic` (with the docstring's max(3, ·)) at all 403 background rates tried (0.001–0.2 Hz, w = 2 s). Restating it is justified, because that file runs its Monte Carlo loop when imported.
- **`sliding_count`.** At every frame it equals the exact step function `detectors/sliding.py:pieces`, with 0 of 200 draws differing.
- **Scoring (`score`).** Against `score.score_detections(tol_sec=1.0, floor=None)` with a ground-truth shim: in 0 of 600 (draw, detector) pairs does a situation outcome or a false-alarm count differ, and Table 1.1's shares are reproduced exactly. The two scorers do credit different members of the merged pair 3a/3b (81 s vs 85 s) in 173 of 600, but "3 both found" is unaffected. The docstring's claim holds.
- **`plant` rounding.** `floor(x/DT + 0.5)` is the same as `_shared.matlab_round` (`detectors/_shared.py:35`) on the tie cases tested. Writing the model out is justified: `simulate_coordination` cannot take chosen event times, and it keeps events out of `hot_window` (simulate.py:943), so it cannot place event 4 in the busier stretch.
- **Correlogram.** It uses `mjc.pairs` and `mjc.hwhm` directly. The width conversion 2·sqrt(ln 2)·σ is the same constant as `GAUSS` in `ch1_followups/correlogram_vs_joint.py:47`.
- **Excess pair counts and prediction.** They use `mcr.excess_cumulants`, `theory.predicted_excess_cumulants` and `theory.g` directly.
- **`bin_count_survival`.** Its per-bin counts equal `count_detect(bin_sec=1).obs` exactly.
- **The 1 s chance line in Figure 1.4 E.** It uses the same binomial tail (p = 1 − e^(−b·bin)) as `theory.chance_clusters_per_min` (theory.py:250).
