## What a cold reader sees, per figure (with the caption covered)

- **Figure 1.** Two dot-strip panels, each with three rows (lr 0.003 / 0.01 / 0.03). In panel A the dots of the top two rows sit against 0, and the bottom row's dots spread from about 0.4 to 0.9 of the way across. At 390 px panel B is off-screen behind a scroll hint. False-friend check: it looks like a strip chart and it is one. Rows are categories and the x-axis is a share, which is the idiom's usual meaning. **Pass.**
- **Figure 2.** Two scatter panels with a log y-axis. Orange dots form one column pinned at x = 0, low down (10⁻³ to 10⁻¹). Blue dots form jittered columns at eighths from 0.125 to 1, around 10⁰–10¹. There is one lone blue dot at x = 0 up high and one lone orange dot far right and low. False-friend check: it is a strip/swarm scatter and the axes mean what that idiom implies. **Pass.** The jitter on a continuous x-axis for a variable that only takes eighths is a small misreading risk (F11).
- **Figure 3.** Six loss curves over about 1,500 training steps. The solid orange "as run" line and a dashed dark-green line hover near 1.5 throughout. Blue, amber and pink-dashed drop to about 0.1–0.3 by roughly step 150–300. Green (lr 0.01) drops only after about step 400. It reads as standard loss curves. **Pass.**
- **Figure 4.** Two step lines counting dead head layers (0–8) against training step. Orange (collapsed) steps up to 1 at about step 30, to 2 by about step 110, and later to 3. Blue (working) is **not** flat: it sits at 1 dead layer at step 10, before orange does, and again at steps 70–90 and 120–130. A stranger sees the working fit lose a layer first. The standard line-plot idiom is used correctly, but the figure does not show what the text says it shows (F8).
