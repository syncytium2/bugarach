# Murderboard run: what the 1973 Hansen paper says, now that it has been read

## The problem

Earlier on 2026-09-14, `detector_history.md` §4.1, *Where greatest-of began*, withdrew the credit for
greatest-of CFAR selection from V. Gregers Hansen's 1973 IEE conference paper, because nobody had read
it. That afternoon Tony's interlibrary-loan copy arrived. The change reviewed here shelves the copy on
the private darkroom shelf and brings §4.1 up to date with what the paper says. It keeps Tony's ruling:
no replacement origin, only what is known and what is not.

The risk was the obvious one. Once the paper was in hand, it was tempting to let its contents decide the
question the ruling leaves open, either way.

## What the review changed

- **The withdrawal was tied to the paper's content (round 2, role 4, blocking).** The draft read as if
  the credit had been withdrawn *because of* what the paper says. The shipped text separates the two.
  The credit was withdrawn because nobody had read the paper. Reading it *neither confirms nor rules it
  out*, because a paper that describes greatest-of without citing a source or claiming priority reads
  the same either way.
- **The unknowns were narrower than the evidence (round 2, role 2, two majors).** The list named only
  some of the paper's pre-1973 references and only two of the fields nobody searched. It now covers all
  21 references and Hansen's other pre-1973 papers, gives examples, and names every unsearched field:
  patents, technical reports, textbooks and handbooks, other proceedings, non-English literature.
- **A guessed citation (round 2, role 2, blocking, in the follow-ups todo).** The todo guessed that
  Hansen's Finn 1967 reference had the wrong year. The December 1967 RCA Review contents page shows the
  year is right, and the volume, end page and initial are misprinted. The todo and the shelf README now
  give the verified form.
- **Quotations and paraphrases tightened (roles 1 and 2).**
  - The Fig. 6 wording now quotes the caption, with its page.
  - The "survey" quotation is limited to what the paper calls a survey.
  - The 1972 memo is attributed to Hansen & Sawyers, the only source for it.
  - Rohling's wording in `lit_needed.md` now matches §4.1.
- **The built page dropped a list, and a paragraph fell into a bullet (round 2, role 10, two majors).**
  GitHub rendered both correctly. `md_to_page.py` needs blank lines that the source lacked. Both are
  fixed and checked in the rebuilt HTML.

## Rounds, and why this one stops unconverged

Round 1 ran all 11 roles. Round 2 re-ran roles 1, 2, 4 and 10 as fresh named agents against the repaired
text. Roles 3, 5–9 and 11 were not re-run. Round 2 still found blocking and major issues, and all of
them are applied. No third blind round was run on the round-2 repairs, so **this run is unconverged**.
The round-2 fixes used the reviewers' own suggested wording wherever they gave one.

Two reviewers noted that the worktree moved under them while they worked. Role 4's round-2 fixes landed
mid-review. Roles 1, 2 and 10 each report which state they checked.

## Residuals

- ⚠ **For Tony (role 2, finding 9a):** §4.1 says the 1973 paper is *"the earliest published description
  of greatest-of this project has found"*. It is true as scoped, but it ranks sources by date, which is
  close to what the ruling rules out. Keep or cut is his call.
- ⚠ **Correspondence (role 2, finding 15):** no record of anyone writing to the IET archives, V. Gregers
  Hansen, or David Shanks of Technology Service Corporation. The question is in the follow-ups todo.
- ⚠ **Unconverged:** no blind round after the round-2 repairs.
- **Stale darkroom page (roles 1 and 10):** the darkroom copy of `detector_history.html` is stale. It is

- **`lit_needed.md` renders as code on GitHub (role 10, finding 4):** part of the Hansen entry renders as
  a code block. It predates this change and is not fixed here.
- **§7 items 3–5 on the built page (role 10, finding 5):** they render as literal text. This predates
  this change and is already in the follow-ups todo.
- **Cell-averaging's origin (role 2, finding 14):** Finn & Johnson 1968 as the origin of cell-averaging
  has the same shape of problem. It is recorded as an open question, out of scope.
