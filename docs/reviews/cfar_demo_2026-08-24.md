# A working CFAR bench, and what it says about our six

Tony asked for a demo of how CFAR and its variants work, then for two readings on
top of it: which of our detectors is closest to CFAR, and what modern radar does
instead. This preserves all three. **Nothing here changes a detector**, and the two
findings that want a decision are pulled out at the bottom.

**Open first:** the demo is a published Artifact —
<https://claude.ai/code/artifact/0b6e0485-cbfe-4020-86ab-bad266ccedd5> — private to
Tony's account until shared. The page source is
[`docs/learned/cfar_scope.html`](../learned/cfar_scope.html); it is hand-authored,
not generated by a tool, which makes it the odd file in that directory. Every number
below comes from [`docs/learned/cfar_scope_check.js`](../learned/cfar_scope_check.js)
— `node docs/learned/cfar_scope_check.js`, no dependencies, about a second.

## What the demo does

A live A-scope: 220 range cells of exponential clutter, a sliding reference window
with guard cells, and the threshold each rule builds from it. Five rules — CA, GO,
SO, OS, trimmed-mean — over the same noise, in four environments (homogeneous, a
clutter edge, two targets inside one window, and an onset ramp). Drag on the plot to
park the window anywhere; an inset shows that window's cells on an amplitude axis
with the two half-means marked and the chosen one lit, so greatest-of selection is a
thing you watch happen rather than a formula.

**The multiplier is solved, not assumed.** At load, each rule's α is found by Monte
Carlo so all five hit the same false-alarm rate in homogeneous noise. Every
divergence after that is the environment breaking a promise all five made — which is
the CFAR argument itself, and the reason the page can be read as an experiment
rather than an illustration.

Two things the arithmetic forced during the build, both worth knowing because they
are the same two traps this repo can fall into:

- **A single-cell target cannot mask itself.** With point targets the guard-cell
  control did nothing at all, because the cell under test is excluded by
  construction and there was no other target energy to leak. Targets now spread over
  ±2 cells the way a matched filter output does, and the skirt cells are scored as
  neither hit nor false alarm. That is Weinberg's *"range spread target"*, and it is
  what a guard interval is for.
- **Averaged over a whole record, a clutter edge looks harmless.** CA read 0.99× its
  design rate on the edge scenario — the damage is local, confined to about one
  window either side of the step, and diluted to nothing by 190 quiet cells. The
  page and the harness therefore score the transition separately. A global
  false-alarm average is the wrong instrument for a local failure, which is a
  lesson our own promiscuity probe is already an example of.

## The numbers

Reference 8 cells a side, 2 guard cells, targets 13 dB, design P<sub>fa</sub> 1e-2,
300 records per row. Ratios are achieved over design.

**A clutter edge, scored inside the transition only:**

| rule | P<sub>d</sub> | ×design overall | **×design in the transition** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GO | 76.8% | 0.83 | **1.12** |
| CA | 77.0% | 0.95 | 2.03 |
| OS | 75.3% | 1.13 | 2.96 |
| TM | 76.7% | 1.13 | 3.32 |
| SO | 74.7% | 2.09 | 9.88 |

Greatest-of holds its design rate through a background step and every other rule
does not — Gandhi & Kassam's *"better than that of any other mean-level CFAR
scheme"*, reproduced.

**Two targets inside one reference window:**

| rule | P<sub>d</sub> |
| --- | --- |
| SO | 73.2% |
| TM | 65.0% |
| OS | 64.4% |
| CA | 52.9% |
| GO | **45.7%** |

And greatest-of comes last, which is the other half of the same sentence — *"the
detection performance in the multiple target environment is quite poor."* The rule
that is best at an edge is worst against a neighbour, and it is best and worst for
the same reason.

**Guard cells, homogeneous, spread targets:** CA falls 78.7% → 53.7% and GO 78.7% →
49.8% when the guard interval is removed. Self-masking is not a small effect once
the target occupies more than one cell.

**The ramp** — our own transition shape — orders like the edge but gentler: SO 1.91×
inside the ramp, GO 0.79×, everything else near 1.

## Which of ours is closest to CFAR

**LoCo in `maxlt` mode, and not narrowly.** It is the only detector with all three
ingredients at once: a rolling rate-local reference, a bar *derived from* that
reference (the 99.9th percentile of the surrogate pool — an order statistic, kin to
OS-CFAR), and a combination rule over the two halves (the max, which is greatest-of
selection). Its own docstring gives greatest-of's reason — the max is taken *"so a
rate edge cannot lower it and false-fire"* — arrived at here independently.

**The one that looks closest and is not — with an asterisk that is now the more
interesting half.** Test window against the mean of a surrounding window is the
textbook CA-CFAR diagram, but rate+context fires where `rate − context ≥ 5 Hz`. The
threshold is additive, and the multiplication is the mechanism. Its effective ratio
is enormous when the tissue is quiet and approaches 1 when it is busy —
over-conservative in sparse recordings, over-permissive in dense ones, and the
bake-off shows the signature at 34.8 firings in a block containing nothing.

**That is the live default, not the limit of the code.** `threshold_mode="multiplicative"`
exists and has been measured: F1 0.636 → 0.667 and probe firings 2.0 → 0.0 on
`baseline_quiet`, which is exactly the symptom an additive offset was predicted to
produce, disappearing when the bar scales with the background (forks §3, a different
tool and bench from the bake-off number above — do not put the two in one sentence).
It has not been switched on because the campaign that would fit `α` has not run and
the shipped value is a placeholder. **So in that mode rate+context is a genuine
cell-averaging CFAR detector, and the only thing between it and being one by default
is a calibration.**

Then CoactDetect (cell-averaging in shape, but its context is centred on the bin
under test with the shift running inside it, so self-masking is built in), then
binned SCE and CICADA (one fixed bar per region, pre-CFAR), then SPIKE-synch
(self-normalising, no bar at all).

**In the strict sense none of them is CFAR**, because no one has stated a design
false-alarm probability anywhere in this repo. LoCo has the architecture; it does
not have the promise.

## What modern radar uses

Answered from background knowledge, not from retrieval. **The families I am
confident about; the attributions I am not, and none of them should reach a document
without the primary in hand.** Named here only so a literature pass has somewhere to
start.

- **CA-CFAR is still the default.** The variants are exception handling.
- **The modern answer to CA-vs-GO-vs-SO is not to choose.** Variability-index CFAR
  tests the reference window for homogeneity, classifies it as homogeneous / edge /
  multi-target, and applies CA, GO or SO accordingly. Automatic censoring does the
  same for how many cells to trim. *(Smith & Varshney, unverified.)*
- **Clutter maps put the reference in time** — a cell against its own history over
  previous scans, rather than against its spatial neighbours. The closest radar
  analogue to a baseline/treatment design. *(Nitzberg, unverified.)*
- **Clutter that is not Rayleigh.** Sea clutter is heavy-tailed; a threshold derived
  from an exponential assumption misses its design rate by orders of magnitude.
- **Multichannel detection estimates a covariance matrix from the neighbouring
  cells**, not a scalar — adaptive matched filter, Kelly's GLRT, ACE. The
  sample-support rule there is the CFAR-loss argument one dimension up.
  *(Kelly 1986; Reed, Mallett & Brennan, both unverified.)*
- **Track-before-detect**: for weak targets, do not commit per scan — lower the
  threshold and integrate over trajectory hypotheses.

## Is there a position for deep learning

**Where it won: classification, not detection.** SAR recognition, micro-Doppler,
interference mitigation, clutter suppression. None of those carry a false-alarm
guarantee, and that is the pattern.

**Where it has not displaced CFAR: the primary detection decision**, for a
structural reason. α is derived from a stated P<sub>fa</sub> and holds as the
background moves, because the threshold is multiplicative on a local estimate. A
network's false-alarm rate is a property of its training distribution. At
operational rates the tail you are promising cannot be measured with feasible data,
and the usual losses optimise the bulk rather than the tail.

**The hybrid that works keeps the CFAR structure and learns a component of it** — a
model that predicts the local background, or that picks which rule applies. The
decision stays multiplicative-on-local-estimate; the estimate gets better.

**This is not hypothetical here, and the model track already found the same failure.**
Centre−surround ties the best hand-written detectors (0.668 ± 0.061 against
CoactDetect's 0.651 ± 0.044) at 1,149 parameters and a fraction of the cost — **and
transfers worse than two of the six from a quiet background to a busy one.** *Fit
busy, deploy quiet.* That is not an analogy for the radar argument, it is the same
one: the learned model's operating point is a property of its training distribution,
and a CFAR bar's is a property of the local window. It won on the bench and lost on
the axis CFAR exists for.

Two constraints that bind anything built on this: the model track is **not approved
to run**, and the bench **plants no assemblies** — participants are drawn fresh per
event, so no group recurs and the benchmark cannot reward the structure a learned
model would exploit.

## What wants a decision

1. **A design false-alarm probability is the highest-value import from radar, above
   any architecture.** It is the scoring rule under which a hand-written and a
   learned detector can be compared honestly — fix P<sub>fa</sub>, measure achieved
   P<sub>fa</sub> on the deployment distribution, compare recall at matched false
   alarms. Today the comparison is F1 on a benchmark and the promiscuity probe
   cannot fail. This is §5.3 and the probe todo restating each other, and the demo
   is a working example of what stating one buys.
2. **The cheapest real upgrade involves no learning at all.** We ship `maxlt`
   unconditionally; a variability test on the two half-contexts could pick symmetric
   versus maxlt per anchor. §5.4 already establishes that the estimator and the
   combination rule are separable.
3. **The history document argues for two fixes that have both since been built** —
   guard cells on all three rolling detectors, and a multiplicative bar for
   rate+context — and still describes them as absent. `forks.md` §3 and §4 are
   correct and current; §5.1, §5.2 and the figure tool's docstring are not. Filed as
   [`docs/todo/2026-08-24-the-history-document-describes-a-tree-that-has-moved-on.md`](../todo/2026-08-24-the-history-document-describes-a-tree-that-has-moved-on.md),
   with the trap written down: one commit title says the guard prediction held and
   forks §4a says it did not, so repairing those sections from `git log` would
   reintroduce a retracted claim.

## What this is not

**Not murderboarded.** Quotations are the ones detector_history already matched
against the primaries on the lit shelf, and the arithmetic is reproducible from the
harness — but the eleven roles have not run on this document, and the radar section
above is explicitly unretrieved. If any of it becomes a shipped explainer, methods
text or figure caption, it goes through `/murderboard` first.

**Not written to the darkroom.** The rule exists so a document meant for a person
does not stop at `docs/`; here the readable copy is the published Artifact, which
Tony can already open. If that reading is wrong, the fix is one `cp`, not a rewrite.

**The model is radar's, not this lab's** — square-law envelope, exponential cells, a
target that multiplies local clutter power. It reproduces the shapes of the argument.
The decibels do not transfer to a count of distinct ROIs.
