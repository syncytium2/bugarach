detector geometry · bugarach

Same statistic, same null,
different window

LoCo and CoactDetect count the same thing — distinct ROIs coactive in a bin — and build their nulls the same way, by circular-shifting each ROI inside a rolling context. Everything that separates them is the shape of the reference window and how it collapses to one number, which is the axis the CFAR literature is organised around. It also decides what a guard interval can and cannot do to each.

Three things that follow

CoactDetect is the only one with the CFAR promise

Radar chooses a false-alarm probability first and derives the multiplier from it. CoactDetect is the one detector here that states one — a per-bin alpha, with the viewer running 1e-4 on FAST and 1e-6 on SLOW, which is the range radar actually uses. LoCo's 99.9th percentile is a tuned constant, not a design point.

The manuscript decided against the per-bin form for a multiplicity problem — thousands of bins at one α. That is not a defect radar would recognise as disqualifying: CFAR's Pfa is per-cell too, and the answer is to set it from the total rate you will tolerate. The function's own default of 0.01 is what makes it look broken.

The surrogate null is partly immune to interferers

In radar, a second target in the reference window raises the estimate by its full amplitude — that is mutual masking, and it is why greatest-of loses to a neighbour. Here the null is built by destroying cross-ROI timing, so a neighbouring coordinated event contributes its rate to the null and not its coincidence.

Inference, not a measured result: both detectors should therefore be structurally more neighbour-resistant than a mean-level CFAR — and the crowding measurement shows a real cost anyway, so rate contamination alone is enough to do the damage. It predicts something cheap to test: the recall cost should scale with a neighbour's event count, not with how coordinated it is.

Neither guard is doing guard-cell work

Measured on a crowded recording with an internal control, the guard's recall gain is flat across the neighbour gap — CoactDetect +0.045 crowded against +0.046 isolated — and on the sparse bench, where nothing can be masked, it raises recall anyway.

It is lowering the bar, and precision pays: excising a span shrinks the null pool, and a fixed high percentile of a smaller sample underestimates the tail. A threshold knob spelled in seconds. The CFAR-shaped fix is censoring inside the estimator — drop the largest reference cells wherever they sit — not a span beside the anchor.

Numbers are from forks.md §4a (the corrected measurement — two earlier versions of that section reported the opposite and are retracted), detector_history.md §5 and §6, and the detector signatures. Crowding costs recall in both: CoactDetect −0.144 for a neighbour inside 15–30 s, LoCo −0.104.
