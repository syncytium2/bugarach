## What I checked and found clean

- The bake-off table (`line_bakeoff/bakeoff.json`) reproduces **exactly** for F1, fold range, recall, precision and parameter count on all 14 rows, including the paired per-fold differences and the 72-parameter delta (3 extra input channels × 8 × kernel 3 = 72).
- The supervised threshold-tightening table and the SSL oracle column reproduce **exactly** from `training/results.jsonl` (288 rows, arms 96/96/48/48 as claimed).
- Probe firings **are** excluded from precision: `BenchResult.precision` uses `n_hit / (n_detected − hot_fa)`, and every table on the page pools through `pool_scores`. (`Score.precision` in `score.py` does *not* exclude them — but nothing on this page reads it.)
- The matching tolerance is the scorer's 2.5 s, greedy-nearest, and pooling refuses mixed tolerances.
- `rigid_frames`' `floor(t + 0.5 + u)` **is** an exact integer shift per ROI for integer `t`, agreeing in distribution with `dither_spike_train(edges=True)`.
- The held-out fold is unreachable from any fit in `tube_self_supervised.py`: `split().train(held)` excludes it structurally.
- The chance loss is correctly identified: `softplus(0) = ln 2 = 0.693`.
- "The two references agree 0.71–0.77" (0.705 / 0.774) and "supervised tube catches 80–83 %, line 76–77 %" both check out.
- `18.36` samples is not clamped (`clamp(0.5, 64)`), and the reported smear widths are in samples as stated — I initially suspected a unit error and it is not one.

**Two cross-cutting answers to the questions posed.** *Do peak- and area-normalisation make the quantities comparable?* Across models and plant sizes, **no** — for two independent reasons: the peak-normalised smear caps vote *height* while the area-normalised DoG reads vote *integral*, so a burst is not capped where it counts (B1); and the probe's difference-of-peaks is only a common scale under an affine model, which a 6-layer GELU stack is not (C6). Within one model at one plant size the ratio is defensible; the cross-model and cross-K readings are not. *Where is the method the wrong tool?* At J = 10–20 s, rigid shift is not the trial-shifting of the cited lineage (A1) — it is a slow-timescale decorrelator, which is exactly what the page's own second open question is about.
