bugarach · the learned detector

Nobody labeled the data, so the benchmark had to be built first.

A {{N:b:learned.tube.n_params|,}}-parameter network that finds coordinated firing in calcium imaging, and the apparatus that makes its score mean something. It ties the best hand-written detector in this project and runs four times faster. The interesting part is what had to exist before that sentence could be checked.

Read the last section before quoting anything here. The recordings the detectors were scored on are simulated — their settings were measured from real ones, but nothing on this page says any detector is right about a real slice. The fold spread is not a confidence interval, and the top three are a tie.

The problem is the absence of a label

A calcium-imaging movie gives you, per cell, a list of times at which that cell fired. The question is when a group fired together — a coordinated event, with a start and an end. Six detectors in this project answer it, each by a hand-written rule: count active cells in a window, compare against a shuffled null, threshold.

Training a network to answer it instead runs immediately into the thing that makes the neighboring fields tractable and this one not. Sleep-EEG spindle detectors, hippocampal ripple detectors and epileptiform-discharge detectors all learn from events a human expert marked. There is no expert corpus of coordinated calcium events. There is no consensus rule to imitate either — the six detectors disagree with each other, which is the reason this project exists.

So scoring a detector against another detector's opinion is the only thing available, and it is worthless: it measures agreement, not correctness. The way out taken here is to manufacture the truth, and to manufacture it from the lab's own recordings so that it is not a toy.

Where the ground truth comes from

Measure {{N:g:source_recordings.provenance.n_slices_assessed}} real recordings without a detector

Baseline regions only — drug and washout windows are counted and skipped, because taking the properties of coordination from a treated slice assumes the answer. Per-cell rate, event jitter, how many cells participate, how many clusters the activity falls into, each against {{N:g:source_recordings.provenance.n_surrogates|,}} circular-shift surrogates. No detector runs in this stage, so nothing it produces can inherit a detector's blind spot.

Derive one generator specification from that measurement

Background rate {{N:b:spec.bg_rate_hz|.4f}} Hz per cell, event jitter {{N:b:spec.jitter_sec|.2f}} s, {{N:b:spec.n_roi}} cells, heterogeneous and bursty background. A human picks the cluster count — here {{N:g:source_recordings.k_chosen}} — from the published scan, because that choice moves the data set and a scan is not a decision.

Generate recordings with the events planted

Eight recordings, {{N:b:spec.duration_sec|,.0f}} s each, split into {{N:b:folds}} folds. Every coordinated event has a known time, so a miss and a false alarm are drawn, not inferred. Each recording also carries a block with nothing planted in it — a trap, described below.

Fit on three folds, score on the fourth, rotate

Hand-written detectors sweep their declared knob; networks train and pick a threshold. Same data, same procedure, same scorer. A detector never sees the fold it is scored on, and the network's threshold is chosen on held-out data drawn from the training regime and never re-picked on the recording being analyzed — a “re-tune on this slice” button would hide exactly the failure the transfer test below measures.

This is the part that took the longest and it is the part worth evaluating. The network is small enough to read in an afternoon. The apparatus that lets its score be compared against six ported detectors on equal terms is where the work went.

The architecture: a difference of Gaussians, learned

Look down a tube as the recording slides past. The tube is dark and single firings are specks; when several cells fire together a bright spot crosses the center. Jitter dims it. A busier background raises the whole field, but a real spot still stands above its own surround.

That last sentence is the architecture. Cells are pooled to one brightness trace, and the trace is convolved with a difference of two Gaussians, both area-normalized so a flat field integrates to zero. Three properties fall out of the shape rather than being trained for:

Any cell count. Cells are summed before the temporal filter, so the model never sees which cell is which and runs on a recording with thirty cells or three hundred.

One cell, one vote. Each cell is max-pooled over the center window before the sum, so a single cell bursting repeatedly cannot imitate a crowd. This is the distinction between coordination and activity, and it is enforced exactly rather than hoped for.

Rate invariance by construction. A zero-integral kernel cancels a uniform change in background. The claim is testable rather than asserted, and panel C below is the test.

Nothing in the model knows what a second is — receptive fields are in samples. The center widths are free parameters initialized at {{N:a:init_centres.0|.0f}}, {{N:a:init_centres.1|.0f}}, {{N:a:init_centres.2|.0f}} and {{N:a:init_centres.3|.0f}} samples, one sample being the finest thing the recording can resolve and therefore the only non-arbitrary place to start. What they converge to is the model's own estimate of how wide a coordinated event is.

The top three are a tie and should be read as one. The learned model leads on the mean by {{N:b:learned.tube.f1.mean|.3f}} against {{N:b:hand_written.coact.f1.mean|.3f}}, and their fold ranges overlap almost completely. Four folds of thirty planted events cannot separate them. The claim the numbers support is that a {{N:b:learned.tube.n_params|,}}-parameter network reaches the level of the best hand-written detectors in this project, having been given no more information than they were.

Cost is where the difference is. {{N:b:learned.tube.detect_sec.mean|.3f}} s to scan two hour-long recordings — four times faster than CoactDetect and seventeen times faster than LoCo, the two it ties with — from {{N:b:learned.tube.train_sec.mean|.1f}} s of training. It is not the fastest detector here: rate+context scans the same fold in {{N:b:hand_written.rate.detect_sec.mean|.3f}} s and sits about a tenth of F1 lower. Top-of-pack accuracy, seconds to fit, milliseconds to run, kilobytes to store, is the combination that makes a per-lab training loop plausible.

The two learned models at the floor are controls, and they did their job. The pooled trace collapses cells before filtering in time, which is precisely what the design argues against; the per-cell bank keeps every cell separate and spends {{N:b:learned.tiny.detect_sec.mean|.2f}} s per fold doing it. Both sit near F1 {{N:b:learned.trace.f1.mean|.2f}}. Building the invariant into the shape beat hoping a general architecture would find it — on this data, at this budget.

What the model said it did not need

Panel B above shows four scales collapsing into one band, which is the model reporting that it is not using its multi-scale capacity. Removing that capacity confirms it. One scale scores {{N:ablation:by_key.s1_c40.f1.mean|.3f}} at {{N:ablation:by_key.s1_c40.n_params|,}} parameters against the shipped four scales at {{N:ablation:by_key.s4_c40.f1.mean|.3f}} and {{N:ablation:by_key.s4_c40.n_params|,}} — a difference far inside the fold spread of {{N:b:learned.tube.f1.sd|.3f}}.

A second ablation went after a suspicious constant. One fitted surround-to-center ratio sat at {{N:a:ratios.1|.0f}} against a ceiling of 40, which this project treats as the search reporting that the range was wrong rather than as an answer. Raising the ceiling to 200 changed the score to {{N:ablation:by_key.s4_c200.f1.mean|.3f}} — identical. The clamp was not doing the work, and saying so is worth more than leaving it unexamined.

Two ways this model is worse than its headline

It fires into the trap. Every recording carries a block with nothing planted in it and a raised firing rate — activity without coordination, which is the thing a coordination detector must not confuse. The learned model fires {{N:b:learned.tube.hot_fa.mean|.2f}} times into that block per fold. The two hand-written detectors it ties with fire {{N:b:hand_written.coact.hot_fa.mean|.2f}} and {{N:b:hand_written.loco.hot_fa.mean|.2f}}. F1 cannot see this — firings inside the block leave both numerator and denominator by design — so the tie at the top is a tie at finding planted events and is not a tie at ignoring the trap.

The mechanism is legible in the kernel and it is a known defect rather than a mystery. Both Gaussians sit on the same axis with no gap, so the surround is largest exactly at the sample under test: an event contributes to the reference that judges it. And an area-normalized difference cancels the mean of a background change without canceling its variance, so a fixed threshold on a zero-mean but rate-scaled signal has a false-alarm rate that climbs with background. Both are classical radar results in a network's clothing, and the fix that literature prescribes — divide by the surround rather than subtract it — is the next experiment, not a finished result.

It transfers asymmetrically. Fitted on quiet recordings and deployed on busy ones, F1 falls from {{N:g:learned.tube.baseline_quiet.baseline_quiet.f1|.3f}} to {{N:g:learned.tube.baseline_quiet.baseline_busy.f1|.3f}}. Fitted on busy and deployed on busy it holds {{N:g:learned.tube.baseline_busy.baseline_busy.f1|.3f}}. The operational rule that follows is free to implement and is implemented: if a lab fits a model on its own recordings, it fits on the busier ones.

Adding an architecture is one class and one line

The requirement was that a new idea could be dropped in and land on the same accuracy-versus-cost curve as everything already measured, with nothing else edited. A registry does it — the sweep, the scorer and the figures all read from it: 

@register("tube", note="center-surround on the brightness trace")
def build_tube(*, n_scales=4, width=8, depth=6, max_center_frames=128):
    ...
    return Tube()

Torch is an optional extra. Nothing in the encoding layer imports it, so the measurement half of the package — the assessor, the generator, the six detectors, the scorer — installs and runs without a deep-learning stack at all. That split is deliberate: the apparatus has to be usable by a lab that will never train anything.

What this does not establish

Still simulated. The generator's settings were measured from {{N:g:source_recordings.provenance.n_slices_assessed}} real recordings; the recordings the detectors were scored on were not real. Nothing here says any detector is right about a real slice.

{{N:b:folds}} folds, eight recordings, thirty planted events per fold, one training run per fold. The ± is the standard deviation across folds and the range is their minimum and maximum. Neither is a confidence interval, and seed variance within a fold has never been measured — so fold-to-fold spread confounds data variation with training variation. This is the oldest open item on the model track and it is a prerequisite for believing any change smaller than {{N:b:learned.tube.f1.sd|.3f}} of F1.

A hit is scored within 1.5 s of a planted event, against a median realized event about 0.8 s across. That buys a trustworthy ranking and costs any claim about timing accuracy.

The cluster count was a human choice and it moves the data set. The scan ships beside the choice; a different reading of it cuts the event rate to roughly a quarter and builds a different benchmark.

No method from the literature has been run on these recordings, so nothing here claims to beat one. What the neighboring fields do, and which of their claims this work can and cannot make, is on the landscape page.

Timings are one machine, one process, no warm-up control — {{N:b:machine.platform}} on Python {{N:b:machine.python}}. They are order-of-magnitude comparisons, not benchmarks.
