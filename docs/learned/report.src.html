Six cells fire together in a third of a second. Finding that is harder than it sounds.

The problem first, on simulated data where the answer is known: the events are there, and almost nothing about the raw record says so.

How everything here is scored

Every number below comes from simulated recordings. A generator plants coordinated events at times it records, so each detection can be checked against truth rather than against another detector’s opinion. Nothing here is measured on a real slice.

Three recordings, forty-five planted events, at three recruitment levels — about 10, 6 and 3 of the 33 cells. A detection is an interval, not an instant: it counts as a hit if the planted event falls inside its span, or within 1.5 s of the nearer edge. Matching is nearest-pair and one-to-one, so a detector that splits one event into three is charged two false alarms rather than credited twice. Detectors reporting wide spans get a wider effective window — binned SCE reports roughly 9-second bins — which is deliberate, because scoring a correct 10-second bin as a miss would be worse, but it does mean the tolerance is not identical across the nine. Recall is the share of planted events found; precision the share of detections that were real; F1 their harmonic mean.

Each recording also contains a five-minute probe — a stretch of dense, random activity with no planted event anywhere in it. Firings inside it are kept out of every precision figure, for all nine detectors alike; folding them in would stop the number measuring the detector and start it measuring how dense the probe was made. It is not a small exclusion, and it is not evenly distributed: of binned SCE’s {{N:r:six.sce.n_detected|d}} detections {{N:r:six.sce.hot_fa|d}} are probe firings, and of CICADA’s {{N:r:six.cicada.n_detected|d}}, {{N:r:six.cicada.hot_fa|d}} are. The learned model fires {{N:r:learned.tube.hot_fa|d}} times in there. Precision below is computed over what is left — which means the probe cannot lower any score on this page. Firings inside it leave both the numerator and the denominator, so a detector that fired five hundred times in there would read exactly like one that never fired at all. It is a diagnostic, not a control, and the counts above are the whole of what it tells you.

Every learned number is a single training run at a single seed. Six runs in total. Nothing here carries an error bar, and with 45 planted events one event is 0.022 of recall — so no comparison on this page, learned or hand-written, has been shown to be a difference. Where the text ranks things, read it as an ordering of point estimates. The gap between an architecture that learns and one that does not is far too large to be seed noise; the gaps between operating points of the same model are not obviously so, and are not treated here as if they were.

Where the six run out

Six hand-written detectors already exist in this repo, each computing “more coincidence than the local background explains” by a different rule: rate+context, CoactDetect, LoCo, binned SCE, CICADA and SPIKE-synchronization. They are ports, held to 1e‑9 against a MATLAB original; CICADA derives from the Cossart lab’s CICADA (MIT, © 2019 Cossart Lab) and the synchrony profile follows cSPIKE/PySpike’s adaptive SPIKE-synchronization semantics (Kreuz lab) — the detector that finds coordination events in that time-varying profile is this project’s own, not PySpike’s. They are not mine, and the comparison below is against other people’s work.

Pooled across all three recruitment levels they look similar. Split by level, they do not: five of the six find nearly every event where ten cells take part — binned SCE finds three fifths — and almost none where three do. Call that collapse the participant floor.

That floor is the interesting part of the problem, and it is what made a learned detector worth trying: a model with the invariance built in might hold on further down.

The difference is where rate invariance comes from. The first two were given a long receptive field and left to discover that activity should be judged against its local background. Centre−surround does not discover it; it is that operation. Building the invariant in beat hoping for it, at half the parameters of either and forty times the training speed of the per-cell filter bank — though only 1.5 times the pooled trace’s, which trains in eight seconds. That is the one claim on this page that does not depend on beating anybody.

The network, layer by layer

The design is Tony DeFazio’s, and it started as a picture rather than an architecture: look down a tube as the recording slides past; when cells align, a bright spot crosses the centre. Every row below exists to force a property the detector must have.

#LayerWhat it doesWhy it is there OutputParams
0inputonsets on a sample grid, cells ordered by firing rate inherited from the shared encoder; this model sums the cells away, so order never reaches it — it is load-bearing for the filter bank N × T0
1cap per cellmax over ±1 sample, so a cell firing repeatedly within 0.2 s counts once one cell, one vote — stops one busy cell imitating a crowd N × T0
2sum over cellsfraction of cells active near each instant space invariance — runs at any cell count, never sees identity 1 × T0
3centre − surround 4 difference-of-Gaussian kernels; each centre width, its surround ratio and its gain are free rate invariance, by construction — a flat field integrates to zero, so a uniform background cancels 4 × T12
3b…and the raw tracethe un-subtracted brightness trace is carried forward unchanged so the head sees the absolute level too — which is the part the invariance does not cover +1 × T0
4dilated stack6 convolutions on those 5 channels, dilation doubling, 8 wide combines the scales and enforces that an event is a run, not a blip 8 × T1,128
5pointwise mixone score per sample thresholded into episodes and widths — what the six return T9

Layer 3 is the idea. A difference of Gaussians is positive in the middle and negative around it, area-matched so a uniform field sums to zero. Raise the background everywhere and the response does not move; concentrate the same activity into a moment and it jumps. That is “observed minus local context” — the quantity every one of the six computes by hand — written as one convolution and then fitted rather than configured. Centre−surround itself is an old idea from vision; what is new here is pointing it at a population coactivity trace in time rather than at an image in space.

Four of the head’s five inputs cancel a uniform background. The fifth does not. Row 3b is the brightness trace passed through unfiltered, and it swings roughly eightfold between a sparse and a busy stretch. So the invariance is built into most of the model rather than all of it — which is also a live alternative explanation for the transfer asymmetry further down, and one this page has not ruled out. Dropping that channel is a one-line change and would settle it.

What it scores

F1 reports one operating point out of a range a threshold moves through, and quoting it alone would flatter whichever detector happened to be tuned nearest its own optimum. Each of the six carries a declared sensitivity knob for exactly this reason, so all seven are shown as curves.

It wins one end of the curve and loses the other. Above precision 0.8 it is ahead of all six; below about 0.65 rate+context is ahead of it. And the participant floor the whole exercise was aimed at turns out to be a knob rather than a limit: turned up to three times the local background (excess 3), rate+context finds {{N:r:six_sweep.rate.3.by_frac.0.1|.0%}} of three-cell events at precision {{N:r:six_sweep.rate.3.precision|.2f}}, while the learned model at precision {{N:r:learned.tube.curve.1.precision|.2f}} finds {{N:r:learned.tube.curve.1.by_frac.0.1|.0%}}. The six were missing three-cell events because nobody had turned their knobs, not because they could not reach them — which retires the motivation this project started from.

Those two sentences are an ordering of point estimates, not a result. Every figure here rests on 45 planted events and one training run. The whole spread across the top three swept detectors is 0.011 of F1, which is about half of one event. Read the ranking as “these are the same to within what this benchmark can see”.

What is left is worth saying plainly. A model specified in one sentence, with {{N:r:learned.tube.params|,}} parameters and {{N:r:learned.tube.train_seconds|.0f}} seconds of training and no tuning against this benchmark, lands among six detectors that were hand-built and calibrated over an upstream campaign. That is a statement about how much of a detector is structure rather than tuning, and it does not require the model to be better than anything.

A score at a tuned operating point is also not what a lab is buying. The question that decides whether a detector is usable is what happens to that score when the next slice is busier than the one it was calibrated on — and that is the next section, where this model does worse than most of them.

Does it survive a change of background?

The claim behind centre−surround is that a uniform change in background cancels. That is testable: fit in one background, deploy in the other, carry the threshold over unchanged. Re-picking it on arrival would hide the failure this tests for.

Both backgrounds are untreated and both are measured — 0.0038 and 0.0175 Hz per cell, a 4.6-fold spread real slices show among themselves.

That spread is narrower than it sounds. Those two endpoints are the quarter and three-quarter points of the slice-population rate; as per-cell rates they sit at roughly the 60th and 83rd percentiles, so this is a 23-percentile band rather than an interquartile range. Any null result on such an axis is weak by construction, and this one is reported knowing that.

Deploying upward costs {{N:s:learned.tube.baseline_quiet.baseline_quiet.precision|.2f}} to {{N:s:learned.tube.baseline_quiet.baseline_busy.precision|.2f}} of precision and F1 {{N:s:learned.tube.baseline_quiet.baseline_quiet.f1|.2f}} to {{N:s:learned.tube.baseline_quiet.baseline_busy.f1|.2f}}. A model whose whole architectural argument is that a uniform change in background cancels loses a third of its precision to a 4.6-fold change in background.

Why it should: centre−surround cancels the mean, not the variance. A uniform rise subtracts out in expectation, but the fluctuations around it grow with the rate and produce chance alignments no surround removes. The uncancelled brightness channel is an equally good explanation and has not been ruled out.

Going up, the learned model is second-worst of five. Only rate+context loses more, and three of the four hand-written calibrations lose almost nothing or improve. Going down, everything is flat and the model sits in the middle. So the honest reading is that this test falsifies the architectural claim it was built to check: rate invariance by construction should have shown up here as a flat line, and it did not.

The failure this test exists to catch does reproduce — rate+context calibrated on the quieter background and carried up loses {{N:s:six_transfer.rate.baseline_quiet.baseline_quiet.precision|.2f}} to {{N:s:six_transfer.rate.baseline_quiet.baseline_busy.precision|.2f}} of precision, the same shape as the historical collapse this whole benchmark was built after. It just does not spare the model that was supposed to be immune to it.

What this still cannot show. The background span is the narrow band described above, so a larger shift is untested. Binned SCE produced no usable operating point in either direction, and CoactDetect none in the quiet-calibrated direction: their F1 peaked at the end of the searched grid, which this project treats as a search that stopped too early rather than an answer, so they are absent rather than scored at a boundary. And every learned row is one training run.

What the model learned about the data

The four centre widths are free parameters. They were initialised as a geometric ladder — 1, 2, 4 and 8 samples, starting at the finest interval the recording resolves — and then trained. Where they ended up is the model’s own estimate of how wide a coordinated event is:

One sample is 0.1 s at this recording’s 10 Hz grid. For comparison, the planted onset spread is 0.36 s and the generator’s own record puts a planted event’s realized footprint at a median of {{N:r:planted_span_sec.baseline_busy.median|.2f}} s on these recordings. The two middle scales landed at 0.42 s and 0.65 s.

That agreement is weaker than it looks, in three ways. The ladder started at 1–8 samples, which already brackets the answer, so the fitted widths moved 1.2–2.1× inside a range that contained the target before training began. Retraining on a quieter background — same generator settings, only the background rate changed — moved the second scale by 30%, while the events it measures moved 11% — about a third of that, not a match. And the footprint above is the generator’s ground truth, not an independent instrument. The honest claim is that the widths land in a plausible range, not that they recover the timescale.
It is still worth leaving them free. An earlier draft handed the model a bank of fixed time-scales centred on a width already measured — the answer written into the input. Making every width trainable is what makes the fitted value a measurement of anything at all.

The measurement this rests on

Every number here comes from simulated recordings, so the generator’s settings have to come from a real one. Measuring them with the six would make each simulated recording a restatement of whichever detector measured it.

The assessor avoids that: it has no operating point, only a comparison against a null built by circularly shifting each cell within the window — which holds every cell’s own firing rate and destroys only cross-cell timing. It was MATLAB-only, which broke the per-lab loop at its first step. A Python port now reproduces interface2’s measure_coordination_timescale.m to a relative 1e‑9 on every quantity it emits, across four parameter settings of one synthetic reference fixture.

What I would do next

  1. Multi-seed everything. Every learned number on this page is one training run. This is the cheapest thing that could change a conclusion, and until it is done no two learned numbers here have been shown to differ.
  2. Drop the raw brightness channel and re-run. One line. It would settle whether the transfer asymmetry is the variance story this page tells or simply the one channel that never had the background subtracted.
  3. Sweep the rate-band split. How many firing-rate bands the cells are pooled into was the question the exercise was built to answer, and it was unaskable while no model scored above {{N:r:learned.trace.f1|.2f}}. The one that finally did pools every cell into a single band.
  4. Vary the sampling rate across training recordings. Every recording here shares one imaging rate, so a model that quietly learned this rig’s sampling rate instead of the event timescale would pass every test on this page.
  5. Then the per-lab loop end to end — assess a real recording, parameterize the generator from it, train, and compare what the model finds against what the assessor measured on the same slice.

What would change these numbers

  • One seed. Stated above and repeated here because it conditions everything: no learned comparison on this page is tested.
  • A generator revision is in flight and moves the distribution this whole loop is fitted to. Every number would need re-running.
  • The background model is flat. The bench gives every cell the same background rate; the fitted heterogeneous model is not wired in yet. Real recordings have 35% of ROIs recording no events in a baseline window, against 2% here. So these recordings are easier than real ones in a way that is known and not yet corrected.
  • The architecture's central claim is falsified on this bench. Rate invariance by construction should have shown up in the transfer test as a flat line; the model lost a third of its precision deploying upward, worse than three of the four hand-written calibrations. Either the mechanism does less than the design says, or the uncancelled brightness channel is doing the damage — one line of code separates those two, and it has not been run.
  • The background span is narrow — the band described above — so a larger shift is untested in both directions.
  • Two of the six are not at their upstream-calibrated settings. Binned SCE in particular runs here at a 10-second bin against a calibrated 2-second one, which is most of why it sits last.
  • Nobody has decided who picks K, and it changes the derived event rate about sevenfold across its scan.
