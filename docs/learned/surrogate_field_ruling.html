Which surrogates enter the screen

A murderboard killed the surrogate a self-supervised detector was built on. The thesis survived, the surrogate did not, and the replacement is a measurement rather than an argument. Eight candidates were on offer. All eight run — Stella's figure 10 shows the field disagreeing on real data, and nothing predicts the winner in advance.

WHY THERE IS A DECISIONThe surrogate gave itself away one cell at a time

The proposal was to train a detector with no labels: discriminate a real recording from a dithered copy of itself, so whatever the model learns to key on is coordination. It failed because the dithered copy is identifiable without looking across cells at all.

A calcium event extractor cannot emit two onsets from one cell inside a single transient, so real within-cell intervals have a hard floor — 0.40 s in the fast stream, 3.20 s in the slow. Independent per-onset dithering has no such floor. The dithered class therefore contains intervals whose probability under the real class is exactly zero.

WHY COUNTING, NOT A MODELThe safeguard could not have fired

The design carried a negative control: dither a recording once, use that as the positive against a further-dithered negative. Flat result clears the design. But its positive class has already been dithered, so every leak arising from dithering sits on both sides of the comparison. The control sees only what a second dither adds — and the leak has already saturated.

The transferable rule

A self-supervised objective needs a control whose reference class is provably free of the defect — and the obvious control, built by applying the same corrupting operation once more, is not that. Anything that manufactures its own negatives inherits this trap. The remedy is a direct diagnostic with no learned model in the loop: count support violations at level, not increment.

WHAT IS ON OFFEREight candidates, and the split that matters is cost

Each candidate preserves more of the recording's structure than the killed one did. What separates them for the purposes of this decision is not their pedigree — it is whether running one means calling existing code or building a new algorithm with its own correctness tests.

THE RULING ON THE FIELDStella's figure 10 says you cannot pick in advance

Tony, 2026-09-10: my read of Stella figure 10 is we need to do all of them. You can't predict a priori which one is right. The figure's own text supports that in more detail than the figure does.

Stella ran six surrogates through the same pattern detector on the same monkey recordings. On simulated data with ground truth, five of the six behaved; on the real recordings, where nobody knows the answer, they returned overlapping but non-identical sets of significant patterns — and each surrogate has its own signature of extras.

One wrinkle before anyone cites this. The paper's discussion says nearly the opposite of its figure — that the five valid surrogates "show almost identical participating neurons, lags, and occurrence numbers" and reach "an almost identical significance level," then names TR-SHIFT as the method of choice. That summary is not what the figure-10 results section describes, and the cell-by-cell disagreements above are all quoted from that section. A reader citing the conclusion gets a different paper from one reading the figure.

Two things transfer regardless. Stella rules out uniform dither independently, on false-positive counts under a completely different analysis — which is our own leak finding arrived at by another route. And their recommended TR-SHIFT is a per-neuron rigid shift applied trial by trial: it is our own rigid shift, no wrap, and the trial structure it depends on is something continuous baseline recordings do not have.

HOW THEY WOULD BE TESTEDThree tiers, cheapest first

A candidate earns the next tier by passing the one before. The decisive tier needs no model at all — it is the tier that would have caught the original failure, and it runs in minutes.

ALREADY SETTLEDTwo axes that are not yours to rule on

J is per-stream

At J = 2.5 s, 50.4% of fast within-cell intervals sit inside 2J against 0.00% of slow ones. One value cannot serve both streams; the grid carries a fast axis and a slow axis.

τ is declared, not fitted

The dead-time floor is a property of the producer's event extractor. It is theirs to state and ours to record — inferring it from our own data would fit the very quantity the screen tests against. This is an ask that should go out whichever way you rule.

THE RULINGWhere the three stand

01

Which candidates run · RULED

All eight. Nothing in the literature or in our own data predicts which surrogate is the right null for this preparation, and the one systematic comparison that exists shows the field disagreeing on real recordings. A field that disagrees is a field you measure.

This retires the suggestion above it, which was to let the literature prune the expensive four. Reading the literature is what removed that option.

02

Grid, or independent contests · OPEN

The four tube variants were explicitly "not a race" — the guard only paid once the bar was multiplicative. Surrogate choice may interact with the pooling operator and with J the same way.

Suggested Grid, and the ruling above makes this the one that now matters — eight surrogates crossed with a per-stream J and a pooling operator is a large product, where before it might have been two or three survivors. The saving grace is that the counting tier has no pooling operator: it is grid-free and runs over all eight at once. Fix the grid at the per-cell discriminator, on whatever the counting tier leaves standing.
03

Whether Stella answers part of it · RULED

It answers it in the opposite direction from the one anticipated. It does not retire candidates — it removes the grounds for retiring any, and it independently rules out the uniform dither we had already killed by counting.

What it does hand over is a caution about its own conclusion section, and the fact that its recommended method is our cheapest candidate wearing a trial structure this preparation does not have.

One thing to confirm before reusing a number

The two measured tables in the run record are in different units and the second does not state its conditions. The leak table reports sub-floor rates per 60-second window, by stream and by J — 29.5% and 38.3% for fast. The saturation table reports 0.932 for one dither, with no stream, J or window length given.

Those are not reconcilable as written. The saturation argument does not depend on the exact value — a leak that is total saturates whatever the conditions — but anyone quoting 0.932 alongside 29.5% should pin its conditions first.
