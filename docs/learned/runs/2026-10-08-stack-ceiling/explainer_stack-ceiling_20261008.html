The tallest stack that shifting can build, and how much of it already stands

A simulated recording, one row per ROI (region of interest, a cell) and one black mark per calcium-event onset. Pick a moment and a window of width w around it. The height is the number of ROIs with an onset in the window. Now take the period p around the same moment and let each ROI's onsets slide around it, wrapping at the ends (a circular shift), each ROI by its own amount. Every ROI with an onset anywhere in the period can be slid into the window, so the tallest stack shifting can build is the number of ROIs with an onset in the period: the ceiling. The fill is the height divided by the ceiling. No probability is computed anywhere on this page.

and at least 3 ROIs stand

The call rule. A tower at a moment stands without any shift. At another moment of the same period, a tower that tall has to be built, and that takes some shift per ROI. The cost to rebuild elsewhere is the median of that shift over the other moments of the period, in seconds. A planted event is costly to rebuild: its ROIs are otherwise far apart. In a stretch where every ROI's rate is raised a tall tower is cheap to build anywhere. A moment is called when the cost reaches the setting and at least 3 ROIs stand; called moments under 0.5 s apart are one call. Fill is offered as the plainer rule to compare.
