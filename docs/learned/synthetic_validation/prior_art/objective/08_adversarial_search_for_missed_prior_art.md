# Lit review 08: prior art the authors have missed

Reviewer brief: assume the scoring and tuning method (a)-(f) is a reinvention of something with a standard name, and try to prove it.
Date of search: 2026-11-13. All statements are tied to an access level. Anything marked "from memory, unverified" was not checked against a source in this session.

- **abstract only**: only the abstract or a repository record was read.

## Headline verdict

The method is not new in its parts. Every element (a)-(f) has a named precedent in at least one field, and one field does almost the whole pipeline end to end under standard names: **gravitational-wave transient searches** ("time slides" for the background, "injections" for planted events, "sensitive volume / efficiency at a fixed false-alarm rate" as the objective, and mock data challenges with a known exact false-alarm probability to measure the bias of the time-slide estimate). The neuroscience name for the shifted negative is **"trial shifting" / "spike-train shifting" surrogates** (Grün 2009; Stella et al. 2022). The statistical name for (a) is **Neyman-Pearson classification** (Tong, Feng & Li 2018); the imaging name for the reported curve in (f) is the **FROC curve** (Bunch et al. 1978; Chakraborty 2013); the speech name for the operating point is **"false reject rate at N false alarms per hour"**.

What I could not find stated anywhere in the exact form used here: (i) the specific rule in (d), "smallest co-active count that the shifted null reaches at most once per hour, per recording", and (ii) a canonical citation for the grid-edge rejection rule in (e). Both are obvious instances of standard principles (CFAR / NP thresholding; "extend the grid"), so they should be presented as applications, not contributions.

## (b) Pitfalls these fields document that the design does not yet address

1. **A cap enforced on an empirical count is violated about half the time.** Tong et al. 2018 (full text): setting the threshold so that the *empirical* type I error is ≤ α gives a classifier whose *population* type I error exceeds α in roughly half of the realisations. The design's (a) and (d) both use the naive rule ("at most once per hour on the shifted null"). At a cap near 1 per hour and a few hours of recording the null count is a small Poisson number, so the realised false-call rate on fresh data will exceed the cap often. The standard fix is a high-probability bound (choose the threshold from an order statistic with a stated tolerance δ) or, at minimum, a confidence interval on false calls per hour at the chosen operating point.

2. **More shift draws do not buy independent null data.** Wąs et al. (full text, Sec. 3.3): the variance of the time-slide false-alarm estimate "starts saturating when a few hundred time slides are used", because every slide reuses the same single-detector triggers. With ±20 s offsets on about 30 channels the replicate shifted negatives are strongly dependent; treating them as independent hours will understate the uncertainty on the false-call rate and on the bias in (c).

3. **The shifted null is contaminated by the events themselves.** Capano et al. (full text): leaving signal triggers in the slid background inflates the estimated background ("a conservative overestimate of the FAP"), while removing them is mean-unbiased but not self-consistent. In this design the shifted negative of a recording still contains the onsets that belonged to real (or planted) coordinated events; after shifting they raise per-channel local rates near event times. The twin-minus-shifted "bias" in (c) therefore mixes at least two things: what shifting destroys in the background, and what leftover event onsets add. They have opposite policy implications and should be separated (e.g. shifted negative of the twin vs shifted negative of the planted recording vs twin).

4. **Shifts remove shared structure that is not the target.** Grün 2009 (full text): the shift must exceed the precision of the joint events yet stay small relative to rate modulation; shifting smooths rate profiles. The ±20 s choice preserves minute-scale co-modulation but erases any shared rate transient faster than about 20 s (state changes, artefacts, common drive). Such transients produce second-scale co-activity that the null says is rare, so they will be scored as true events on real data and never as false calls. The GW literature flags the same failure for detectors with correlated noise (time slides underestimate the false coincidence rate for co-located detectors; **search snippet only**, arXiv:0908.2085). On simulated data the twin exposes this only if the simulator contains such transients.

5. **A single null construction has unknown systematic error.** Thompson et al. 2018 (full text): two independent constructions of simulated false alarms gave reliabilities of 24% and 51% for the same population; the authors could not turn this into a formal systematic error bar, and noted each construction misses specific false-alarm types. The design has one null. A second, differently built null (for example a within-window or interval-jitter null, or dithering with a dead time, per Stella et al.) would bracket the false-call rate on real data, where no twin exists.

6. **Tuning and measuring on the same simulated sets is optimistic.** Thompson et al. tuned Robovetter thresholds and measured completeness/effectiveness on the same injected, inverted and scrambled sets, and noted simulated sets "do not entirely emulate the observed data". Element (e) addresses the first half; the second half (simulator realism) is addressed only by one differently-assumed column. Name it as an inverse-crime control and say what it does not cover.

7. **Results depend on the matching rule.** Chakraborty 2013 (full text): the proximity criterion is a judgement call and must be identical across compared methods. Bilen et al. (full text): collar-based matching puts "significant emphasis on start and end times"; they move to intersection criteria. Tatbul et al. (full text): one long call covering several events, or one event split across calls, needs an explicit cardinality rule. The design uses one-to-one matching; it should state the tolerance, show the curve is stable under a reasonable change of tolerance, and say how merged and fragmented calls are counted (a merged call that swallows two planted events loses one under one-to-one matching, which penalises long-window detectors).

8. **No chance baseline on the curve.** Kim et al. 2022 (full text): under a lenient scoring protocol a random score matched trained models; they recommend always reporting an untrained baseline. With generous matching windows and dense planted events a random caller at the capped rate will "find" a nonzero share. The curve should carry that floor.

9. **Unbounded x-axis and comparison at one point.** Chakraborty: the FROC x-axis is unbounded and ordinate-at-fixed-abscissa is only one of several figures of merit; Bilen et al. integrate up to a stated e_max. If detectors' curves cross, a single operating point misleads; a partial area up to the cap (PSDS-style) is the established complement.

10. **Timing is not scored.** Quickest-detection theory (Veeravalli & Banerjee, full text) treats delay as the quantity traded against false-alarm rate. The design scores found/not found only. If call timing or span matters downstream, timing error at the operating point should be reported.

11. **False-alarm rate "per hour" assumes stationarity.** FAR = 1/E∞[τ] is defined for a stationary pre-change process. Wąs et al. explicitly leave non-stationarity uninvestigated. If false calls cluster in particular states, a recording-average cap hides hours that far exceed it. Report the distribution of false calls across time blocks, not only the mean.

12. **Multiplicity across settings and detector classes.** Usman et al. (full text, Sec. 3.6) apply a trials factor when candidates are ranked in several classes. Searching many settings against the same null and then quoting the null rate at the winner is the same problem; (e)'s separate confirmation draws handle it on simulated data but not for the per-recording threshold in (d), which is set and used on the same recording.

## (c) Elements for which I found NO exact precedent

Stated plainly, with what was searched.

- **(d) as worded.** I found the principle everywhere (CFAR; NP thresholding from a null sample; GW thresholds at a fixed false-alarm rate; trigger rate budgets) and one calcium-imaging paper that thresholds the number of coactive cells for significance (Miller et al. 2014, Methods not opened). I did **not** find any source that sets the minimum co-active channel count, per recording, as the smallest count a channel-shifted null reaches at most once per hour. Searches: calcium "population events"/"synchronous calcium events" + shuffled threshold; multielectrode "network burst" + surrogate threshold; seismic coincidence trigger tuning; PubMed query on SCE thresholds (zero hits); CFAR reviews. The neuroscience precedents I located use a p-value or percentile per frame, not a per-hour event rate. Verdict: a known variant of a standard idea; the per-hour, per-recording form appears uncommon. Not a novelty claim worth making, but also not something to cite as "standard practice" without a source.

- **(c) as a routinely reported number.** Comparing a surrogate-based false-alarm estimate against ground truth on simulated data is established (Capano et al. mock data challenge; Stella et al. false-positive analysis; Thompson et al. inversion vs scrambling). I found no source that reports, as a standing column, "false calls per hour on an unplanted twin minus false calls per hour on the shifted negative" and calls it the bias of the negative. Verdict: known variant; the explicit bias column appears uncommon and is a reasonable thing to keep, provided the contamination issue in pitfall 3 is handled.

- **(e) grid-edge rejection.** Only tutorials and software documentation; no canonical methodological citation found (one search; Bengio 2012 surfaced but was not opened). Treat as common practice without a standard reference.

- **Not found at all:** any sharp-wave-ripple, spindle or interictal-spike detection paper that tunes to a false-calls-per-hour cap on a shifted null. Those fields (as far as the snippets show) tune to F1 or SD thresholds against human labels. This is the one place the design is ahead of its home field's practice rather than behind another field's. Caveat: the ripple and spindle sources were reached at snippet level only.

- **Not covered in this pass:** scan statistics, NAB, change-point evaluation metrics, DET curves (Martin 1997), McClish partial AUC, interictal spike detection, Withers 1998. No claim is made about them.

## (d) The three most important things to add or change

1. **Name the framework and replace the naive cap.** Call (a) what it is: Neyman-Pearson selection (Tong et al. 2018), equivalently "efficiency at a fixed false-alarm rate" (Usman et al. 2016) or "miss rate at N false alarms per hour" (keyword spotting), and call the reported curve a FROC-type curve (Chakraborty 2013; Bilen et al. 2020). Then adopt the NP lesson: an empirical cap is exceeded about half the time. Either choose thresholds in (a) and (d) with a stated violation tolerance, or report an interval on false calls per hour at the operating point that accounts for the dependence among shift replicates (Wąs et al.).

2. **Cite the shifted negative under its existing names and decompose its bias.** (b) is trial shifting / time slides (Grün 2009; Stella et al. 2022; Wąs et al. 2010). For (c), cite Capano et al. 2017 and split the bias into (i) shared sub-20 s background structure destroyed by shifting and (ii) leftover event onsets inflating the null. Add a second, independently constructed null so real-data false-call rates are bracketed rather than point-estimated (Thompson et al. 2018).

3. **Harden the scoring.** State the matching tolerance and the rule for merged and fragmented calls; show sensitivity of the curve to that tolerance (Chakraborty; Bilen; Tatbul); put a random-caller floor on the curve (Kim et al. 2022); report a partial area up to the cap alongside the single operating point; and report false calls by time block, not only the hourly mean.
