### Claim 3. How a "hit" is defined

| Convention | Definition as stated in the source | Proposed / attributed to | Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| Any-overlap (OVLP) | A reference event is detected if any hypothesis overlaps it; a hypothesis with no overlap is a false positive; durations ignored; multiple hypotheses on one reference not counted as false alarms; a "guard band" may allow near misses (Ziyabari et al. use zero). | Name and critique: Ziyabari/Shah et al. | Time-aligned event scoring (TAES) | Per-event fractional TP = detected duration / reference duration; FP fractional and capped at 1 per event; one long hypothesis over several references credits only the first. | Ziyabari/Shah et al. Sec. 2.5 ("a new metric") | full text opened |
| Epoch / sample-based | Reference and hypothesis sampled at fixed intervals, each sample scored. Ziyabari use 0.25 s; SzCORE proposes 1 Hz; Temko uses 8 s epochs with 50% overlap. | Long-standing; Temko Sec. 2.1.1; Ziyabari Sec. 2.3; SzCORE Sec. 2.3.1 | full text opened |

**Verdict, claim 3: SUPPORTED as a summary.** Important nuance for our chapter: the dominant seizure-detection convention (any-overlap) is NOT one-to-one. Our one-to-one event matching is therefore stricter than the seizure-detection default, and should be attributed to the right lineage.

## (b) Wording

**Use**

- "Scoring a continuous-time detector by the share of reference events detected together with the number of false detections per hour of recording is long-established practice in automated seizure detection (Gotman 1990; Wilson et al. 2004; Saab & Gotman 2005; Shoeb & Guttag 2010), where Temko et al. (2011) call these 'the two most common event-based metrics'."
- "The unit varies: per hour, per 24 hours, or per day."
- "The same field documents the weakness we guard against. Temko et al. (2011) showed that lengthening each detection raised the event detection rate and lowered false detections per hour together, so that 'both event-based metrics can be made arbitrarily good', and recommended reporting the mean duration of false detections or epoch-based metrics alongside."
- "Any-overlap scoring ignores duration and has been described as 'very permissive' (Ziyabari et al. 2017; Shah et al. 2021)."
- "Current practice pairs event-based with sample-based scoring and caps event duration (SzCORE; Dan et al. 2024)."
- "One-to-one matching of events to detections with a minimum overlap follows sleep-spindle evaluation (Warby et al. 2014); the seizure-detection default (any overlap) is more lenient."
- "Operating points are chosen for tolerability to the user, and quoted targets range from about one false detection per hour to fewer than one per 24 hours; we read this spread as showing that the acceptable rate is a choice rather than a derived quantity." (flag as our reading)
- "Keyword spotting reports the same pair: false-reject rate at one false alarm per hour (Sainath & Parada 2015)."

**Avoid**

- "the standard metric pair" or "the gold standard": several sources say there is no standardisation. Say "most common" or "long-established".
- "introduced by Gotman (1982)": the 1982 abstract has no per-hour rate; unverified until the full text is read.
- "SzCORE warns that event metrics can be gamed": it does not; it adopts any-overlap.
- "gamed": no source uses the word. Their terms are "misleading", "arbitrarily good", "over-optimistic", "permissive", "artificially high".
- "Warby et al. report sensitivity and false detections per hour": they report recall, precision and F-score.
- "false positive rate" for FD/h without definition: it is a count per unit time, not FP/(FP+TN). Note also that Shoeb & Guttag and Baumgartner et al. call this quantity "specificity"; do not copy that usage.
- "the literature states the acceptable false-alarm rate is a convention": not stated verbatim anywhere I opened.
- "seizure detection uses one-to-one matching": it generally does not.

## (d) Things that contradict or complicate the claims

1. **Standardisation is weaker than "standard pair" implies.** Temko 2011, Ziyabari 2017 and SzCORE 2024 each open by complaining that metrics differ between papers. The pair is common, not universal; some papers report only epoch-based metrics (Temko Sec. 1).
2. **Any-overlap is not one-to-one.** Under OVLP, several detections on one seizure are usually not false alarms, and one long detection can credit several seizures. Our one-to-one rule is stricter and has a different lineage (Warby 2014; TAES partly).
3. **The current benchmark keeps any-overlap.** SzCORE chose any overlap plus generous tolerances (30 s before, 60 s after) on purpose, to favour sensitivity. So the newest standard is lenient on timing and relies on sample-based scoring and a 5-minute cap, not on stricter event matching.
4. **FD/h is not comparable across datasets.** Temko Sec. 4.6: it depends on seizure density (their data ~2.6 seizures/h vs 3.3-4.9 elsewhere) and on mixing healthy with sick subjects (0.12 vs higher FD/h). The same caution applies to any "false calls per hour" in our data: it depends on the event rate and on which recordings are included.
5. **False-detection definitions differ**: no overlap with a reference (Temko), outside a two-minute window from onset (Kelly), declaration in the absence of a seizure (Shoeb), with or without merging within 30 s (Temko's FD/h (30 s)).
6. **Temko's remedy is not a duration constraint on true detections.** MFDD measures the duration of FALSE detections only. A detector could still make over-long true detections; that is caught only by epoch/sample-based precision or by TAES. If our worry is long calls in general, the right companions are a time-based metric (fraction of recording flagged, sample precision) as well as a duration statistic.
7. **TAES can look harsh.** In the Ziyabari worked example TAES gives 33% sensitivity where OVLP gives 100%; the authors themselves keep OVLP alongside it. A third-party snippet says the 2025 SzCORE challenge ranked by event-based F1, not by sensitivity at fixed FD rate (search snippet only, unverified).
8. **Terminology trap.** "Specificity" is used for false detections per hour/24 h in Shoeb & Guttag 2010 and Baumgartner et al. 2018, and for TN/(TN+FP) in Temko 2011. SzCORE and Warby both argue true-negative-based metrics are ill-defined for events.
9. **Not checked:** interictal spike detection; the Epilepsia text of SzCORE; any FDA/ILAE device-validation standard (e.g. Beniczky & Ryvlin 2018), which might state an explicit acceptable rate. From memory, unverified: such standards discuss false alarm rate qualitatively rather than fixing a number.
