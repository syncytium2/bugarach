# Lit check 05: surrogate methods (train shifting as an established null)

Date: 2026-11-13. Scope: is "shift each cell's whole event train by its own random offset" an established surrogate, what does it keep and destroy, and what cautions apply to using it both to set a threshold and to count false calls.

## (b) Wording

### Use

- "Shifting each unit's entire train by its own random offset is an established surrogate, introduced for joint-spike analysis by Pipa et al. (2008) and evaluated as 'spike train dithering' (Louis, Borgelt & Grün 2010), 'SHIFT' (Louis et al. 2010) and 'trial shifting' (Stella et al. 2022), where it was the recommended choice."
- "The shift leaves every within-cell interval unchanged and destroys between-cell timing on scales shorter than the shift range (Pipa et al. 2008). Rate changes are preserved only approximately: they are smoothed over the shift range (Louis, Borgelt & Grün 2010; Grün 2009; Stella et al. 2022, Table 1)."
- "The shift range declares what counts as rate and what counts as coordination (Amarasingham et al. 2012; Harrison et al. 2013; Pipa et al. 2008). With ±20 s, co-variation slower than roughly the shift range is treated as shared rate; co-variation faster than that is treated as coordination and will be detected as such."
- "In calcium imaging, an independent circular shift of each cell's activity is a standard null for the number of co-active cells (Dard et al. 2022; Bocchio et al. 2020), with the threshold set at a high percentile (99th) of the surrogate distribution."
- For the validation design: "Spike-train surrogate benchmarks count false positives on independently generated data that reproduce the recorded rate profiles (Stella et al. 2022; Louis, Borgelt & Grün 2010; Pipa et al. 2008). Our negative is instead a surrogate of the recording itself."
- When describing spike papers, keep their terms (spikes, firing rate, ISI); for our data say "events", "event rate", "inter-event intervals".

### Avoid

- **"shift predictor" as a name for our method.** The shift predictor (Perkel et al. 1967; Gerstein & Perkel 1972) shifts by whole stimulus periods, is treated as a special case of trial shuffling (Louis, Borgelt & Grün 2010) and assumes stationarity across trials. Cite it as ancestry only.
- **"keeps each cell's event count"** while onsets pushed past the ends are dropped. The count is kept only with wrap-around (which Louis, Borgelt & Grün use expressly so as not to underestimate chance coincidences) or if the analysis window excludes the outer 20 s. Say "keeps each cell's event count up to edge losses" and give the measured loss, or change the implementation.
- **"keeps slow shared rate changes" without a scale.** Say "approximately keeps shared rate changes slower than the shift range". Minute-scale is defensible for ±20 s; tens-of-seconds is not.
- **"destroys which cells are active within a second or two of each other"** as the only thing destroyed. It destroys between-cell timing at every scale up to about the shift range.
- **"rate-matched"** as if it were a literature term. It is ours; none of the sources uses it. Define it once.
- **"exact" or "valid" test.** A shift centred on the observed train is the spike-centred form that Platkiewicz et al. (2017) show is not exact for per-spike jitter.
- **"the false-positive rate of the detector is X"** when X was counted on the same shift surrogate that set the threshold. That number is near the nominal level by construction (Takigawa et al. 2024).
- Citing **Villette 2015** for the co-active-count null; citing **See 2018** as calcium imaging; citing **Dard 2022** as eNeuro; stating **Malvache 2016**'s reshuffling rule before the supplement is read; calling **Miller 2014** a circular shift.
- "Stella et al. show train shifting preserves rate modulation exactly": their Table 1 says "Approx.".

## (d) What contradicts or complicates the claims

1. **Drop versus wrap.** Our shift drops onsets pushed past the ends. The published version wraps, and says why: to conserve overlap and not underestimate the expected coincidence count (Louis, Borgelt & Grün 2010, p. 367). Dropping has two effects. (i) The count is not preserved: for a uniform shift in ±20 s the mean absolute shift is 10 s, so a cell with a roughly constant event rate loses about 10 s / T of its events on average, up to 20 s / T (about 1.7% and 3.3% for a 10-minute recording). (ii) The first and last 20 s of every surrogate are depleted, so chance co-activity is underestimated there and the threshold is too low at the edges. These figures are my arithmetic, not from a source. Wrapping has its own published caveat (start and end rates must match).
2. **Rate changes are smoothed, not kept.** All three Grün-group sources say so. The practical statement is Louis et al. 2010 (Front): with a sharp shared rate step, SHIFT gave up to 10% false positives at a 1% level. The calcium analogue is any shared change in event rate that is fast relative to 20 s (a drug wash-in front, a state change, a stimulus): the shift surrogate will under-predict co-activity around it and the detector will call events there.
3. **What ±20 s declares.** In Pipa et al.'s terms the shift range is the lower bound on co-modulation that counts as "rate". Their ratio of shift to coincidence window is 2-5; Harrison et al. say "somewhat larger". Ours is 10-20 (20 s against 1-2 s). That buys power but means any shared modulation on a 2-20 s scale is, by our definition, coordination. This is a definitional choice the chapter should state, since the claim "destroys only second-scale co-activity" is not what the surrogate does.
4. **The calcium precedent is not the same null.** Dard 2022 and Bocchio 2020 use a *circular* shift with no stated bound, presumably over the whole recording, which keeps each cell's count exactly and removes shared slow rate changes as well. Our bounded shift keeps slow shared changes (approximately) and loses events at the edges. The calcium papers therefore support "independent per-cell shift, percentile threshold on the co-active count"; the preservation of slow co-modulation rests on the spike-train sources only.
5. **Threshold and false-call count from the same generator.** If the co-active-count threshold is a percentile of the shift surrogate, then the number of false calls on further shift surrogates is fixed near the nominal level by construction and says little about the detector. Takigawa et al. 2024 state the condition and demonstrate the failure. For detectors that do not use the shift internally, the count is informative; for the one that does, an independently built negative is needed (a generative model matched to the recorded rate profile, as in Stella et al. 2022, or a different randomisation).
6. **Our negative is not their negative.** The spike-train benchmarks generate independent data from a model. A shifted copy of a real recording still contains each cell's contribution to real coordinated events (for example, a cluster of onsets in one cell), merely displaced. Whether that makes the false-call count higher or lower than on truly uncoordinated data is not settled by any source found; the sources only establish that train shifting is empirically conservative on generated data.
7. **Chance re-alignment.** With independent uniform offsets in ±20 s, the difference between two cells' offsets is triangular on ±40 s, so two cells that were co-active within ±1.5 s remain so with probability about 3/40 ≈ 7% per surrogate (my arithmetic). Pairs are not fully decoupled; higher-order groups are, almost entirely. Pipa et al. make the same point qualitatively (destruction probability rises steeply with pattern complexity).
8. **Spike-train assumptions that do not obviously carry over.** The case against per-spike uniform dithering rests on refractoriness (dead-time of 1-2 ms), ISI regularity (CV < 1) and spike loss under millisecond binning with clipping (Stella et al. 2022). None of these is established for calcium event onsets, whose minimum separation is set by indicator kinetics and the event detector, and whose regularity is unknown here. The argument *for* whole-train shifting does carry over, because it does not depend on what the within-cell structure is, only on leaving it untouched. Conversely, the benchmark results (false-positive and false-negative percentages) were obtained at millisecond coincidence windows and 15-25 ms shifts on trial-structured data; they are evidence about the method's behaviour, not numbers that transfer.
9. **Trial structure.** Stella et al. note trial shifting "requires a segmentation of the spike trains into longer temporal sequences", with offsets independent across trials so that a repeated pattern is displaced differently each time. A single continuous recording with one offset per cell is the NeuroXidence/SHIFT form; within one surrogate, all of a cell pair's coincidences move together. Many surrogates are needed for the same reason.
10. **Corrections to the brief's citation list**: Dard 2022 is in *eLife*; See 2018 is electrode recording of spikes; Villette 2015 uses a between-cell permutation for sequences; Miller 2014 uses a within-cell interval shuffle; Malvache 2016's rule is unread. The library README's statement that Louis, Borgelt & Grün ch. 17 is "still not held" is out of date (it is on the shelf), and Pipa 2008 is still marked "unread" there.
