# Literature verification 04: gravitational-wave transient searches

Scope: whether time-shifted negatives, injection-based sensitivity at a false-alarm cap, and the signal-contamination bias are established tools in LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA transient searches. Checked 2026-11-13.

## How to read the access column

- **Full text, local**: I downloaded the arXiv PDF, converted it to text and searched or read the passages myself. Quotes in these rows were seen directly.
- **Full text, via fetch**: the ar5iv HTML full text was opened, but I read it through a fetch tool that returns a small model's extraction. Short quotes are as that tool reported them; section numbers are ar5iv's. Treat the wording as close but re-check before quoting in print.
- **Abstract only**: only the publisher abstract page was reached, and it was paraphrased by the tool.
- DOIs marked (M) are written from memory or from the standard publisher pattern and were not confirmed on a page.

**What the shift preserves and destroys, as the sources state it.** Preserved: each detector's own trigger list (rates, glitch population, clustering in time), because the whole list is moved rigidly. Destroyed: any between-detector coincidence at the physical lag, whether from signals or from correlated noise. No source I opened lists "what is preserved" as a formal property; this summary is my reading of the procedure descriptions.

**Verdict, claim 1: verified.** Time slides are standard, used from initial-LIGO burst searches through GWTC-3. Number of shifts ranges from 100 (Babak) to about 10^7 (GW150914).

## (b) Wording

**Use**

- "Estimating the accidental-coincidence background by shifting one data stream in time relative to another is standard in gravitational-wave transient searches (Babak et al. 2013; Usman et al. 2016; Abbott et al. 2016)."
- "The shift must exceed the coincidence window, so that true coincidences cannot survive (Usman et al. 2016, Sec. 2), and the method assumes that noise in different detectors is not correlated at the lags being destroyed (Abbott et al. 2016, Sec. III)."
- "More shifts do not help indefinitely: because every shift reuses the same single-channel event trains, the variance of the estimated false-alarm rate reaches a floor set by each channel's rate times the coincidence window (Was et al. 2010, Eqs. 7 and 10; Eq. 14 for three detectors with at least two coincident). Their derivation assumes stationary Poisson triggers."
- "Search sensitivity is measured by adding simulated signals and counting those recovered above a significance threshold (Usman et al. 2016, Sec. 4.1; GWTC-1 Sec. VII; GWTC-3 Sec. IV E). The threshold is a stated choice: 1 per 100 years in pipeline comparisons, p_astro > 0.5 in GWTC-3, with looser false-alarm thresholds for marginal and subthreshold lists."
- "A real signal in one detector, shifted against noise in another, adds events to the background, so the background is over-estimated and significance is conservative (Abbott et al. 2016, Sec. V). The field's remedy is to remove a confirmed signal's triggers before assessing quieter events (Abbott et al. 2016, PRX 6:041015; called hierarchical removal in GWTC-3 App. D)."
- "Limiting the span of time shifts so the background follows slow changes in the noise has precedent (GWTC-1, Sec. III A), at the cost of fewer independent shifts."

**Avoid**

- "By more than the light-travel time" as the only rule. Sources give several: larger than the coincidence window (Usman), than light travel plus template autocorrelation (GW150914), than glitch duration (Babak).
- "Unbiased" without the condition. GW150914 says unbiased only under uncorrelated noise, and says over-estimated when a loud signal is present.
- "Detection is defined by a false-alarm-rate threshold" as a general statement about the catalogs. GWTC-3 uses p_astro.
- "Convention" as if quoted. The papers describe a purity trade-off; they do not use that word.
- "Inclusive / exclusive background" attributed to Capano et al. Their terms are "all samples" and "coincidence removal".
- Citing Nitz et al. 2017 or GWTC-1 for hierarchical removal. Neither contains it in the text I searched.
- "Time slides preserve the slow common modulation" attributed to GW literature. In GW practice shifts are from 0.1 s up to the whole analysis period; nobody in the sources I opened argues for a short shift on that ground.
- "The removal method is unbiased." Capano: mean-unbiased, but the median under-estimates the false-alarm probability, i.e. it is anti-conservative in most realisations.

## (d) Contradictions and complications

**Claim 4**

- Direction and size of the bias are rank-dependent. In GW data the contamination dominates only the far tail of the background; at the level of a marginal event, removing GW150914 and GW151226 had almost no effect (O1 BBH paper). GWTC-3 says the effect is "particularly muted" at the threshold used for ⟨VT⟩. So the GW literature supports "the bias exists and is conservative", but also "it is often negligible at the working threshold". We should measure it in our setting, not assume it is large.
- Capano et al. do not endorse removal as simply correct. Keeping everything is self-consistent (false positives occur at the stated rate) but over-estimates on average; removing coincidences is mean-unbiased but under-estimates in the typical realisation. They recommend keeping everything when signal rates are unknown. Their study covers the loudest event only.
- The GW mechanism is a signal's single-detector trigger pairing with noise elsewhere. Our case differs: a planted event contributes onsets on many channels at once, and all are scattered, so the inflation may scale differently with the number of channels. The analogy holds qualitatively, not quantitatively.
- For sensitivity estimates specifically, I did not verify how GW pipelines keep injections out of the background they are scored against. From memory, unverified: injection runs are scored against the background of the injection-free data. If so, the closest analogue to best practice is to build our negative from the recording without planted events.

**Claim 5**

- The GW validity condition is independence of noise across detectors. A slow rate modulation shared by channels is correlated noise in that sense. GW practice where noise is shared (H1-H2) is to not use time slides at all, not to shorten them. Our short-shift choice is an adaptation and should be presented as one.
- The follow-up by Was et al. points the other way from our argument: for moderate fluctuations, long shifts are still acceptable. It does not say short shifts are needed.
- A bounded shift (up to 20 s) with event windows of 1 to 2 s gives few effectively independent negatives per recording, and Was et al.'s floor applies on top: repeated negatives from the same recording share the same per-channel trains. Their D-detector formulas (Eqs. 19 and 20) assume stationary Poisson triggers, which bursty channel trains violate, so the floor is a lower bound on our uncertainty at best.
- GW pipelines enforce a minimum relative shift larger than the coincidence window. With an independent random offset per channel, some channel pairs will draw nearly equal offsets and keep their true alignment. With many channels this is a real leak of structure into the negative unless a minimum pairwise offset is imposed.
- GstLAL does not use time slides, so "the field uses time slides" should read "one of two main approaches".
