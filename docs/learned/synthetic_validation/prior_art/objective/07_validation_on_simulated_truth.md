Date of check: 2026-11-13. ## Headline

- Claim 1 holds for spike sorting and seismology, but **not as worded for calcium spike inference**: Theis 2016, spikefinder and CASCADE use REAL simultaneous electrophysiology as ground truth, not simulation, and Theis 2016 states outright that performance on simulated data did not predict performance on real data.
- "The established way" overstates. The sources call simulation an "alternative and complementary" route beside paired recordings. Use "an established way".
- Drawing simulation parameters from the measured data has precedent (Stella 2022; CASCADE for noise and frame rate) but is not the norm in assembly benchmarks, which mostly use a designed default plus one-at-a-time sweeps.
- The best-established term for claim 4 is **applicability domain** (OECD QSAR validation Principle 3; Netzeva et al. 2005).
- Claim 5 has strong, quotable support (Theis 2016; Magland 2020; Rupprecht 2021; Buccino & Einevoll 2021).

## (b) Wording

### Use

- "Scoring a detector against events planted at known times is an established way to validate when the real truth cannot be observed. Spike sorting does it with hybrid recordings, in which known spikes are added to real recordings (Rossant et al. 2016; Pachitariu et al. 2016), and with fully simulated recordings (Buccino & Einevoll 2021; Magland et al. 2020). Assembly and spike-pattern detection do it with planted assemblies (Mölter et al. 2018; Russo & Durstewitz 2017; Quaglio et al. 2018)."
- "Rossant et al. (2016) used the hybrid recordings both to choose detection thresholds and to score the result" (precedent for tuning on planted truth).
- "Taking the simulation's parameters from the recordings follows Stella et al. (2022), who built artificial data with the firing statistics of each experimental dataset under two different generating models. CASCADE applies the same principle to supervised spike inference by resampling ground truth to the noise level and frame rate measured in the target data (Rupprecht et al. 2021)."
- "Most assembly benchmarks instead use a designed default with one-at-a-time sweeps (Mölter et al. 2018); Mölter et al. note that performance 'could depend strongly on the parameters of the data' and that each real experiment 'may occupy a slightly different region of parameter space'."
- "Tuning over a range of simulator settings rather than one setting follows the logic of domain randomization (Tobin et al. 2017). Scoring on settings and on a simulator variant left out of tuning is the simulator analogue of leave-one-domain-out evaluation (Gulrajani & Lopez-Paz 2021)."
- "Results are claimed only inside the applicability domain, the region of measured parameters the simulated pool covered (the term is from QSAR validation: OECD Principle 3; Netzeva et al. 2005). A recording outside it is flagged as untested." Optionally add: "analytical chemistry applies the same rule to a procedure's range (ICH Q2(R2))."
- State the limit plainly: "Agreement on simulated data does not guarantee agreement on real data. Theis et al. (2016) found that performance on simulated calcium data did not predict performance on real data, and Magland et al. (2020) found that simulated spike-sorting benchmarks may not reproduce the firing and noise statistics of real recordings. The simulation tests only the properties it contains."
- For null-only simulated data use "false positives"; for real recordings without truth, follow Stella et al. (2022) and avoid "false positive" (they note one "cannot speak of false positives or false negatives" without ground truth).

### Avoid

- "the established way" or "the standard way" → "an established way". Sources say "alternative and complementary" (Buccino & Einevoll 2021).
- Citing Theis 2016, Berens 2018 or Rupprecht 2021 as "validation by simulation". They use real paired recordings. Cite them for held-out testing, for matching to the target data, and for the limit.
- "CASCADE simulates data matched to the recording". It resamples real ground truth and adds noise; only noise level and frame rate are matched.
- "Assembly benchmarks match their simulations to data". Only Stella 2022 was verified to do so; Mölter, Russo and Lopes-dos-Santos 2011 use designed values.
- "Domain randomization shows that..." as a general result. Tobin et al. tested a hypothesis on one robotic vision task, for training.
- "Gulrajani & Lopez-Paz recommend leave-one-domain-out". They list it as one of three selection methods; their recommendation is that the selection method be specified and that test-domain access be limited.
- "validated range (ICH Q2)" as if it were the defined term, or as covering several conditions at once. ICH defines "range" and "reportable range" over the measured quantity.
- "hybrid ground truth" for our design unless planted events are added to real recordings. Ours is fully simulated; "simulated ground truth" is the accurate label.

## (d) What contradicts or complicates the claims

1. **Calcium spike inference rejected simulation as a benchmark.** Theis et al. 2016 show simulated-data rankings did not predict real-data rankings, and the field's benchmarks since (spikefinder, CASCADE) rest on real paired recordings. A reader from that field may read "validated on simulation" as the weaker option. The honest framing: we use simulation because no paired truth exists for coordinated events, and we name the limit.
2. **Why simulation misled in Theis 2016:** algorithms built on the same generative model as the simulator scored well. Charles et al. 2021 say the same of simple simulations. This is the argument for the held-out simulator variant, and it also means one variant is a thin test: both of our simulators may share assumptions the real data violate.
3. **Simulated data may be optimistic about false positives specifically.** Magland et al. 2020: precision is below recall on real paired data but often above it on synthetic data. Our headline risk is false coordinated events, so the simulation may understate it.
4. **Matching to data is partial in every precedent.** CASCADE matches two properties; Stella 2022 matches single-train statistics for null data only. Neither planted events at data-derived participation, jitter and spacing. That part of our design (planting events whose parameters are themselves estimated from detections on real data) has a circularity the precedents do not face: the measured parameters depend on a detector. No source examined addresses this.
5. **Designed grids are the norm in assembly benchmarks**, and they cover a wider range than any one dataset. A pool drawn only from what was measured is narrower. That is what the untested flag is for, but it means the precedent supports sweeping beyond the measured range as well.
6. **Domain randomization and domain generalization are training-side literatures.** They support the shape of the design (range over point; held-out domains; declared selection rule) but neither validates a detector's error rates. Gulrajani & Lopez-Paz's main finding is cautionary: elaborate generalisation methods did not beat the plain baseline once selection was done fairly.
7. **Seismology support is thinner than the brief assumes.** One paper read in full (CRED) uses a semi-synthetic test; FAST's synthetic test is in an unread supplement. No source was found that calls injection tests the standard there. From memory, unverified: "injection" and "injection-recovery" are the standard terms in gravitational-wave searches and exoplanet transit surveys, which may be better precedents; none was checked.
8. **Applicability domain has no single agreed method.** Sahigara et al. 2012 compare several ways of drawing it and they disagree on which queries are inside. Our rule (inside the range the pool covered) is the simplest, range-based form; say which form is used.
9. Bibliographic notes: the Kilosort 2016 paper's title is "Fast and accurate spike sorting of high-channel count probes with KiloSort" (NIPS 29); the bioRxiv preprint has a different title. The MEArec journal year is 2021 (online 2020). "Stella et al. 2019" is 3d-SPADE (Biosystems); "Stella et al. 2022" is the surrogate comparison (eNeuro); they are different papers with different relevance. The Lopes-dos-Santos paper in the local library is the 2011 PLoS ONE paper, not the 2013 one.
