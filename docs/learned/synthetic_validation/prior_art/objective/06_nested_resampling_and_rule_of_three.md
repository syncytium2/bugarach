# Literature verification 06: resampling nested data (A) and the rule of three (B)

Date of check: 2026-11-13. Every row states the access level actually achieved. Anything not opened is labelled. PubMed/PMC was used to retrieve several full texts; DOIs are given as links.

Access-level vocabulary: **full text opened** / **abstract only** / **preview only** / **could not access**. "From memory, unverified" marks statements I did not confirm in a source during this check.

## Bottom line

- **A1 supported.** Three neuroscience primary sources opened in full text say it directly, with numbers.
- **A2 supported, but the sources do not agree on the within-animal step.** "Resample animals first" is common to all. Whether to then resample *within* animals with replacement (Saravanan) or not (Ren et al.; the plain cluster bootstrap) differs by source. Our text must say which we do.
- **A3 is the weak point.** No source opened supports a trustworthy 95% interval from 3 animals, and several say the opposite for 5 to 12 clusters. With 3 to 7 animals the cluster bootstrap should be described as a way to stop under-stating uncertainty, not as a calibrated interval.
- **A4 partly supported.** The procedure is not a "double bootstrap" and is not a textbook "parametric bootstrap". Closest established name: bootstrap predictive simulation (bootstrap analogue of posterior predictive simulation), i.e. propagating parameter uncertainty. The standard references for this were mostly not opened (books; one journal page would not load).
- **B1 supported for the arithmetic and the name; primary full texts not opened.** 3/T is the ONE-SIDED 95% bound. The two-sided exact bound is 3.69/T.

## (b) Wording to use and wording to avoid

**A1. Use:** "Recordings from the same animal are not independent. Treating them as independent under-states uncertainty: intervals are too narrow and false-positive rates exceed the nominal level (Lazic 2010; Aarts et al. 2014; Galbraith et al. 2010)."
**Avoid:** "53% false positives" (53% is the share of papers with nested data). "80% false positives" without "up to". "Always inflates" (Galbraith: within-cluster comparisons become conservative).

**A2. Use:** "We use a cluster (hierarchical) bootstrap: animals are resampled with replacement and [each selected animal's recordings are kept intact / recordings are then resampled with replacement within animal], following [Ren et al. 2010; Field and Welsh 2007 / Saravanan et al. 2020]." State the within-animal rule explicitly and cite the source that matches it.
**Avoid:** "the hierarchical bootstrap resamples at the highest level first, then within" as if that were one agreed recipe. Citing Ren et al. for a scheme that resamples within animals with replacement (they recommend the opposite). Citing Davison and Hinkley 3.8 for a specific rule until someone reads it. Calling it our method.

**A3. Use:** "With 7 animals, and 3 in the smallest group, the cluster bootstrap cannot deliver a calibrated 95% interval. Simulation studies find nominal-95% cluster-bootstrap intervals covering roughly 85 to 94% with 5 to 12 clusters (Cameron et al. 2008; Flynn and Peters 2004), and the percentile interval is known to be too narrow at small n (Hesterberg 2015). We therefore report these as resampling ranges that respect the nesting, which are wider and more honest than the recording-level bootstrap, and likely still too narrow. With 3 animals the range mostly reflects which animals were drawn." Report the number of animals beside every interval, and show the per-animal values.
**Avoid:** "95% confidence interval" for the 3-mouse group without qualification. "The hierarchical bootstrap corrects for nesting" (it removes one error and leaves the small-sample one). "Saravanan et al. show the bootstrap is unbiased" without adding that this was shown with 1000 subjects and that they report elevated false positives at very small N. Also do not adopt Saravanan's reading of the bootstrap proportion as "the probability the hypothesis is true"; that is their interpretation, not a standard one.

**A4. Use:** "Parameter uncertainty is propagated into the simulations: for each bootstrap resample of animals the full parameter vector is re-estimated and one recording is simulated from it. This is a bootstrap predictive simulation (Harris 1989; Fushiki et al. 2005), the resampling analogue of posterior predictive simulation." Re-estimating the whole vector per resample keeps the dependence between parameters; say so.
**Avoid:** "double bootstrap" (means something else, twice over). "parametric bootstrap" as the sole label (that is simulation from the single fitted vector). "posterior predictive" without "analogue of" (no prior, no posterior). The simulated set inherits A3's limits: with 3 animals it spans only the parameter vectors those 3 animals can produce.

**B1. Use:** "With zero false calls in T hours, and treating false calls as a Poisson process, the one-sided 95% upper confidence bound on the false-call rate is -ln(0.05)/T, about 3.0/T (the 'rule of three'; Hanley and Lippman-Hand 1983; Jovanovic and Levy 1997). The upper end of the two-sided exact 95% interval is 3.7/T."
**Avoid:** "the rate is below 3/T" (it is a bound at 95% confidence, not a fact). "95% confidence interval" without "one-sided". Applying it when T pools recordings whose false calls cluster by animal or by artifact episode: the Poisson assumption needs independent events at a constant rate, and pooled hours from few animals are the same nesting problem as A1. The binomial 3/n approximation is quoted for n > 30 (Eypasch); the Poisson-exposure form has no such condition but does need the Poisson assumption.

## (d) What contradicts or complicates the claims

1. **A3 is not supported for 3 animals, and only weakly for 7.** Every source that tested a cluster bootstrap at 5 to 12 clusters found it anti-conservative (Cameron et al. 2008 Table 2: 15% error at G = 5 for nominal 5%; Flynn and Peters 2004: 86 to 94% coverage, "24 clusters per arm" as a minimum). No source tested 3. My own arithmetic, not from a source: with 3 animals there are only 10 distinct resamples, and 1 in 9 resamples contains a single animal three times. Extending Hesterberg's Table 5 formula (for a mean of roughly normal animal-level values, an idealisation), a nominal 95% percentile interval would cover about 75% at n = 3 and about 88% at n = 7. Treat those two figures as rough, unverified estimates.

2. **The fixes in the econometrics literature do not transfer directly.** The wild cluster bootstrap-t that rescues few-cluster inference in Cameron et al. is a test of a regression coefficient with a studentized statistic. It is not an interval for a rate, participation or jitter parameter, and it does not generate parameter sets for simulation.

3. **Sources disagree on the within-animal step** (table under A2). Resampling within animals with replacement adds within-animal sampling noise on top of between-animal noise; Flynn and Peters note that a naive two-stage scheme double-counts within-cluster variance and needs a shrinkage correction. Keeping animals intact avoids that. With 1 to 2 recordings per animal (12 from 7; 5 from 3) within-animal resampling has almost nothing to resample, so the intact-cluster version is the natural choice here; this is my inference, not a source statement.

4. **The alternative the neuroscience sources actually recommend is a mixed model** (Aarts, Lazic, Galbraith), not a bootstrap. Saravanan et al. report LMM had fewer false positives than the bootstrap at small N. A reader may ask why we did not use one; the answer (non-normal parameters, need for simulated parameter sets) should be stated. Saravanan also note LMMs can be biased with few clusters, so neither route is clean.

5. **Saravanan et al. is in a small journal and makes some non-standard statements** (bootstrap proportion as the probability a hypothesis is true; "the bootstrap cannot be used on a metric that does not obey the law of large numbers"). Cite it for the neuroscience application and the with-replacement recipe; cite statistics sources for properties.

6. **Unequal recordings per animal.** Flynn and Peters flag that with unequal cluster sizes each cluster resample has a different composition and total size, which can distort variability; Saravanan 2.3 says the weighting choice should be made deliberately. Our animals contribute unequal numbers of recordings, so this applies.

7. **Rule of three depends on the Poisson assumption and on sidedness.** 3.0/T is one-sided; a reader expecting a two-sided 95% interval would compute 3.7/T. Statpages argues that choosing one-sided after observing zero is not a valid exact procedure; if the one-sided bound was the plan in advance, say so. If false calls come in bursts, the effective exposure is smaller than T and the true bound is higher.
