Two draws of the learned-versus-coded comparison

Can a lead of about a hundredth of F1 (a detection score from 0 to 1) between a learned detector and a hand-written one be told apart from the luck of which simulated recordings were drawn? This report reruns the project's comparison of four learned detectors with six hand-written ones on recordings the first run never saw. Each run is a draw: one full run of the comparison on its own set of simulated recordings. Simulated recordings only, untreated activity only, one of the lab's two event streams (section 10).

Why this report exists. A rehearsal of this comparison put two learned models slightly ahead of the reference hand-written detector under its false-alarm budget. Whether a lead that small is real depends on how far a result moves when nothing but the recordings changes, and one run cannot measure that, so the comparison was run a second time. It feeds the project's decision about which detectors to keep, which the project says is not yet final. Sections 1–5 explain the comparison itself, so that the rest can be read cold; section 6 onward is what the second draw adds.

The answer, in plain words.
  • Changing only the recordings moves a score by about a hundredth. F1 balances events found against false alarms. On new recordings, with the models' settings and training seeds held fixed, the learned models' scores moved by a median of 0.010 and the hand-written detectors' by 0.003 (section 9); a rerun that also changed those would likely move them further. The rehearsal's lead was about that size, so it could not have been told from luck.
  • As the comparison was run, every learned model trailed the reference detector, CoactDetect, in every held-out quarter of the recordings in both draws, when all were held to one shared cap on false alarms (the budget, section 5). The closest, chorus_gain_norm, trailed on average by two to three times the typical move (section 9).
  • Part of that shortfall is one setting. Every detector joins calls that fall close together into one call. The learned models joined calls within 2 s, CoactDetect within 8 s. With both sides joining at the same gap, chorus_gain_norm still trailed under the cap, by 43%–72% of its as-run gap. Judged on F1 alone, the two chorus models (learned models that pool a vote from every imaged cell, section 3) came out at or slightly above CoactDetect where their training worked, by less than the typical move and without a test that separates it from luck (section 9).
  • Training often failed for the two chorus models, and the failures pull their averages down (section 8).
  • What this does not show. Anything about drugs, the lab's second event stream, or real recordings. The simulator was fitted to a data folder with a known defect, and whether to refit it is the project lead's open decision (section 10).

1. The problem: finding coordinated events

A calcium-imaging recording of a brain slice is turned into a raster: one row per imaged cell, called a region of interest (ROI), and one mark per calcium event, at the moment the event rose. A coordinated event is several ROIs' events rising together, within a fraction of a second, more often than their own firing rates would explain. Finding them is the job of every detector here.

It is hard for three reasons, and Figure 1, a simulated recording, shows two of them plainly. The cells fire on their own too, at rates that differ from cell to cell and drift over minutes, so chance coincidences are common. Some stretches are dense — many cells active, none of it coordinated — which a detector that watches the overall rate will mistake for events. And an event recruits only a few of the imaged cells, as few as 3 here, which at this scale is a thin column of marks that the answer key has to point to.

2. Why a simulation, and what it simulates

On a real recording nobody knows which coordinated events happened, so a detector can only be compared with another detector, which measures agreement, not truth. A simulation plants the events and keeps the answer key. Every recording here comes from the project's bench, a simulator whose background activity was fitted to the untreated stretch of real recordings, before any drug — the baseline. The lab's data come as two event streams, a fast and a slow one, for which the export records an event's duration by different rules, and which respond to drugs differently; this work uses only the fast stream.

What one bench recording holds.

  • 33 ROIs over 2700 s (45 minutes), at a quiet or a busy background: 0.0052 or 0.019 events per second per ROI, the 25th and 75th percentiles of real baseline rates.
  • 15 planted coordinated events, at least 120 s apart: 5 at each of three sizes, recruiting 30%, 18% and 10% of the ROIs (10, 6 and 3 cells), each cell's onset scattered by 0.36 s (standard deviation).
  • 6 distractors: bursts built exactly like a planted 18% event (6 cells, the same scatter), placed without regard to the planted events; a call on one counts as a false alarm.
  • A dense stretch of 300 s, from minute 20 to minute 25, in which every cell fires faster and nothing is planted (the project's glossary calls it the promiscuity probe).
  • For every recording seed, an empty recording: the same background with nothing planted, at seed + 100,000.

Words used from here on. A detector's call is one claimed event, with an onset and an end. A call matches a planted event when the event lies within 2.5 s of the call, one call to one event (the project's standard tolerance, score.py). Every detector keeps calls at least some gap apart, usually by joining calls closer than that gap into a single call (locust instead drops the lower of two nearby calls); that gap is its merge. The reference is CoactDetect at the settings of section 3; it anchors the false-alarm budget of section 5.

A detector's score is its F1, the harmonic mean of recall (the share of planted events it matched) and precision (the share of its calls that matched one). Calls in the dense stretch are left out of precision and policed separately, by the budget. Hits and calls are summed over the recordings scored together at each background, and the two backgrounds' F1 scores are averaged. Because distractors look exactly like 6-cell events, a detector that finds every planted event and calls every distractor separately scores precision 15/21 and F1 0.83. A merge long enough to join a distractor to a nearby planted event hides that distractor inside a hit, as in Figure 1, the simulated recording, so a long merge can score above 0.83; it is a practical ceiling, not a hard one.

3. The contestants

Six hand-written ("coded") detectors, each a rule with its own settings:

  • rate+context compares the population's event rate with the rate in a longer window around the same moment.
  • CoactDetect and LoCo (Local Coincidence) count how many distinct cells are active in a short window, and compare that count with a null: the same count when each cell's events are circularly shifted in time — moved by the cell's own offset within a context window around the test window, wrapping at its ends — which keeps every cell's rate and pattern and breaks only the timing between cells. The sliding versions used here compute that null exactly rather than sampling shifted copies.
  • binned SCE (synchronous calcium events) makes the same count in fixed time bins.
  • locust counts cells co-active across a few frames against one cell-count threshold built from circularly shifted copies.
  • SPIKE-synch measures how tightly event times align across cells and calls where that alignment is high.

Three of the six descend from published methods and three were designed here; the origins are recorded in docs/detector_history.md. Binned SCE descends from Cossart, Aronov and Yuste (2003), from Rafael Yuste's laboratory, who tested counts of coactive cells against surrogates with each cell's intervals reshuffled; the circular-shift null is the form the Cossart lab later adopted in its own laboratory (Dard and colleagues, 2022), in its CICADA software, through which it reached this project. locust is a modified port of CICADA. SPIKE-synch applies a threshold to the SPIKE-synchronization measure of Kreuz, Mulansky and Bozanic (2015); the Kreuz lab introduced threshold detection on that measure itself (Kreuz and colleagues, 2017; applied by Cecchini and colleagues, 2021), so this is an implementation, not a new method. rate+context, CoactDetect and LoCo were designed here; by the author's account they resemble radar constant-false-alarm-rate detection (Finn and Johnson, 1968) without having been derived from it.

The reference, sliding CoactDetect. CoactDetect counts distinct cells in a 2 s window that slides with the data and calls an event where the count is improbably high: a z-score using the null's exact mean and standard deviation over a 120 s context, at a nominal significance level α = 10⁻⁵ (the tail is treated as Gaussian, so the true rate of chance calls is higher). A 1 s band at the center of the counting window is left out of the context, so the core of an event does not raise its own threshold (its edges can). Calls within 8 s are joined. Goal 1 of the project, which tuned the coded detectors, chose these values on bench seeds 1–48 and checked them on seeds 49–96, none of which is used here. LoCo and CoactDetect were first written with fixed time bins; on three real baseline recordings, shifting a recording by a fraction of a second lost 30–36% of their calls, so both now slide (sources in section 11).

Four learned models ("nets"): small neural networks trained on bench recordings to give a probability of a coordinated event at every 0.1 s frame (Figure 2, the four architectures). Where that probability crosses a threshold chosen in training, the net calls an event, and its calls are joined within a fixed 2 s; each coded detector instead tunes its own merge.

  • chorus_norm passes every cell through one shared encoder, standardizes each cell's encoder output over time ("norm"), turns it into a bounded vote, and pools the votes of the whole "chorus" of cells into three statistics: their mean, their spread, and the mean of the loudest few. The shape — one encoder shared by every element, then a pool that ignores their order — is the construction formalized as Deep Sets (Zaheer and colleagues, 2017) and reached independently by PointNet (Qi and colleagues, 2017); the three statistics are this project's choice. (1,897 parameters in its default configuration.)
  • chorus_gain_norm is chorus_norm with a fitted steepness ("gain") and midpoint on each vote. (1,905 parameters.)
  • line_length measures, at each moment, the share of imaged cells lit — the project's "relative length" of the field, not the signal-processing line-length feature; each cell's vote is bounded in height, though a cell that bursts holds it for longer. (1,233 parameters.)
  • tube widens each cell's onsets to about a second, averages them over cells, and compares that against its own surround in time with a center-minus-surround filter. It does not count distinct cells: two bursting cells can score like four distinct ones. It is the project's control for tuning inflation: untuned, it tied CoactDetect in an earlier comparison on the retired simulator, so if tuning lifts it clear too, the gains are about the amount of tuning, not the architecture. (1,149 parameters.)

Each net has 24 configurations: 23 drawn at random from its hyperparameters (learning rate, training length and its own shape parameters) plus the untuned default, the same 24 in both draws.

These are the models their names claim. Built through the project's model registry, as the trainer builds them, chorus_norm and chorus_gain_norm carry their registered settings. Built by calling their bare builder functions, as a figure script or a notebook might, both silently build plain chorus, and nothing complains. The runs went through the registry: both declarations give chorus_gain_norm a search over its vote's steepness and chorus_norm none, and the fitted checkpoints differ by the 8 parameters that steepness and midpoint add. The same trap caught the project's own drawing check, which passed on the wrong model: a check that runs correctly on the wrong subject is a different failure from a check that cannot fire.

4. One run: nested cross-validation

A score is honest only if nothing about the recordings it is measured on was used to choose what is being measured. Each run therefore uses nested cross-validation (CV) (Figure 3, the nested cross-validation). Its 48 recording seeds are split into 4 outer folds of 12 (24 recordings: each seed at both backgrounds). Each fold in turn is held out; everything — a net's configuration and threshold, a coded detector's settings, the false-alarm budget — is chosen on the other three, then scored once on the held-out fold. Folds are numbered 0–3, as in the runs' files (WSMIP064's own report numbers them 1–4).

A net's configurations are compared by inner fits. Each inner fit trains on two folds and is scored on the other two; for any one held-out fold that leaves three pairs of training folds (Figure 3, the nested cross-validation), and across the draw there are 6, so a net has 432 inner fits per draw (24 configurations × 3 training repeats × 6 pairs). The best pooled inner F1 wins. The winner, the winner under the false-alarm budget (section 5) and the untuned configuration are each refitted at 5 training repeats and scored on the held-out fold (40–60 refits per net per draw; fewer when two of the three pick the same configuration). A net's score in a fold is the mean of its refits' F1. Every net fit trains on 10 recordings drawn from its training folds and picks its threshold on 2 more. A coded detector is tuned by a coordinate search on all 72 training recordings.

A defect was fixed before either run started. In the rehearsal, the nets for two different held-out folds trained on the same ten recordings (docs/todo/2026-09-17-two-bake-off-folds-train-the-same-model.md). Recordings are now dealt round-robin across the training folds, and a check refuses to start a run unless every outer fold trains its own models on its own recordings. Both runs passed it.

5. Two selections and one shared budget

Each detector and net is chosen two ways, and each detector–selection pair is an entry (the nets also have an untuned entry). Chosen on F1 alone takes the best score on the training folds. But F1 leaves the dense stretch out of precision, and a detector that fires there, or on an empty recording, is not usable. So each is also chosen under the budget: the best F1 among candidates whose calls per hour stay under three ceilings shared by every entry (Figure 4, the shared budget). The three ceilings are calls per hour in the dense stretch at each background, and on the quiet empty recording. Each is the reference's own rate on the fold's training recordings times 1.6, a margin carried over from the bench's calibration of the binned CoactDetect (source in section 11). The budget is therefore anchored to CoactDetect, and its reference settings are admissible by construction. A net's candidate is a configuration and a threshold, and under the budget the threshold chosen on the inner fits is carried onto all 5 refits.

What the budget does not police. Only the quiet empty recording is gated; the busy one is only reported. Binned SCE fires 21–33 times per hour on the busy empty recording (CoactDetect 2–6 calls per hour) and passes. And the dense-stretch ceiling counts calls, not time spent calling: a detector whose long merge joins a whole dense stretch into one call is charged once.

6. Why two draws

The two runs' settings files differ in exactly two fields, the recording seeds and the draw number, and by the branch history their code differs only in the option that sets the draw (Figure 5, the two draws): WSMIP064 ran the first draw and WSMIP065 the second. The rehearsal before them differed in five ways: a simulator since retired, the coded side tuned on only three of its settings, an older budget, the fold defect of section 4, and no limit on CoactDetect's context window, which reached 240 s in some folds — wider than the planted spacing, so its null contained the events it was testing. Its notes say not to quote it as a comparison (source in section 11). It is quoted here only for the size of the margin that prompted this rerun: under its budget chorus_norm led CoactDetect by +0.011 F1 and chorus_gain_norm by +0.016.

8. What the runs carry besides the models' skill

Training that failed

Inner selection scores each configuration at 3 training repeats, so a configuration that fails at some of them can still win. In the second draw's fold 1, chorus_norm's winner on F1 alone made one call per recording at 2 of its 5 refits (Figure 7, the collapse): at a low threshold the whole recording became one call, which matches one of the 15 planted events, so F1 is exactly 0.125. That is consistent with an output that barely varies with the input; the output itself is not examined here. Under the budget the threshold sits above everything those refits output, so they call nothing. The fold's mean fell to 0.501, against 0.745 untuned. It is not rare in this search (Table 2): about a third of chorus_norm's inner fits did it and a sixth of chorus_gain_norm's. Across the two draws, 2 refits of chorus_gain_norm did the same, and under the budget 3 refits called nothing at all (1 of tube's in the first draw, fold 0; 2 of chorus_norm's in the second draw, fold 1). The two draws share their configurations and training seeds, so the same fits largely fail in both (111 of chorus_norm's are the same fit): Table 2 shows where training fails in this search, not two independent measurements of how often.

Searches that found no admissible setting

Under the budget, locust's search found no admissible setting among those it reached, in any fold of either draw: its starting point fires 449–527 times per hour in the dense stretch at the quiet background, against ceilings of 15–18 per hour, and every setting reachable by changing one setting across its whole grid was also over. Binned SCE did the same in 1 fold (the second draw, fold 2). Those table entries carry ‡ and are not results.

Whether the held-out folds kept to the budget

The budget is enforced on the training recordings. On the held-out recordings the run checked each choice again (Table 3). Every net had refits go over — tube most, in 11 of 20 refits in the first draw — against CoactDetect's 1 of 8 folds (the first draw, fold 2), and the ‡ entries went over as their starting points do. For the nets two causes are possible and neither was measured: a threshold chosen on inner fits and carried onto refits whose output is scaled differently, and chance, since a held-out fold holds only an hour of dense stretch per background; for CoactDetect, only chance. A choice over its ceiling was not held to the budget on those recordings; how that moved its F1 was not measured.

Settings at the top of their grid, and the crowded-recording check

A longer merge scores better on this bench: CoactDetect's own F1 rises from 0.733 at 2 s to 0.748 at 8 s and 0.797 at 30 s in the second draw, partly because a long merge hides distractors inside hits (section 2). Inside a fold the grids are fixed, so a setting that wanted to go further stops at the top, and the merge sat at the top of its grid in every fold for all six coded detectors (Table 4). CoactDetect and LoCo started there, at goal 1's 8 s; the others moved there.

Goal 1 guards against exactly this with a fourth check this search did not apply: a setting may not lose more than 0.02 F1, on goal 1's crowded recordings (events as little as 6 s apart), against the setting it replaces. WSMIP064's tool applied it afterwards to every coded choice of the first draw, and the same tool, unchanged, to the second. It refuses rate+context under the budget, LoCo under the budget, binned SCE on F1 alone and SPIKE-synch under the budget in every fold of both draws; and binned SCE under the budget in 3 of 4 folds of the first draw. CoactDetect passes in every fold of both draws, so the comparison of the nets with it is untouched; the nets' merge is fixed, so the check does not apply to them. Those entries carry § and are left out of the between-draw spread of section 9. Binned SCE's first-draw lead on F1 alone is one of them.

9. What the pair of draws can claim

How far results move between draws

Figure 8, the between-draw moves shows each entry's second-draw mean minus its first. Among entries with no flagged fold, the nets moved a median of 0.010 F1 in absolute value (at most 0.027) and the coded detectors 0.003 (at most 0.011); CoactDetect's two selections count once. That is the scale any lead has to beat. It was measured with the 24 configurations and the training seeds held fixed, so it is the move from changing the recordings alone, and probably understates how far a full rerun can move a result.

The rehearsal's leads (+0.011, +0.016) are about that size. The rehearsal ran six seeds per fold where these runs ran 12, so its own luck was, if anything, larger: a single run could not have told its lead from the luck of the recordings. With five things changed at once (section 6), the two draws cannot say which of them turned that lead around.

The moves are not independent noise either: 7 of 8 unflagged net entries rose and 4 of 5 unflagged coded entries did not. The nets' mean signed move minus the coded detectors' is +0.015: the second draw favors the nets by about that much.

The headline gap, as run

Figure 9, every net against CoactDetect shows each net minus CoactDetect in every outer fold of both draws. Under the budget (panel B) every net sits below zero in every fold. chorus_gain_norm, the closest on its mean over all four folds, trails by 0.030 F1 in the first draw (standard deviation over folds 0.005) and 0.025 in the second (0.018): on average two to three times the typical between-draw move, though single folds trail by less. A paired t-test over the four folds, which the run computed, gives t = −13.0 (p = 0.001) in the first draw and t = −2.8 (p = 0.07) in the second, on 3 degrees of freedom; the folds share training data, so both p-values are optimistic (Dietterich, 1998; Bengio and Grandvalet, 2004). The gap itself moved 0.005 between draws, less than the typical move, which is what agreement should look like but not proof of it.

Chosen on F1 alone (panel A), the best net's mean also trailed (−0.007 for chorus_norm in the first draw, −0.029 for chorus_gain_norm in the second), but those means carry the failed refits of section 8. In the folds where their training worked, chorus_norm and chorus_gain_norm were within 0.029 of CoactDetect, on either side, as run. Untuned, chorus_norm was −0.012 against CoactDetect in the first draw and +0.006 in the second.

At a matched merge

The nets joined their calls within 2 s and CoactDetect within 8 s, and a longer merge scores better on this bench (section 8). So every net's held-out output and CoactDetect's were decoded again with both sides at the same merge — 2, 4, 8 and 16 s, the grid of WSMIP064's tool — changing nothing else (Figure 10, the matched merge, Table 5). No threshold or setting was re-chosen. A longer merge can only lower a detector's call counts, so the nets' budgeted choices at 4–16 s and CoactDetect's at 16 s stay within their ceilings; at 2 and 4 s CoactDetect's merge is shorter than the 8 s it was chosen at, so it calls more there, and its budget was not rechecked at those merges. Each re-decode at the run's own merge reproduced the run's F1: exactly, except 6 refits, off by at most 0.0015, all at thresholds near the top of the grid (probably the precision of re-running the model; not investigated).

Under the budget, part of chorus_gain_norm's gap is the merge, and the rest survives. With both sides at the same merge it trails by 0.014, 0.015, 0.017, 0.017 in the first draw and 0.011, 0.013, 0.015, 0.018 in the second (at 2, 4, 8 and 16 s), against 0.030 and 0.025 as run: 43%–72% of the gap remains, and at 8 s it is still below zero in all 8 folds.

On F1 alone, the two chorus models come out at or slightly above CoactDetect. Over the folds where they trained, their mean is above CoactDetect's at every matched merge, by 0.005–0.022, and at 8 s they are ahead in 11 of those 13 folds. That is within the typical move, and it is not established: the same paired test at 8 s passes p < 0.05 in 1 of the 4 model-by-draw cases, and with their failed folds counted — a user of these models gets the failures too — the mean at 8 s is below CoactDetect's in 3 of the 4. line_length and tube trail at every merge in every fold.

The matched merge is not the better comparison, only the other one: at 8 s CoactDetect runs at settings tuned for 8 s and the nets at thresholds chosen for 2 s, and at 2 s the reverse. The first cost is visible: re-decoded at 8 s, one line_length fold on F1 alone lost 0.107 F1 (the line running off the left of Figure 10, the matched merge).

Where the as-run gap is

It is in precision, and in the smallest events. Pooled, chorus_gain_norm found at least as many planted events as CoactDetect under the budget (recall 0.90 against 0.86 in the first draw, 0.85 against 0.84 in the second), but that hides where (Table 6). On the two larger event sizes both find nearly every event. On 3-cell events at the busy background CoactDetect finds only 0.35 and 0.28 of them (first and second draw), chorus_gain_norm 0.64 and 0.52; at the quiet background the two are closer, and chorus_gain_norm finds fewer. Its precision is lower at both backgrounds.

In Figure 11, where the false alarms fall, chorus_gain_norm makes fewer than CoactDetect in the dense stretch and more outside it (9.1 against 6.4 per recording in the first draw, 7.6 against 6.0 in the second). line_length shows the same pattern in both draws, chorus_norm in the first only; tube instead calls more than CoactDetect in the dense stretch. The score files do not record which of the false alarms outside the dense stretch fell on distractors.

Tuning

Tuning on F1 alone raised line_length over its untuned default by 0.032 and 0.029 F1, the same direction in both draws (paired tests over the folds, p = 0.14 and 0.04); consistent, small, and not firmly established. tube, the control for tuning inflation, moved by +0.002 and −0.005, less than the typical move: tuning did not lift it. For chorus_norm the change was +0.005 and −0.063; the second is the failed training of section 8, so tuning's effect on chorus_norm cannot be separated from it.

10. Limits a reader must be told

  • Baseline only, fast stream only. By the project's decision (docs/goals/README.md), these runs use only the untreated part of the recordings and only the fast stream. Nothing here says how any detector behaves under a drug, or on the slow stream, whose event rate under tetrodotoxin (TTX, a sodium channel blocker applied to the slice) rises while the fast stream's falls (docs/FOUNDATIONS.md §9).
  • The simulator was fitted to a folder with a known defect. The bench's constants were measured on the steps_excluded export (the lab's data folder), in which motion correction pinned 12 ROIs in 4 recordings to the frame's floor value, about 0.03% of the folder's events. Measured again on the corrected export, on both workstations, seven of the bench's eight fitted rates and shares were unchanged to four decimals or moved far inside their bootstrap intervals; the share of ROIs a 6-cell event recruits sits just outside its interval on both exports, because the bench stores 0.18 (6 of 33) where the data say 0.19. Whether that settles it, and whether to point the bench at the corrected export, is the project lead's open decision; these results are conditional on it (sources in section 11).
  • The two sides were not treated alike (Table 7). The merge is the difference measured above; the others were not varied.
  • Settings fixed before the runs and never varied: the budget's margin of 1.6, the 2.5 s tolerance, 10 training and 2 threshold recordings per fit, 3 and 5 training repeats, and the coordinate search's minimum gain of 0.002. How the headline depends on any of them is untested.
  • What the budget does not police (section 5): the busy empty recording, and time spent calling in the dense stretch.
  • The crowded-recording check came after the run (section 8). The search could choose settings the check refuses, and did; the § entries are reported, not results.
  • Recall by event size is shown only for the headline pair (Table 6); every other number pools the three sizes.
  • Simulation only. Every result is on simulated recordings; the one real-recording number quoted (30–36%, section 3) comes from another document.
