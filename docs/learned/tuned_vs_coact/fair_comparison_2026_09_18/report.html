Do the learned detectors beat the hand-written ones once both are tuned fairly?

Goal 2 of the current program, the fair comparison, run on the workstation WSMIP064 from 2026-09-18 at 16:14 to 2026-09-19 at 05:58. Simulated recordings only, the fast stream, baseline conditions only (section 8). Written for a reader who has not followed this project.

The question. This project detects coordinated events — moments when several cells fire together — in calcium-imaging recordings. It has six hand-written ("coded") detectors and a family of neural networks ("nets") trained to do the same job. An earlier comparison had the nets ahead, but the nets had been run at one untuned setting and the coded detectors tuned on a single knob each, so the margin could have been about who got tuned, not about which approach is better. This run tunes both sides with the same care, chooses every setting without looking at the recordings it is scored on, and asks again.

The answer, in words (the numbers are in section 6): once both sides are tuned, no net is ahead of CoactDetect, the coded detector the budget is anchored to. The one coded detector that finishes ahead of CoactDetect does so with a setting that goal 1 of this program has already ruled out, for a reason explained in section 7.

Abbreviations used throughout: ROI, region of interest (one imaged cell); F1, the harmonic mean of recall (the share of planted events found) and precision (the share of calls that were real), from 0 to 1; CV, cross-validation; GPU, graphics processor; t, the paired t statistic across the four outer folds, on 3 degrees of freedom.

1. The problem: finding coordinated events

Each row of a raster is one ROI; each tick is a moment the cell fired. A coordinated event is several ROIs firing within a fraction of a second of one another, more often than chance would put them together. Two things make that hard. The cells fire at very different rates, so a busy cell lands near others by accident all the time. And the whole field sometimes becomes busier for minutes at a stretch, which raises chance coincidences everywhere without anything coordinated happening. A detector has to find the few real events without firing on either. Figure 1, one simulated recording, shows all three things a detector meets: a planted event (marked in the lane above the raster), two distractors — bursts of correlated firing that are not coordinated events and must not be called — and the probe stretch, five minutes in which every ROI fires at 0.06 Hz, six times the quiet background rate, with nothing planted in it.

2. Why a simulation, and what it simulates

A real recording has no answer key: nobody knows for certain which moments were coordinated, so F1 cannot be computed on it. The project's bench is a simulator whose parameters are measured from real baseline recordings, so its recordings look like real ones, but every planted event is known. Each bench recording is 45 minutes of 33 ROIs holding 15 planted events, 5 at each of three participation levels (30%, 18% and 10% of the ROIs), each event's onsets spread by 0.36 s, with events at least 120 s apart. It also holds 6 distractors and the probe stretch.

Every recording seed is simulated at two backgrounds, the quiet and busy ends of the range real baseline recordings span: 0.0052 Hz and 0.019 Hz per ROI. A detector's score is its F1 pooled within each background, then averaged over the two, so doing well at only one background is not enough. A call counts as a hit if it lands within 2.5 s of a planted event; calls inside the probe stretch are counted separately (as false alarms per hour) and kept out of precision, so the probe measures "fires on busy but uncoordinated activity" without swamping every other number.

This run used 96 recordings: 48 recording seeds (1000–1047), each at both backgrounds, plus one recording per seed with nothing planted at each background, used to count false alarms.

Ten detectors, four learned and six coded, listed in Table 1. The coded detectors are the project's ports of established methods. The four nets are the ones that led the earlier untuned comparison, plus tube, kept as a control: it tied CoactDetect untuned, so if tuning lifted it far ahead too, the leaders' margins would be about tuning rather than design. 4. How the comparison was kept fair

4.1 Nested cross-validation: choose on some recordings, score on others

If a setting is chosen by looking at the recordings it is then scored on, the score flatters it. Nested cross-validation prevents that, and Figure 3, the nested layout, shows it. The 48 recording seeds are split into 4 outer folds of 12. Each fold takes one turn being held out. With it set aside, every candidate setting is judged on the other three folds alone, by rotating which of those three is scored while the other two train (the inner loop). The winner is then retrained on all three training folds and scored once on the held-out fold. The held-out fold is never seen while choosing.

Both sides went through exactly this. Each net had 24 configurations, drawn at random, before the run started, from a grid over its learning rate, its number of training steps (lr: 0.003, 0.01, 0.03, steps: 900, 1800, 3600) and 3 or 4 settings of its own design, with the untuned setting always among them. Each inner candidate was fitted at 3 training seeds, and each chosen configuration refitted at 5. Each coded detector's search walked every setting in goal 1's declared grids (Table 1), one setting at a time, using the same inner loop.

4.2 A defect fixed before the run: two outer folds were training the same model

A net does not train on every training recording: it fits a run of 10 consecutive recordings from the list it is given, and the last two recordings on the list pick its threshold. Until 2026-09-18 that list was in fold order, so the run of 10 fell inside the first training fold, and held-out folds that share their first training fold trained exactly the same model. Four outer folds were then fewer than four independent fits, and a paired comparison over folds counts independence it does not have. Figure 4, the fitting draws before and after, shows it on this run's own split. The fix deals the recordings across the training folds in turn, starting after the held-out one, so every fit reaches every training fold and the threshold recordings rotate too. Before launching, the run replayed the exact draws for every training seed and would have refused to start if any two outer folds shared a fitting set or threshold recordings; its declaration records fold_check.distinct = true.

4.3 The coded side runs in its better mode

CoactDetect and LoCo each have two modes: binned, which counts coincidences in fixed time bins, and sliding, which counts them in a window that moves smoothly. Goal 1 of this program tuned both detectors in sliding mode, found better settings than the binned ones, and measured sliding mode keeping its calls when a recording is shifted by a fraction of a second, where binned mode can lose them. The project's shipped operating points are still binned — switching them changes the browser viewer's defaults, and that switch has not been made — so a search that took its unsearched settings from the shipped points would have tuned the coded side in the worse mode. That would repeat the unfairness this comparison exists to remove. So this run lays goal 1's chosen sliding values under every setting it scores for those two detectors, and uses them as the reference below: CoactDetect at alpha 1e-05, a 120 s context window, a 8 s merge gap and a 1 s guard; LoCo at the 99.9th percentile with a 8 s merge gap. The declaration records each coded detector's window mode.

4.4 Two selections, and one false-alarm budget for everyone

Choosing on F1 alone rewards a detector that calls more: extra calls can pick up extra planted events faster than they cost precision. So every contestant was chosen twice from the same fits (Figure 5, the two selections): once on F1 alone, and once on F1 among the candidates within a shared false-alarm budget. The budget is anchored to the reference CoactDetect: on each outer fold's training recordings, a candidate may fire at most 1.6 times as often as the reference does, both in the probe stretch at each background and on the quiet recordings with nothing planted. A net's threshold is chosen inside the budget too. Neither selection is the headline over the other; they answer different questions.

4.5 The size of the run

1,963 jobs: 1 reference measurement, 24 coded-detector searches (6 detectors x 4 outer folds, on the processors), 1728 inner fits and 210 outer refits of nets (on one GPU). It ran for 13.7 hours of wall time with no errors, 12.7 hours of it spent training nets.

5. A second, independent draw of recordings

Four outer folds give only 3 degrees of freedom, and a margin of a hundredth of F1 can come and go with which recordings happened to be drawn. So the workstation WSMIP065 ran the same comparison on a disjoint set of recordings (Figure 6, the two draws): recording seeds 2000–2047 instead of 1000–1047, with everything else held fixed. A difference between the two runs is then a difference between draws of data, and a margin that appears in both is not an accident of one draw. The replicate finished at 07:48 on 2026-09-19 and is reported in its own page by WSMIP065; this page does not quote its numbers.

How much the t values can carry. They are plain paired t statistics over four folds. The folds' training sets overlap (each training fold serves three held-out folds), which makes the folds less independent than the statistic assumes, so these values overstate the evidence; a correction for that (Nadeau and Bengio's) would shrink them, and none is applied here. Read them as a ranking of consistency, not as tests. The replicate (section 5) is the real check.

7. Checks that qualify the result

7.1 The crowded-recording veto, applied after the fact

On this bench, planted events are at least 120 s apart. A detector that merges calls closer together than some gap into one call therefore loses nothing here by merging widely, and gains: merging removes near-duplicate calls, which raises precision. On real recordings, coordinated events can come a few seconds apart, and a wide merge fuses them. Goal 1 found exactly this (its first search ran merge gaps out to about a minute and lost 0.25 to 0.32 F1 on crowded recordings), and added a veto: a setting may not score more than 0.02 F1 below the setting it replaces on crowded recordings, whose event spacing matches the crowded end of real recordings. This run's search did not apply that veto, so it was applied afterwards to every coded choice, by tools/crowded_check_fair_comparison.py on 12 crowded recordings at each background. Table 3 gives the outcome. CoactDetect passes in every fold. Binned SCE's 30 s merge gap does not, so its lead is the known artifact, not a better detector.

7.2 Choices that are not admissible, and choices that missed the budget on new data

  • locust under the budget: in 4 of 4 folds the budget refused every setting its search tried, and the search returned its starting point, which is itself over the budget. locust therefore has no admissible result under the budget in this run.
  • CoactDetect under the budget: admissible on its training folds by construction, it exceeded the budget on the held-out fold in 1 of 4 folds. The budget is set from training data and a new draw can land on either side of it.

7.3 Refits that failed to train

Among the 210 held-out refits of nets, 2 scored below 0.2 F1:

  • chorus_gain_norm, fold 3, refit seed 3: F1 0.125, in the choice on F1 alone — the failed-training signature (the model predicts nearly everywhere).
  • tube, fold 1, refit seed 4: F1 0.000, in the choice on F1 under the shared budget.

Each one pulls its fold's mean down by about a fifth of its shortfall; they are included in every mean above, not dropped.

8. Limits

  • Baseline only, fast stream only (Tony, 2026-09-17: "for this training run use only baseline"). The bench is fitted to the baseline periods of the fast stream. Nothing here says how any detector behaves in treatment periods or on the slow stream.
  • The bench's fitted constants carry a known, small contamination. They were last measured on the export folder steps_excluded, which still contains motion-correction floor-pinning in a few ROIs — about 0.03% of events (HANDOFF-slow-comodulation-on-the-de-pinned-export.md, decision 3). Re-measured on the corrected folder on 2026-09-17 by both workstations, every constant moved by less than its own bootstrap interval, and the two constants that describe coordination (onset spread and participation) did not move. That makes the effect small; it does not make it absent, and moving the bench to the corrected folder has not been decided.
  • Simulation only. Every number is on simulated recordings. The bench is measured from real ones, but a detector that wins here has not thereby been shown to win on real data.
  • Four outer folds, so every paired comparison rests on 3 degrees of freedom, and the folds share training data (section 6).
  • The coded searches move one setting at a time and can end in a different place depending on the path; under the budget a search can even score higher than without it, because the budget changes which moves are available. The two selections are two searches, not one search filtered.
  • Chosen values at the edge of their grid mean the search might have gone further. The coded side's edges are recorded per fold in results.json; most coded detectors chose the top of their merge-gap grid (Table 3).
  • min_rois, the fewest ROIs a coded detector will call an event on, can learn the bench's planted participation levels (30%, 18% and 10% of 33 ROIs), so its tuned value is a fact about this simulator.
  • One training run per seed, on one GPU. GPU and processor arithmetic differ slightly; every net in this run was trained on the same GPU.
