The nets' merge gap, tuned

An addendum to the fair comparison's report, which it assumes you have read: goal 2's weekend run of 2026-09-18 and its replicate on a second draw of recordings. Simulated recordings, baseline periods, the fast stream.

A detector's merge gap is how close two of its calls may be before they are combined into one. The coded detectors' searches tuned theirs and the nets ran at a fixed 2 s; this page tunes the nets' by the same rules, on their training folds, without retraining. It is worth about one noise unit, and the check that bounds it refused a wider gap every time. Each chorus net gains 0.008 to 0.014 F1 against CoactDetect counting every refit, and 0.008 to 0.014 F1 with the refits under 0.2 F1 set aside on both sides, against a between-draw scale of 0.010 F1; tube gains a fifth of that. In all 64 choices of both draws the training folds preferred a still wider gap and the crowded-recording check refused it. Under the budget CoactDetect remains ahead of every net on average in both draws and in 29 of 32 net-folds. Two things the reader should carry into the rest: on F1 alone the sign of the margin depends on whether the refits that failed are set aside, and every gap on this page is decided by a 0.02 F1 allowance the project lead has not signed.

1. The question

A detector's calls closer together than its merge gap are merged into one (the report's Figure 3, merging calls). In the fair comparison the coded detectors' searches tuned that gap and CoactDetect took 8 s in every fold, while the nets decoded at the fixed 2 s their threshold picker returns — the one setting the two sides did not share. What turns on it: if that fixed 2 s were the nets' handicap, tuning it should close the shortfall against CoactDetect, and the report's ranking rests on it not being. The report's own matched-gap re-scoring (its section 7) bounded what the gap could be worth without letting either side re-choose; the project lead asked for the measurement (2026-09-19).

No net was retrained. A merge gap is applied when a net's per-frame output is turned into calls, after the network has run, so each of the 3,881 saved fits of both draws was run once more on its recordings and decoded at every gap in the grid, and the gap was then chosen on the training folds by the rules section 5 sets out.

2. What the search ran into: the crowded-recording check

Every fold's training fits wanted a wider gap than the fold was allowed to take, and held-out F1 is largest at the top of the grid in every one of them — so what the nets chose is a boundary the check imposed, not an optimum they found (Figure 1, held-out F1 against the merge gap; Figure 2, the gap each fold chose and the settings refused).

In every one of the 64 choices the inner fits preferred at least one setting the check refused, and in every one at least one of those was a wider gap. The bench barely charges for merging: its planted events are a floor of 120 s apart, so a wider merge there mostly deletes duplicate calls — held-out F1 is largest at the widest gap in every one of the 64 fold-rows, though it dips somewhere along the way in 18 of them, which a pure duplicate-deletion account does not predict. The crowded recordings are what charges for fusing two real events. This is the check that decided the report's own headline: binned SCE's first place on F1 alone did not stand because its 30 s gap failed the same check in 7 of its 8 choices (the report's section 9, the coded detectors' choices), and CoactDetect's grid stops at 8 s because goal 1's crowded check refused its 16 s. The nets meet the check — and they meet it by being stopped at the same place. What the check does not do is make the accepted settings free: re-measured on the five outer refits, the chosen gaps lose a median 0.012 F1 there, up to 0.063, against a held-out gain of about 0.010 F1 — the same size, and 8 of 64 choices lose nothing at all. 2 of 64 lose more than the 0.02 the check allows: chorus_gain_norm on F1 alone in this run's draw, fold 3, by 0.063 F1 and chorus_gain_norm on F1 under the budget in the replicate's draw, fold 2, by 0.024 F1. That cost is each net against its own 2 s, not against CoactDetect, and the two comparisons point different ways: on the same crowded recordings the tuned chorus nets score +0.013 to +0.025 F1 against CoactDetect (Figure 5, what the chosen gaps cost on the crowded recordings), while tube loses 0.101. And the check is applied at margins near its own instability: a choice's crowded cost moves by a median 0.002 F1 and as much as 0.045 between the inner fits it is enforced on and the outer refits that produce the reported number, against a 0.02 limit, and the finest refusal on the page turned on 0.0009 F1.

3. Under the budget, the answer holds

Every net gains from a wider gap and every net stays behind CoactDetect.

Held to the shared cap on false alarms, every net gains from the wider gap and every net still trails CoactDetect on average, in both draws, whether or not the low refits are set aside. Fold by fold, CoactDetect is ahead in 29 of 32 folds; a net leads only chorus_norm in this run's draw, fold 2, by +0.004 F1, chorus_norm in the replicate's draw, fold 1, by +0.001 F1 and chorus_gain_norm in the replicate's draw, fold 4, by +0.012 F1. The mechanism is not the one to reach for: every one of the 32 gated choices widened its gap, but the chosen threshold is unchanged in 26 of them, so the gain comes from merging at the same threshold rather than from buying a lower one. Two things the comparison does not hold level, and both cut the same way. The budget never bound CoactDetect — its F1 under the budget equals its F1 alone in every fold of both draws — while for the nets it binds at selection, on the inner fits, and not at scoring: 44 of the 160 budget-chosen refits are over budget on the fold they were scored on, against 41 as run, so tuning the gap left slightly more of them outside it. Read the heading as selected under the budget rather than scored under it. And with the low refits set aside chorus_norm in the replicate's draw sits inside the noise band rather than clearly behind.

4. On F1 alone, the sign depends on how the failed refits are counted

Scored the same way on both sides, the gap moves each chorus net by about one noise unit (Figure 4, the chorus nets minus CoactDetect on F1 alone).

On F1 alone the sign of the margin depends on two choices a reader must see separately: whether the refits that scored under 0.2 F1 are set aside, and whether the baseline is scored the same way. Set aside on both sides, in this run's draw chorus_norm goes −0.007 → +0.004 F1 (3 of 4 folds ahead); in this run's draw chorus_gain_norm goes −0.015 → −0.007 F1 (2 of 4 folds ahead); in the replicate's draw chorus_norm goes +0.005 → +0.015 F1 (4 of 4 folds ahead); in the replicate's draw chorus_gain_norm goes +0.003 → +0.013 F1 (4 of 4 folds ahead). So on this selection the gap itself is worth 0.008 to 0.011 F1, about the 0.010 F1 between-draw scale. The gap carries chorus_norm in this run's draw (−0.007 → +0.004 F1) across zero; chorus_norm in the replicate's draw and chorus_gain_norm in the replicate's draw ended ahead having started there, inside the noise band either way. The set-aside is not even-handed and cannot be: CoactDetect has one value per fold and no refits, so dropping low refits can only move a net's margin upward — every set-aside column in Table 1 is at or above the column beside it. The parent report's position is the stricter one, that a user of these models gets the failures too; this page reports the set-aside as the sensitivity, not as an equal accounting. The set-aside is concentrated, not spread: in each case one fold carries it — fold 3 (+0.120 F1) for chorus_gain_norm in this run's draw, fold 2 (+0.253 F1) for chorus_norm in the replicate's draw and fold 4 (+0.127 F1) for chorus_gain_norm in the replicate's draw — while that row's other folds do not move at all. One more reason not to read the replicate's lead as corroboration: the source of the 0.010 F1 scale also measures a systematic shift of about +0.015 F1 in the nets' favour in that second draw, which is the size of the lead itself.

Counted as refits rather than as decodings — each is scored once as run and once at the tuned gap — 7 refit scorings of both draws fall under 0.2 F1: 4 carry the run's failed-training signature, one call per recording, and 3 made no calls at all at the threshold their selection chose, which the report counts as a different failure. The set-aside covers both, and the cut sits in an empty band: no refit of either draw scores between 0.2 and 0.388.

5. How the gap was chosen

  • On the training folds only, from the inner fits, as the run chose each net's configuration (the report's section 4.1 and its Figure 4, the nested cross-validation layout): for each outer fold, the pooled F1 of the nine inner fits — three training seeds by three inner folds — that never saw it.
  • The grid: 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 15, 30 s. It holds every value of CoactDetect's own grid and reaches the top of binned SCE's — the synchronous-calcium-event detector — so it spans the full range any coded detector was allowed. It is coarser than the union of their grids, which also holds 0.1, 0.5, 4, 10 and 20 s, and the coarseness probably understates the nets: 10 s was refused nowhere because it was never offered, while 15 s was refused nearly everywhere.
  • On F1 alone the gap is a configuration setting and each fit still picks its own threshold by its own rule, at that gap, on its two threshold recordings (pooled as four draws, as the run's own picker does). Under the budget threshold and gap are chosen together, and a pair is allowed only if the pooled inner rates are within the fold's budget.
  • Goal 1's move rule. The search starts at the run's 2 s and moves only for a gain of at least 0.002 inner F1 — the coded searches' step, a fifth of the noise scale, so it binds rarely.
  • The crowded-recording check, inside the search: a move may lose no more than 0.02 mean F1 on 24 three-hour crowded recordings (seeds 1 to 12 at both backgrounds, built by the bench's tail-recording generator) against the choice it replaces, on the same inner fits. ⚠ That allowance is a judgement the project lead has not signed, and it decides every gap on this page.
  • Two variants: the run's configuration kept, so only the gap and — under the budget — the threshold move; and the configuration re-chosen too. The re-chosen variant is mostly unscored: where it prefers a configuration the run never refitted, that fold has no held-out number, and nothing here retrains.
  • Held out, as in the run: the chosen configuration's five outer refits on the held-out fold, at the chosen gap, each at the threshold its own rule picks there, or at the chosen threshold under the budget.

At 2 s the re-decoding reproduces both runs exactly: every fit's picked threshold, every recording's counts and every empty recording's call count match the run's own files, for all 3,881 fits, and so do both selections' inner and held-out F1. Both draws were re-decoded on one machine, WSMIP064, on a GPU — the machine that trained this draw's fits, and not the one that trained the replicate's, whose rows matched exactly all the same. That is why the match here is bit-for-bit where the report's CPU re-scoring, in its Figure 11, what the merge gap is worth on each side, matched only to 0.0015 F1 per refit.

7. Limits

  • The report's limits hold except the two this page changes (its section 11): the merge gap is no longer tuned for one side only, and the nets are now scored on crowded recordings. The rest stand, including simulated recordings, baseline periods and the fast stream only, and a bench fitted to an export folder with a known, uncleared contamination of about 0.03% of firings.
  • The crowded check bound the two sides at different times, not only one of them. For the nets it runs inside this selection, comparing a wider gap against their as-run 2 s. For CoactDetect it had already run in goal 1, which tried 16 s and refused it — which is why its grid stops at 8 s (the report's section 11). Inside this run its declared reference already carries that 8 s, so the after-the-fact check had nothing left to refuse, and reading it as a pass would credit a test that could not fail.
  • Every chosen gap is a boundary. Held-out F1 is largest at the widest gap in the grid in every fold of both draws (Figure 1, held-out F1 against the merge gap), so the nets stopped where the check stopped them, and CoactDetect stopped where its grid ended. Neither is an optimum, and the two landing near each other is not agreement.
  • The allowance that decides it is unsigned, and no sensitivity is reported: every number here moves with 0.02.
  • The two sides' gaps match by name, not by operation. A net merges runs of frames above its threshold; CoactDetect merges significant sliding windows two seconds wide. The same nominal gap starts fusing real events at different separations.
  • The noise scale is one number standing for two sources. The median absolute move of a net's held-out F1 between the two draws, measured in the replicate's own report; the draws differ in their recordings and in the machine that trained them, which no measurement here separates. Refit-to-refit spread within a fold is comparable: the standard error of a fold's five-refit mean exceeds 0.010 F1 in more than half the chorus-net folds.
  • Four folds. Every t here has 3 degrees of freedom, and "4 of 4 folds" cannot reach significance on a sign test. The scale, not the t, is what separates a margin from a result.
  • The scorer's match tolerance is 2.5 s and interacts with a merge gap of 0 to 30 s; the nets' tolerance curve has never been measured.
  • Nothing was retrained, and 5 of the held-out refit decodings run at an end of the threshold grid, where the picker's own documentation says the value is not an operating point.

8. Where everything is

  • tools/tune_net_merge_gap.py: the re-decoding and the selections; its docstring states the selection and move rules, and the grid is its GAPS_SEC. Its output is net_merge_gap.json and replicate_net_merge_gap.json beside this page, each recording the machine, the torch version and the commit of both trees it ran from.
  • The nets and the tuning tool whose rules this reuses are on branch tune-bench-comparison, not on main: the tool needs that worktree's src and tools on PYTHONPATH.
  • tools/build_net_merge_gap_page.py: this page. tests/test_tune_net_merge_gap.py: the reproduction, the move rule, the crowded refusals and the statistics, checked against the committed output.
  • The between-draw scale and the replicate's own numbers are in docs/learned/tuned_vs_coact/replicate1/report.html, section 9.
  • The re-decoded arrays are not committed; score and crowded regenerate them from the saved fits. On the workstation that produced them the two draws took about six and seven hours of wall clock, overlapping.
