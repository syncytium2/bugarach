A thousand parameters, fitted in six seconds, against six detectors and a background that moves.

The whole pipeline in one place: what gets measured from real recordings, how that becomes a benchmark with answers, what the two candidate networks actually compute, and where they break.

What is new here, and what is assembled. The comparison against the six and the cost figures were run on 16 August and are reproduced. The regime shift on the fitted background — section 5 — was run for this report and had never been measured: the earlier version of that test ran on a flat background, which is not the data anything else here is scored on. It changes a conclusion, and not in the direction the architecture predicted.

0 · The problem

A handful of cells fire together inside a third of a second. That is the event every detector here is trying to find, and the difficulty is not subtle: on the raw record it is almost invisible. Below is one simulated recording from the data set this report scores on — twenty-one minutes of it — with the answer marked, because the generator planted it.

Three words are used throughout and are worth pinning down now. A detection is an interval, and it counts as a hit if a planted event falls inside it or within 1.5 s of the nearer edge. Recall is the share of planted events found; precision the share of detections that were real; F1 their harmonic mean, so it falls if either does. Matching is one-to-one, so a detector that splits one event into three is charged two false alarms rather than credited twice. Rates are quoted per cell — per region of interest, the segmented patch of image one cell occupies, which is what the axes label Hz/ROI.

1 · The pipeline, end to end

Coordination cannot be validated against another detector’s opinion, so nothing here is. The chain starts at real recordings, measures them without a detector, turns that measurement into a generator, and only then asks any detector anything — on data where the answer is recorded rather than inferred.

{{SVG:pipeline}}

Two properties of that chain carry the argument. First, the assessor at stage 1 uses clustering and circular-shift surrogates, not a detector, so the settings it produces are not downstream of the thing being tested. Second, ground truth reaches the scorer only. No detector, learned or hand-written, is shown a label — the learned models see the same rate-sorted raster the six see, and pick a threshold on the same three folds the six sweep their knob on.

The place a human is required is marked in red, and it is not the modelling: it is choosing K. K=3 was chosen for this data set; K=4 drops the cluster rate to roughly a quarter of it — {{N:spec:k_scan.4.clusters_permin|.3f}} per minute against {{N:spec:k_scan.3.clusters_permin|.3f}}. The whole scan ships beside the choice so that the judgement is visible rather than buried.

What K is

K is a threshold on how many cells are active at the same moment. The assessor bins the recording, counts how many distinct cells have an onset in each bin, and calls a bin co-active when that count reaches K. Runs of co-active bins become one event. So K is not a tuning parameter on top of a definition of coordination — K is the definition, and raising it does not make events smaller, it deletes the ones with fewer participants.

2 · The two models, and what each one computes

Two architectures were designed against the same requirement — a small model that finds coordinated events on a lab’s own data after a few seconds of fitting. They differ in where they refuse to give up information, and that difference is the whole experiment. A third, a pooled-trace baseline, exists as a control and is described at the end of the section.

What they are handed is a binary array. One number per cell per frame: did this cell have an onset here. Not amplitude, not width, not the fluorescence trace it came from, and not the cell's identity — rows arrive sorted busiest-first, so a row index means how active this cell is relative to the others and nothing else. Permute the input cells and the array comes back bit-identical, which the encoder has a test for rather than a claim about.

Both obey one constraint taken from the six: one cell, one vote. Coactivity is the number of distinct active cells, never a count of onsets, so reducing over cells before smoothing over time destroys the very distinction that separates coordination from one busy cell firing repeatedly.

The per-cell bank — keep every cell, discover the background

{{SVG:arch_tiny}}

Each cell goes through the same small filter, is squashed so its contribution is bounded, and only then is pooled — within rate bands, so the busiest cells stay separable from the quietest. That pooling width is the knob the model was built to turn: at one band it degenerates to the coactivity trace the six threshold, and at four it keeps the rate distribution’s structure.

Nothing tells it what the background is. The head has a 2,047-sample receptive field, and the intent was that it would use that span to work out the local level and judge excess against it. The bound is a sigmoid, so “one cell, one vote” here is approximate: an exact cap needs a fixed window, and a fixed window is the assumption the design refuses to make.

Centre minus surround — make the background cancel

{{SVG:architecture}}

The second model does not try to learn the background. It caps each cell inside a window, sums the cells into a single brightness trace, and convolves that trace with four difference-of-Gaussian kernels whose centre and surround are area-matched, so a flat field integrates to zero. A uniform change in background rate cancels rather than shrinks. That is the same “observed minus context” every one of the six computes by hand — here it is a 12-parameter operator, and its widths are fitted.

Three properties come out rather than being enforced. Cells are summed, so the model runs at any cell count. Each cell is capped inside the centre window before the sum, so distinctness is exact here rather than approximate. And how tight an event must be is a learned kernel width, initialised at one sample — the finest thing the recording can resolve — and free to grow. Multiply a fitted width by the frame interval and it is directly comparable to what the assessor measured on real slices.

Its head needs to see only 127 samples against the per-cell bank’s 2,047, because it is handed the contrast rather than having to infer it. Half the parameters, a sixteenth of the span.

Samples, not seconds — and why that is a design commitment

Every width above is in samples, and that is deliberate rather than incidental. Nothing inside a model knows what a second is. Receptive fields, kernel widths and the centre window are all counted in frames; the frame interval is the loader’s business and never reaches the network. Two things follow, and they are the reason for the rule.

First, a model trained on one rig transfers to a rig with a different frame interval by retraining on frames, with no unit conversion to get wrong — and a unit conversion applied twice, or not at all, is the classic way a detector silently mis-scales. Second, and more useful: a fitted width is a measurement. Because the centre widths are free parameters counted in samples, multiplying a fitted width by the frame interval turns the model’s own estimate of “how tight an event is” into a number comparable with what the assessor measured on real slices, without the model ever having been told the answer.

Where seconds do legitimately appear, and they must be read as conversions rather than as anything the model used: the 1.5 s matching tolerance and the Hz/ROI rates are properties of the scorer and the data set, not of any network. An earlier draft of this page glossed a receptive field as “about three and a half minutes”, which reads as if the model were reasoning in time. It is 2,047 samples; at this data set’s 0.1 s frame interval that happens to be 204.7 s, and the model is indifferent to that fact.

What the operator actually looks like

A box labelled “centre − surround” asserts a mechanism without showing one, and a reader cannot tell from it whether the kernel is sensible, what width it settled on, or whether the cancellation it claims actually happens. So here is the operator itself, read out of a trained model rather than drawn by hand.

Two things in that figure are worth more than the confirmation.

The four scales collapsed into one band. They were initialised a doubling apart — 1, 2, 4 and 8 samples — and trained to {{N:a:centres.0|.1f}}, {{N:a:centres.1|.1f}}, {{N:a:centres.2|.1f}} and {{N:a:centres.3|.1f}} samples. A four-scale bank that converges to four nearly identical scales is not using its scales; it is one scale with redundant copies.

Tested, and the bank does not earn its parameters. Re-run through the same folds and the same scorer with the bank cut to one kernel, F1 is {{N:ablation:by_key.s1_c40.f1.mean|.3f}} ± {{N:ablation:by_key.s1_c40.f1.sd|.3f}} against the published four-scale {{N:ablation:by_key.s4_c40.f1.mean|.3f}} ± {{N:ablation:by_key.s4_c40.f1.sd|.3f}} — the same number, {{N:ablation:by_key.s1_c40.n_params|,d}} parameters instead of {{N:ablation:by_key.s4_c40.n_params|,d}}. It does not help the transfer test either: one scale loses 0.25 of F1 from quiet to busy where four lose 0.24. On this data set the multi-scale design buys nothing, and the reason is probably the data rather than the idea — the generator plants a single event width, so there is no scale structure to find. A data set with varying event widths would be the test that could still vindicate the bank, and it has not been built.

The fitted width is a measurement, and it is in the right range. Around {{N:a:centres.0|.1f}}–{{N:a:centres.3|.1f}} samples at a 0.1 s frame interval is roughly 0.4–0.7 s of event width, against the assessor’s measured within-cluster onset spread — the jitter the generator was given, {{N:b:spec.jitter_sec|.2f}} s. The model was never told that number. ⚠ It should not be quoted as recovering the timescale: a previous run of this model on a quieter background moved its fitted widths by 40%, so the width is a function of the background as well as of the event.

A fitted surround ratio presses its clamp — and it turns out not to matter. The surround is a multiple of the centre and the code clamps that multiple at 40. The fit above reached {{N:a:ratios.1|.0f}}, within 10% of the ceiling, which by this project’s rule about the threshold grid is the search reporting that the range was wrong. So the ceiling was raised to 200 and everything re-run. Three things came back. The ratio only presses the ceiling when the model is trained on the busy background; trained on the quiet one it settles near 21, half the ceiling, and the clamp never binds. Released to 200 the fold scores are identical to three decimal places, and so is the transfer penalty (−0.24 either way). And a single-scale model, freed, runs its surround out to 59 — a real optimum, not another wall — with still no change in F1. The clamp is a bookkeeping wart, not a cause, and in particular it is not the explanation for section 5: the direction that fails is the one where the clamp was never binding.

And the control

A third model, pooled trace, collapses the cell axis immediately and then filters in time — deliberately the thing the design argues against, included so that “distinctness matters” is a measured claim rather than an assertion. It is not a candidate.

3 · Is a learned coordination detector new?

Asked plainly, because it decides whether any of this is worth writing up: has someone already trained a network to emit coordinated population events? A survey of the obvious neighbours says the learning in this field sits one level below this one, and the nearest thing that learns at this level learns a parameter rather than a detection. That is a real gap, and it is narrower than “nobody has done deep learning on calcium imaging” — a great many people have.

where the work happenswhat is learned there representative work
fluorescence → per-cell events learned, and standard — supervised nets beat classical deconvolution at this CASCADE; DeepCINAC; the spikefinder-challenge winners
per-cell events → population dynamics learned — but a latent trajectory, not a list of discrete events with times RADICaL
population activity → coordinated events hand-written everywhere found — ICA, PCA/Promax, similarity clustering, SVD, graph methods, item-set mining, and surrogate significance testing Mölter et al.’s benchmark of assembly detectors; Russo & Durstewitz; CICADA
choosing a classical detector’s parameters learned — a net predicts the settings a human expert would have picked; the classical detector still makes the call autoMEA

The row in red is where this work sits, and it is the row where the survey found no learned method. The learning in this field is one level below — turning fluorescence into per-cell events, which is exactly the input to a coordination analysis rather than its output. The nearest thing that learns at the coordination level, autoMEA, learns the knob: a supervised network on multi-electrode array data that outputs the burst-detection parameters a human expert would have chosen, so the operator stops being the bottleneck. Here the network is the detector, and what replaces the operator is the pipeline in section 1.

One more thing the assembly-detection literature shares with this project, and it is worth naming because it is the methodology rather than the model: Mölter, Avitan and Goodhill benchmark their algorithms by generating surrogate data with known assembly structure — the same move as section 1, arrived at independently, and evidence that the generate-with-truth half of this pipeline is a normal thing to do rather than a liberty taken here.

The honest verdict. No prior work was found that trains a network to emit coordinated population events, scored against planted ground truth, on a data set parameterised from the lab’s own recordings. That combination appears to be new. Two qualifications belong with the claim, and the first is the serious one: a survey is not a proof of absence — this is four searches and a reading of the neighbours, not a systematic review. ⚠ No paper was retrieved in full. And none of the pieces is novel individually: centre-surround is a difference-of-Gaussians and older than any of this, dilated convolutions are standard, and training-on-simulation is what CASCADE already does one level down. What has not been done before, as far as this survey reaches, is putting them at this level of the analysis.

4 · Against the six

Six hand-written detectors already exist in this repo, each computing “more coincidence than the local background explains” by a different rule: rate+context, CoactDetect, LoCo, binned SCE, CICADA and SPIKE-synchronization. They are ports held to 1e‑9 against a MATLAB original; CICADA derives from the Cossart lab’s CICADA (MIT, © 2019 Cossart Lab) and SpikyDetect — the sixth — is this lab’s own event detector running on top of a published synchrony profile: cSPIKE / PySpike (Kreuz lab) supply an adaptive SPIKE-synchronization value per spike, i.e. synchrony as a function of time, and the detection of coordination events in that profile — binning, a hysteresis scan, an artifact gate — is ours. PySpike is not a detector and is not being compared to here. They are not mine, and this comparison is against other people’s work.

All nine went through one procedure: fitted on three folds of the data set, scored on the held-out fourth, all four rotations. Hand-written detectors sweep their one declared knob; learned models train and pick a threshold. Same data set, same scorer.

The difference that survives is cost. For a model meant to be retrained inside an app on a lab’s own simulated data, that is the case.

The per-cell bank does not train, and its number is not an operating point. It sits at the floor ({{N:b:learned.tiny.f1.mean|.3f}}) at {{N:b:learned.tiny.train_sec.mean|.0f}} s of training — more than forty times the model that works — and its threshold lands on the low edge of the searched grid, which this project treats as a search that stopped too early rather than an answer. Its precision of {{N:b:learned.tiny.precision.mean|.2f}} is the precision of something that almost never fires. So the reading of section 2 is not “both designs work, one is cheaper”: one of the two designs did not learn.

Why it failed is not settled. The obvious explanation — that discovering rate invariance is harder than being handed it — is the reading, not the finding, because the model that works also trains at ten times the learning rate of the two that do not, and this project’s own diagnostic ranks the loss weighting and batch size as the leading untested cause. A controlled comparison has not been run.

5 · The shift to a busier background

Everything above is a single background rate. The test that matters for deployment is what happens when a detector is fitted in one background and run in another, because untreated slices vary 4.6-fold among themselves — 0.0038 Hz and 0.0175 Hz per cell, both measured, both untreated, taken from the same assessment of {{N:g:source_recordings.provenance.n_slices_assessed|d}} slices with {{N:g:source_recordings.provenance.n_surrogates|,d}} surrogates that set it. Those endpoints are the 25th and 75th percentiles of the slice-population rate; read as per-cell rates they sit nearer the 60th and 83rd, which narrows the axis and is caveat 4 in section 6. No drug is involved: this is a generalization question, not a pharmacology one.

The knob and the threshold are carried over unchanged. Re-picking them on arrival is the failure being tested for — it hides the collapse at the exact moment a lab cannot afford it. The six are calibrated on held-out recordings from the training regime and graded on the target, which is the same experiment the learned models get rather than a fixed operating point evaluated twice.

This run is why the report exists. The earlier version of this test used the bench’s flat background, so it did not sit on the data everything else is scored on. Re-run on the fitted background — heterogeneous across cells, bursty in time, measured from 85 real recordings — it says something different.

The failure this test exists to catch does reproduce here — and the learned model is not the one that survives it. Calibrated quiet and deployed busy, rate+context falls from {{N:g:six_transfer.rate.baseline_quiet.baseline_quiet.f1|.2f}} to {{N:g:six_transfer.rate.baseline_quiet.baseline_busy.f1|.2f}}, and its precision from {{N:g:six_transfer.rate.baseline_quiet.baseline_quiet.precision|.2f}} to {{N:g:six_transfer.rate.baseline_quiet.baseline_busy.precision|.2f}} — a genuine collapse, of the kind that was previously only a figure in someone else’s deck. And from an essentially identical starting point, centre−surround falls further than CoactDetect and LoCo do — {{N:g:learned.tube.baseline_quiet.baseline_quiet.f1|.2f}}→{{N:g:learned.tube.baseline_quiet.baseline_busy.f1|.2f}} against {{N:g:six_transfer.coact.baseline_quiet.baseline_quiet.f1|.2f}}→{{N:g:six_transfer.coact.baseline_quiet.baseline_busy.f1|.2f}} and {{N:g:six_transfer.loco.baseline_quiet.baseline_quiet.f1|.2f}}→{{N:g:six_transfer.loco.baseline_quiet.baseline_busy.f1|.2f}}, with precision {{N:g:learned.tube.baseline_quiet.baseline_quiet.precision|.2f}}→{{N:g:learned.tube.baseline_quiet.baseline_busy.precision|.2f}} against CoactDetect’s {{N:g:six_transfer.coact.baseline_quiet.baseline_quiet.precision|.2f}}→{{N:g:six_transfer.coact.baseline_quiet.baseline_busy.precision|.2f}}.

That is a negative result about this report’s own architecture, and it lands on the claim the design was built to support. “Rate invariance by construction” predicts the centre−surround model should barely move across this axis while a model that had to discover the invariant should. It moved more than two of the hand-written detectors it ties with. The mechanism the design cancels is the mean background; what a busier background also brings is variance, and chance alignments that grow with rate are not removed by subtracting a local level.

The direction is the usable part. Panel B is not a mirror of panel A: fitted on the busy background and deployed quiet, every detector holds or improves, and centre−surround goes {{N:g:learned.tube.baseline_busy.baseline_busy.f1|.2f}}→{{N:g:learned.tube.baseline_busy.baseline_quiet.f1|.2f}}. So the operational rule is fit on the busier recordings and deploy downward, which is a cheap thing for an app to enforce and a real finding about how to use these models rather than about how good they are.

Two smaller readings. Binned SCE has no operating point on this axis at all — its sweep peaks at the edge of its grid in both regimes, so it is absent from the figure rather than plotted at a boundary value. And the learned models that never trained are flat lines at the floor: a model that does not fire cannot degrade, and their apparent stability is not robustness.

6 · What this does not establish

  1. The data set is simulated. Its settings are measured from 85 real recordings; the recordings the detectors are scored on are generated. Nothing here says any detector is right about a real slice.
  2. One training run per fold. No learned number here has a seed error bar, so fold spread confounds data variation with training variation. This is the cheapest outstanding thing that could change a conclusion, and it is not done.
  3. Four folds, eight recordings, thirty planted events per fold. Every interval quoted is a fold range, not a confidence interval, and the top three are a tie.
  4. The regime axis is narrower than it sounds. Its endpoints are p25 and p75 of a slice-population rate divided by a derived cell count; as per-cell rates they sit around the 60th and 83rd percentiles. A null result on this axis would therefore be weak evidence — which cuts against the flat lines, not against the collapses.
  5. The architecture comparison is not controlled. Ten-fold difference in learning rate between the model that trains and the two that do not, and the leading alternative explanations are untested.
  6. The promiscuity probe cannot fail. Firings inside the dense random block leave both the numerator and the denominator of precision, so it reports promiscuity rather than penalising it. No “does not fire on dense random activity” claim is supported.
  7. K=3 was chosen by a human and the choice moves the data set; K=4 drops the cluster rate to roughly a quarter and would build a different benchmark.
  8. The novelty survey is a survey. Four searches and a reading of the neighbours, no paper fetched in full, no systematic review.
