Why a third of chorus_norm's fits do not train

A diagnosis from the saved fits of the two runs of the project's fair comparison of coded detectors against learned nets (goal 2). The runs' results were not changed; 14 training runs were replayed from the runs' own starting points to watch the failure happen. Simulated recordings only.

chorus_norm and chorus_gain_norm are two of the small neural networks the project trains to find coordinated events, moments when many cells in a recording become active together. Each reads a recording as one row per cell (a region of interest, ROI) in 0.1-second frames and outputs a score for every frame, as a logit (log-odds). A threshold chosen during training turns the scores into calls, and calls are scored by F1 (the harmonic mean of precision and recall) against the events planted in the simulation.

The answer.
  • The problem. In both runs of goal 2, 146 and 153 of chorus_norm's 432 inner fits collapsed: each called every recording it was scored on as a single event, which matches one of the 15 planted events, F1 0.125 (Figure 1, a collapsed fit on a held-out recording).
  • For chorus_norm the cause is the learning rate (lr). 292 of 396 fits collapse at lr 0.03; 7 of 468 fits below it. The encoder's shape shifts the share. chorus_gain_norm is different: its encoder's shape matters as much as the learning rate.
  • The signal stops in the head, the eight layers that turn the pooled per-ROI statistics into the output. In the second run, 142 of the 153 collapsed chorus_norm inner fits have a head layer whose units' outputs do not vary, against 0 of the 279 working ones, while the head's input still separates events from the rest (Figure 4, where the signal stops).
  • A collapsed fit never leaves its flat start, and a slower start prevents it. Replayed exactly, a collapsed fit and a working fit of the same configuration both start with a flat output. The working one leaves it within 220 steps; the collapsed one never does, and its head goes silent later. The same collapsed fit trains at lr 0.003 or 0.01, or at 0.03 with the learning rate ramped up linearly over the first 200 steps, which also trains 6 of 7 other collapsed runs. This is a known failure of training at too large a step size, and a warm-up is its standard remedy (§4).
  • It is not the failure found earlier in plain chorus (PR #596), where the encoder barely responded to events. Here the encoder's output still does.
  • What it did to goal 2. Tuning chose no lr-0.03 configuration for chorus_norm, so the collapse took 11 of its 24 configurations out of contention rather than putting dead models into its held-out scores; 2 of its refits collapsed anyway. How to repair it is a decision for goal 2 (§5).

1. The problem

Figure 1, a collapsed fit on a held-out recording shows what a collapse is. Both fits come from the same configuration and differ only in their training seed, which sets the starting weights and the order of the training crops. The working fit's output rises at events and its threshold turns the rises into calls. The collapsed fit's output barely moves, and it sits far above the fit's threshold, so the whole recording is one call.

How the fits are organized. A configuration is one setting of the net's size and training: the encoder's width and depth, how many of the most active ROIs one pooled statistic averages (top m), the learning rate and the number of training steps. Goal 2 tunes each net over 24 configurations by nested cross-validation. Inside each outer fold, every configuration is trained at 3 training seeds on each of the 6 pairs of 4 inner folds and scored on the other two: these are the inner fits, 432 per net per run. The configuration tuning picks is then trained afresh and scored on recordings held out from all of that: a refit. Goal 2 was run twice, on different simulated recordings; the replicate report calls the two runs draws, and so does this page.

Which fits collapse is not repeatable; how many is. 111 of chorus_norm's collapsed fits are the same fit (configuration, training seed and pair of folds) in both draws. If each draw collapsed its fits independently at its configurations' own rates, the expected overlap would be 111.5. So the overlap says the configuration decides how often a fit collapses, and nothing about which ones do (chorus_gain_norm: 38 observed, 39.6 expected).

2. Which configurations collapse

For chorus_norm the learning rate separates the configurations almost completely (Figure 2, collapse by configuration): every configuration at lr 0.03 collapses in 42% to 92% of its fits, and below 0.03 the collapse is rare. Within lr 0.03 the encoder's shape shifts the share (Table 1, collapse by shape): the narrow, deep encoder (4 × 6) collapses most. Training length is tangled with depth: at lr 0.03, 900 steps 65% (141 of 216 fits); 1,800 steps 82% (59 of 72 fits); 3,600 steps 85% (92 of 108 fits), and the 3,600-step configurations are all 6 layers deep. These shape and length shares are described, not tested.

chorus_gain_norm does not follow the learning rate the same way. At lr 0.03 its 4 × 6 configurations collapse in 78% of fits and the other shapes in 8%; at lr 0.01 its 4 × 6 configurations still collapse in 25%.

Inside a configuration, the fold pair makes no difference to whether a fit collapses (p = 0.939 for chorus_norm, p = 0.470 for chorus_gain_norm; collapse labels shuffled within each configuration and draw). The training seed does, for chorus_norm (p = 0.001; chorus_gain_norm p = 0.279): at a risky configuration some starting points escape and some do not. Pooled over configurations the seeds look alike (34%, 32%, 38% of chorus_norm's fits at seeds 0, 1 and 2), which is why the test is made inside them. 5 pairs of configurations in the grid (both nets) differ only in step count; the shorter one's fits are the longer one's stopped early, so they are left out of these tests.

3. Where the signal stops

chorus_norm passes each ROI's activity through a shared encoder, standardizes the encoder's output over time, turns it into a vote per ROI, pools the votes into three statistics (their mean, their spread across ROIs, and the mean of the top m) and feeds those to a head: 8 layers of 8 units, each followed by a GELU, an activation that passes positive input and maps negative input to a small dip (no lower than −0.17) that still carries a gradient (Hendrycks & Gimpel 2016). The head's size was never tuned. The nets are trained with Adam (Kingma & Ba 2015) at its default β1 0.9 and β2 0.999; lr 0.03 is 30 times the step size that paper suggests as a default.

Every second-draw fit of both nets was reloaded and run on one simulated recording (quiet background, simulation seed 2000); call this the census. Call a head layer silent when none of its 8 units' outputs varies over the recording (standard deviation under 0.001): it passes nothing that changes. In chorus_norm, 142 of the 153 collapsed inner fits have a silent head layer, against 0 of the 279 working ones (Figure 3, silent layers and flat outputs); in chorus_gain_norm, 51 of 75 against 0 of 357. The output of a collapsed fit barely moves: its standard deviation over the recording has a median of 0.0055 logits against 5.20 in working fits. Every collapsed fit's threshold is the lowest value on the threshold grid (0.0001), below its whole output, so the whole recording is one call.

This is the test that separates this failure from the earlier one. PR #596 found plain chorus's encoder barely responding to events. If that were happening here, the head's input would not separate event frames from the rest either. It does: measured as the largest standardized difference between event frames and the rest (d) over the head's input channels, collapsed chorus_norm fits have a median of 1.84 and working ones 2.60. At the output the collapsed fits' separation falls to a median of 0.13, against 4.29 in working fits (Figure 4, where the signal stops). The encoder still hears the events; the head does not pass them on.

Round 1 of this page's review counted a layer as dead when none of its units' outputs ever rose above 0.001. That is a sign test, and GELU's negative dip carries signal without going positive: it counted 152 of the collapsed and 1 of the working chorus_norm fits, including one working fit whose output varies normally. The silent-layer rule counts what reaches the output. 24 collapsed chorus_gain_norm fits have no silent layer; §6 says what is known about them.

4. When it happens, and what prevents it

The collapsed fit of Figure 1, a collapsed fit on a held-out recording and its working sibling were replayed exactly as the run trained them, and each replay reproduced its saved checkpoint to the last bit. Both start with an output that barely varies (standard deviation 0.0008 and 0.0011 logits on the first batch). The working fit's loss falls below 1.0 by step 220; the collapsed fit's never leaves its starting level (Figure 6, the replays). Measured on the training batches, the working fit's head never has a silent layer. The collapsed fit's first has one at step 230, well after its loss has stalled; from then on it has one at 135 of the 158 logged steps, and ends with 2 of its 8 silent (Figure 5, silent layers during training). So the silent head marks a fit that never left its flat start, and does not begin the failure: the replay with a 50-step warm-up stays flat without a silent layer on any of its training batches.

The collapsed fit was then replayed with one thing changed and the same starting weights and training crops. It trains at lr 0.003 and at lr 0.01, which change the learning rate for the whole run; and at lr 0.03 when the learning rate is ramped up linearly over the first 200 steps, which changes only the start. A 50-step ramp is not enough: its loss retraces the fit as run.

The 200-step warm-up was then tried on one collapsed fit, chosen by hand, from each of 7 other lr-0.03 configurations (Figure 7, warm-up on other fits, Table 2, fit by fit). Two of those configurations differ only in step count, so their fits are one training run counted twice; counted once, 6 of 7 distinct runs train. If warm-up did nothing, each would train only as often as its configuration's fits do, which predicts 1.4 of 7. The one that does not train (e86433df) was not tried with a longer ramp. Of the 3 lr-0.03 configurations left out, 2 collapse least of all (15 of 36 fits, 18 of 36 fits) and 1 is the shorter twin of one that was tried.

Final loss: the mean of the last 5 logged steps. A fit trains when that is below 0.5; a constant output's loss is about 1.4 (2 ln 2 times the share of frames outside events), and the collapsed fits sit near 1.5.

Settled early, mostly. The configurations that differ only in step count show when a collapse is decided: of the collapses in the longer twin, 60 of 62 in chorus_norm were already there when the shorter twin stopped (900 against 1,800 steps, 900 against 3,600 steps, 1,800 against 3,600 steps), and 2 came later. 0 fits collapsed at the shorter length and worked at the longer (chorus_gain_norm: 8 early, 0 late, 3 recovered).

A known failure, with a standard remedy. Units that stop responding under too large a step size are a long-standing observation in training deep nets; Gulcehre et al. (2022) measure more of them at larger learning rates, and Sokar et al. (2023) define such "dormant" units by a threshold on their activity, as this page does. A learning-rate warm-up is the usual remedy (He et al. 2016; Goyal et al. 2017). Why it helps Adam is argued (Liu et al. 2020; Ma & Yarats 2021); Ma & Yarats's rule of thumb, 2/(1 − β2), is 2,000 steps at Adam's default, ten times the ramp tried here.

5. What it means, and the choice it leaves

  • For chorus_norm, tuning stepped around it. A configuration whose fits collapse scores badly in the inner selection, and tuning chose none at lr 0.03 (Table 3, what tuning chose). The collapse therefore did not put dead models into chorus_norm's held-out scores wholesale; it took 11 of its 24 configurations out of contention, so chorus_norm was in effect tuned over a smaller grid than the one the fair-comparison report declares.
  • For chorus_gain_norm it did not. Tuning chose lr-0.03 configurations for it (30 refits), and both of its collapsed refits are among them.
  • The refits that collapsed. 4 refits collapsed across both draws: chorus_gain_norm, first draw, lr 0.03; chorus_gain_norm, second draw, lr 0.03; chorus_norm, second draw, lr 0.01; chorus_norm, second draw, lr 0.01. They are behind the † on chorus_norm (second draw) and chorus_gain_norm (both draws) in the replicate report, whose † is its own flag (F1 near 0.125 with the threshold at the grid floor, or F1 undefined) and agrees for these four. The census holds the second draw's 3; all 3 have a silent head layer.
  • A repair is a decision for goal 2, not this page's. Three are open. Drop lr 0.03 from chorus_norm's grid, which accepts the grid tuning already used in effect (for chorus_gain_norm it would change what tuning chose). Add a linear learning-rate warm-up to training, which changes every net, since they share one trainer; 200 steps was tried here. Or change the head, 8 layers of 8 units and never tuned, which was not tried. Each changes chorus_norm's numbers in goal 2 and needs both draws run again to compare.
  • A collapse can be caught when it happens. On the census recording the output's standard deviation separates the two groups without overlap, in both nets and in the refits: at most 0.132 logits in every collapsed fit, at least 0.803 in every working one. A check at the end of training could refuse a fit whose held-out output does not vary, instead of scoring it. The gap was found on one recording and one draw; a cutoff inside it has not been tested on others.

6. Limits

  • Simulated recordings only. The census and the replays use the second draw's fits; the collapse counts use both draws.
  • The census runs each fit on one recording, which some fits trained on and others did not. The separations above held on that recording and were not tested on others.
  • The two as-run replays reproduced their checkpoints exactly. The counterfactual replays are the same code with one change and have no checkpoint to match; they are judged by training loss, not by calls on held-out recordings.
  • The warm-up was tried on 8 distinct collapsed runs of chorus_norm, chosen by hand, and on no chorus_gain_norm fit.
  • 24 collapsed chorus_gain_norm fits have a flat output without a silent head layer (2 at lr 0.003, 11 at lr 0.01, 11 at lr 0.03; encoder 4 × 6: 23, 8 × 4: 1). What stops them is not established here.
  • The seed effect inside a configuration is measured, not explained.
