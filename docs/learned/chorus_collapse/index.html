Why a third of chorus_norm's fits do not train

A diagnosis from the fits of goal 2's two draws, reloaded and replayed; nothing in the runs was retrained. Simulated recordings only.

The answer.
  • It is the learning rate at the start of training. chorus_norm collapses in 292 of its 396 inner fits at a learning rate of 0.03 and in 7 of 468 below it.
  • What breaks is the head, the eight-layer stack that turns the pooled per-cell statistics into the output: 152 of 153 collapsed chorus_norm fits have a head layer whose units never switch on, so almost no signal passes and the output barely moves; 1 of the 279 working fits has one.
  • It happens in the first steps. Replayed exactly, a collapsed fit's head starts losing whole layers by step 30 and its loss never leaves its starting level. The same fit, with the same initial weights and the same data, trains at a learning rate of 0.01 or 0.003, or at 0.03 with a 200-step warm-up; a 50-step warm-up is not enough.
  • This is not the failure PR #596 fixed. That was plain chorus's per-cell encoder starting deaf at every learning rate; chorus_norm's standardization repaired it. This is a second mechanism, in the head, and almost entirely at the top of the tuning grid.

1. The finding it explains

In goal 2's comparison each net is tuned by inner fits: each of 24 configurations is trained at 3 training seeds on each pair of 4 folds, 432 inner fits per net per draw. The replicate report (docs/learned/tuned_vs_coact/replicate1/) found that 146 and 153 of chorus_norm's 432 inner fits in the two draws made exactly one call on every recording they were scored on — the whole recording as one call, which matches one of 15 planted events, F1 0.125 — and that 111 of them are the same fit (configuration, training seed and pair of folds) in both draws. The draws share their configurations and training seeds and differ only in their recordings, so the collapse follows the fit's configuration and starting weights more than its data. It matters because chorus_norm's scores mix a working model with one that never trained.

2. Which configurations collapse

Figure 1, collapse by configuration shows each configuration's collapse share. For chorus_norm the learning rate separates them almost completely: every configuration at 0.03 collapses in 42% to 92% of its fits, and below 0.03 the collapse is rare. Within 0.03 the per-cell encoder's shape shifts the share: by width, 4 units 80% (172 of 216 fits); 8 units 67% (120 of 180 fits); by depth, 4 layers 60% (108 of 180 fits); 6 layers 85% (184 of 216 fits). Nothing else in the fit's identity matters: across all of chorus_norm's inner fits the share is 34%, 32%, 38% at training seeds 0, 1 and 2, and between 30% and 38% across the 6 pairs of training folds. chorus_gain_norm shows the same pattern, milder.

3. What a collapsed fit is

chorus_norm passes each cell through a shared encoder, standardizes the encoder's output over time, turns it into a vote, pools the votes into three statistics, and feeds those to a head of 8 layers of 8 units, each followed by a GELU (a smooth activation that passes positive input and squeezes negative input to small values near zero). Call a head layer dead when none of its 8 units ever outputs more than 0.001 over a whole recording. Every second-draw chorus inner fit was reloaded and run on one bench recording of that draw (Figure 2, what the head does). In chorus_norm, 152 of the 153 collapsed fits have a dead head layer, against 1 of the 279 working fits. A dead layer passes only the small negative residue of its GELUs, so the output hardly moves: the median spread (standard deviation over the recording's frames) of the output logit is 0.0055 in collapsed fits against 5.20 in working ones, and the fit's threshold falls to the bottom of its grid, which makes the whole recording one call. The working fit with a dead layer still varies (output spread 3.1), carried by that residue. The collapsed fit without one (lr 0.003) has the same flat output (spread 0.047).

In chorus_gain_norm, 60 of 75 collapsed fits have a dead head layer, against 3 of 357 working fits. The other 15 collapsed fits have the same flat output without a dead layer, and they are not concentrated at lr 0.03 (2 at lr 0.003, 8 at lr 0.01, 5 at lr 0.03). Their vote gains, the one parameter chorus_gain_norm adds, end a median 3% from where they started, against 13% in working fits. That is a second route to the same failure, and this page does not establish what it is.

4. When it happens, and what prevents it

A collapsed fit (configuration 2736f584, lr 0.03, training seed 0) and the one working fit of the same configuration were replayed exactly as the run trained them; each replay reproduced its saved checkpoint to the last bit. Both start with an output that barely varies (spread 0.0008 and 0.0011 on the first batch). The working fit escapes and its loss falls; the collapsed fit's loss never leaves its starting level (Figure 3, the replays), and its head has a dead layer by step 30 and 4 of its 8 at the end, against 0 in the working fit (Figure 4, the head dying). The collapsed fit was then replayed with one thing changed. With the same initial weights and the same data it trains at lr 0.01 or 0.003, and at lr 0.03 with the learning rate ramped up over the first 200 steps; a 50-step ramp is not enough. A 200-step warm-up at lr 0.03 was then applied to 8 more collapsed fits from 8 lr-0.03 configurations (one of them the configuration of Figure 3): 7 of them train (Table 2). The one that does not, e86433df, is from a configuration that collapsed in 31 of its 36 fits; a longer warm-up was not tried.

5. What it means, and the choice it leaves

  • Tuning already stepped around it, at a cost. A configuration whose fits collapse scores badly in the inner selection, so tuning never chose a lr-0.03 configuration for chorus_norm: its 95 refits across both draws (the chosen configurations, trained afresh and scored on held-out recordings) are 35 at lr 0.003, 60 at lr 0.01, 0 at lr 0.03. The collapse therefore did not put dead models into chorus_norm's held-out scores wholesale; it removed 11 of its 24 configurations from contention, so chorus_norm was in effect tuned over a smaller grid than the one declared.
  • The refits that did collapse are the same failure. 4 refits collapsed across both draws (chorus_gain_norm, first draw, lr 0.03; chorus_gain_norm, second draw, lr 0.03; chorus_norm, second draw, lr 0.01; chorus_norm, second draw, lr 0.01); each puts a † beside its net in the replicate report. The second draw's 3 were in the census, and 3 of them have a dead head layer.
  • A repair is a design decision for goal 2, not this page's. Three follow from the evidence: drop lr 0.03 from the chorus grids, which accepts the smaller grid tuning already used in effect; add a learning-rate warm-up (200 steps rescued 7 of 8 fits tried); or change the head (8 layers of 8 units, never tuned), which was not tested here. Any of them changes chorus_norm's numbers in goal 2 and would need both draws re-run to compare.
  • A collapse is detectable when it happens. On the census recording, the output's spread separates the two groups without overlap in both nets and in the refits: at most 0.13 in every collapsed fit, at least 0.80 in every working one. A check at the end of training could refuse such a fit instead of scoring it.

6. Limits

  • Simulation only; the census and the replays use the second draw's fits; the collapse table uses both draws.
  • The census runs each fit on one recording, which some fits trained on and others did not. A unit counted dead there could switch on elsewhere. The separation by output spread held on this one recording; it was not tested on others.
  • Replays are exact for fits as run (checked against the checkpoints); a counterfactual replay is the same code with one change, and was scored by its training loss, not re-scored on held-out recordings.
  • Warm-up was tried on 9 collapsed fits, not on the whole grid or on chorus_gain_norm.
